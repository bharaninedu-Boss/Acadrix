ME3591 – Unit 1 Visual Guide

ACADRIX • Fundamental Concepts in Design

Use these simplified technical sketches for understanding and exam presentation. They are schematic, not manufacturing drawings.

1. Direct, Bending and Torsional Loading

Remember: direct load → axial stress; bending moment → bending stress; torque → torsional shear stress.

2. Eccentric Loading

An eccentric force produces direct stress + bending stress. The bending moment is M = P × e.

3. Curved Beam – Neutral Axis

Unlike a straight beam, the neutral axis of a curved beam does not generally pass through the centroid. For the rectangular and circular sections, use the appropriate curved-beam relation from the notes/data book.

4. Crane Hook / C-Frame Idea

For a crane hook or C-frame, identify the critical curved section, calculate the neutral-axis location, determine inner/outer fibre stresses, and check against the allowable stress.

5. Principal Stress Concept

QuantityMeaning
σ1Maximum principal normal stress
σ2Minimum principal normal stress in the plane
τmaxMaximum in-plane shear stress = (σ1 − σ2)/2

Use the principal-stress transformation before applying the specified failure theory.

6. Failure-Theory Decision Map

Exam rule: if the question specifies a theory, use that theory. Do not switch theories merely because another equation is familiar.

7. Fluctuating Stress Cycle

For a fluctuating stress: σm = (σmax + σmin)/2 and σa = (σmax − σmin)/2. The stress ratio is R = σmin/σmax.

Exam Presentation Tip

  1. Draw only the geometry needed for the calculation.
  2. Mark load, dimensions, section and critical location.
  3. Write the governing equation beside the sketch.
  4. Keep units in N, mm, N/mm² and N-mm unless another system is explicitly used.
  5. For standard dimensions, state the adopted PSG value after calculation.
