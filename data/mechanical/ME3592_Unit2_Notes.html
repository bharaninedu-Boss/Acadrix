ME3592 — Unit II: Measurement of Linear, Angular Dimensions, Assembly and Transmission Elements

Exam focus: understand the instrument, principle, construction, working, applications, advantages/limitations and important formula for each measurement method.

1. Linear Measuring Instruments

Vernier Caliper

Used for external, internal and depth measurements. It combines a main scale with a sliding vernier scale. Least count = 1 main-scale division − 1 vernier-scale division.

Micrometer

Uses a precision screw mechanism to convert spindle rotation into small linear displacement. Main parts: frame, anvil, spindle, sleeve/barrel, thimble and ratchet. Use the ratchet for approximately uniform measuring pressure.

Vernier Height Gauge

Used on a surface plate to measure or mark vertical dimensions. A sliding carriage carries the vernier and scriber/dial attachment.

Depth Micrometer

Measures depth of holes, slots and recesses. A flat base rests on the reference surface while the spindle enters the feature.

Bore Gauge

A comparative instrument for internal diameter measurement. It is normally set against a standard and the deviation is read with an indicator.

Telescoping Gauge

A transfer gauge for internal dimensions. The expanding contacts are locked inside the bore and then measured externally using a micrometer.

2. Gauge Blocks

Gauge blocks, or slip gauges, are precision length standards used for setting instruments, comparators and sine bars. Select a combination that gives the required dimension with the minimum practical number of blocks.

Precautions: keep faces clean, avoid unnecessary handling, protect from damage/corrosion and separate wrung blocks carefully after use.

Wringing

Wringing is the process of joining clean gauge-block faces by sliding one block across another and then bringing them into intimate contact. Cleanliness and a thin protective film are essential.

3. Comparators

A comparator does not normally give the absolute size; it indicates the deviation of a workpiece from a preset standard. Main types: mechanical, electrical/electronic, optical and pneumatic.

Mechanical Comparator

Small plunger movement is magnified by levers, gears or a combination. Advantages include simple construction, portability and no external power requirement. Limitations include friction, backlash and limited range.

Electrical/Electronic Comparator

A transducer converts displacement into an electrical signal, which is amplified and displayed. High magnification and small moving mass are important advantages.

Pneumatic Comparator

Variation in air flow or pressure caused by the clearance between nozzle and workpiece is used to indicate dimensional variation. It is suitable for clean, repeatable comparative inspection of small dimensions.

4. Opto-mechanical Measurements

Measuring Microscope

Uses an optical system and calibrated movement of the work stage/crosshair to measure small linear dimensions, angles, thread features and profiles.

Profile Projector

Projects an enlarged silhouette of the workpiece onto a screen. It permits non-contact measurement and comparison of profiles, angles, radii and small features.

5. Angular Measurement

Bevel Protractor

A direct-reading angular instrument using a main scale and vernier. It is convenient for general workshop angle measurement.

Clinometer

Measures small inclinations of a surface with respect to a reference plane.

Angle Gauges

Precision blocks with known angular values. They are combined to build a required angle by addition/subtraction.

Precision Level

Measures very small angular deviations of machine-tool surfaces using a sensitive spirit level mechanism.

Sine Bar

Works on the sine principle. If h is the slip-gauge height and L is the distance between roller centres:

sin θ = h / L

Therefore θ = sin⁻¹(h/L). Sine bars are used with slip gauges for accurate angle setting and measurement. Errors can arise from roller-centre distance, slip-gauge combination, parallelism and flatness.

Autocollimator

An optical instrument for very small angular differences. A collimated beam is reflected from a plane mirror; a small mirror tilt produces twice that angular change in the reflected beam. Applications include straightness, flatness, squareness, alignment and angular indexing.

Angle Dekkor

An autocollimator-type instrument used to measure small angular deviations. It can compare the angular setting of a component with a known angle-gauge setting.

Alignment Telescope

Used for establishing and checking a reference line for alignment of machine-tool elements and other components.

6. Screw Thread Measurement

Important elements: major diameter, minor diameter, effective/pitch diameter, pitch, lead, thread angle and form.

Major Diameter

Can be measured with an ordinary or bench micrometer for external threads.

Minor Diameter

May be measured by suitable V-pieces, taper parallels or rollers depending on the thread and access.

Effective Diameter

Methods include one-wire, two-wire and three-wire methods. The three-wire method is especially accurate because the wires establish controlled contact with the thread flanks.

Pitch

Measured using a pitch-measuring machine, toolmaker's microscope or screw pitch gauge. Pitch is the axial distance between corresponding points on adjacent threads.

7. Gear Measurement

Purpose: verify dimensional and functional accuracy of gears. Important checks include runout, pitch variation, tooth profile, tooth thickness and lead. Functional checking may be performed by a rolling gear test.

Analytical vs Functional Checking

  • Analytical: individual gear elements are measured separately.
  • Functional: gear is checked for its behaviour when meshed/rolled with a mating or master gear.

Exam Checklist

  • Draw neat labelled sketches for vernier bevel protractor, sine bar, autocollimator, comparator and profile projector.
  • For every instrument write principle → construction → working → applications → advantages/limitations.
  • Remember sine-bar relation: sin θ = h/L.
  • Know screw-thread elements and three-wire method.
  • Differentiate analytical and functional gear measurement.
