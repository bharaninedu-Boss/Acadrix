ME3592 — Metrology and Measurements

Unit I: Basics of Metrology · R2021 · ACADRIX

Syllabus coverage: Measurement, need and role in quality control; SWIPE factors; errors and error control; measurement uncertainty and estimation; statistical analysis; measurement system analysis; calibration; air gauging; ISO standards.

1. Metrology and Measurement

Metrology is the science of measurement. In engineering, it deals with measurement of dimensions and related quantities and with the establishment, reproduction, conservation and transfer of measurement units and standards.

Measurement is the process of obtaining a numerical value for a quantity by comparison with an accepted standard. In manufacturing, the main purpose is to check whether a component satisfies its specification.

Why measurement is needed

Quality control
Interchangeability
Process control
Inspection and acceptance
Calibration and traceability
Reduction of manufacturing errors

2. General Measurement Process

A practical measurement system can be viewed as: measurand → sensing/measurement element → signal conditioning → indicating/recording element. The selected standard, workpiece, instrument, operator and environment all influence the result.

Exam point: Always distinguish the measured value from the true value. The difference is the measurement error.

3. Factors Affecting Measurement — SWIPE

S — Standard · W — Workpiece · I — Instrument · P — Person · E — Environment

S — Standard

Calibration, thermal expansion, stability with time, elastic properties and geometric compatibility affect the reference.

W — Workpiece

Cleanliness, surface finish, scratches, hidden geometry, elastic properties, datum arrangement, support and thermal equalization.

I — Instrument

Scale error, amplification, friction, backlash, hysteresis, zero drift, calibration error, deformation, repeatability and readability.

P — Person

Training, skill, ability to select instruments, appreciation of precision and proper measurement planning.

E — Environment

Temperature, humidity, vibration, illumination and thermal radiation. Standard, workpiece and instrument should reach thermal equilibrium.

4. Standards and Traceability

A standard provides a reference for assigning a numerical value to a measured quantity. Measurement standards are arranged in a traceable hierarchy. A commonly shown chain is:

National standards → National reference standards → Working standards → Plant laboratory reference standards → Plant laboratory working standards → Shop-floor standards

Accuracy generally degrades as the measurement reference moves toward shop-floor use, so traceability and periodic comparison are important.

5. Accuracy and Precision

Accuracy
Closeness of the measured value to the true value.
Precision
Closeness of repeated readings to one another under stated conditions.
Repeatability
Agreement when the same observer, instrument, method, location and conditions are used over a short time.
Reproducibility
Agreement when observers, methods, instruments, locations or times differ.

6. Errors in Measurement

Error = Measured value − True value

6.1 Systematic errors

Errors that show a consistent or predictable tendency. Typical sources include instrument, environmental, observation and loading effects. They can often be reduced or corrected by calibration, controlled conditions and suitable measurement methods.

6.2 Random errors

Unpredictable variations that remain after systematic effects are reduced. Repeated observations and statistical treatment are used to characterize them.

Error control

  • Calibrate instruments against suitable standards.
  • Control temperature, vibration, cleanliness and illumination.
  • Use correct contact and measurement method.
  • Minimize loading and handling effects.
  • Repeat measurements and use statistical analysis where appropriate.

7. Measurement Uncertainty

Measurement uncertainty expresses the doubt associated with a measurement result. It is not the same thing as an error; the true error is generally not known exactly.

Type A evaluation

Evaluated from statistical analysis of repeated observations.

Type B evaluation

Evaluated using other available information such as calibration certificates, specifications, previous data, resolution or assumed distributions.

Combined standard uncertainty

For independent input quantities: uc = √(u₁² + u₂² + … + uₙ²)

Use the exact uncertainty model and coverage convention specified in the problem. Do not mix standard uncertainty and expanded uncertainty.

8. Statistical Analysis of Measurement Data

For readings x₁, x₂, …, xₙ:

Mean x̄ = (x₁ + x₂ + … + xₙ) / n
Sample standard deviation s = √[ Σ(xᵢ − x̄)² / (n − 1) ]
Standard uncertainty of the mean = s / √n

Use the standard deviation to describe scatter and the standard uncertainty of the mean when the mean of repeated observations is the reported quantity.

9. Measurement System Analysis

Measurement system analysis checks whether the measurement process is capable of providing dependable results. Important characteristics include bias, repeatability, reproducibility, stability and discrimination/resolution.

10. Calibration

Calibration establishes the relationship between instrument indications and values assigned by standards under specified conditions. It supports accuracy, traceability and confidence in measurement results.

Basic calibration sequence

  1. Select a suitable reference standard.
  2. Control the measurement environment.
  3. Apply known values across the required range.
  4. Record instrument indications.
  5. Determine deviations/corrections.
  6. Document the calibration and maintain traceability.

11. Air Gauging — Principle

Air gauging is a pneumatic comparative measurement method. Compressed air passes through a measuring nozzle toward the work surface. A change in the gap changes the air flow/back pressure, which is converted into an indication of dimensional variation.

Key idea: Air gauging is mainly a comparative method. It is useful for checking small dimensional variations and is particularly suitable for production inspection.

12. ISO Standards

ISO standards provide internationally agreed requirements and terminology that support consistent measurement, quality and traceability. In an exam answer, state the relevant standard only when the question or supplied material identifies it; do not invent a standard number.

Quick Revision

  • Metrology = science of measurement.
  • Measurement compares a quantity with a standard.
  • SWIPE = Standard, Workpiece, Instrument, Person, Environment.
  • Accuracy = closeness to true value.
  • Precision = repeatability of readings.
  • Error = measured value − true value.
  • Systematic errors are predictable/consistent; random errors are unpredictable.
  • Type A uncertainty uses statistical data; Type B uses other information.
  • Calibration establishes the relation between indication and a reference standard.
  • Air gauging works by relating air-flow/back-pressure change to dimensional gap.
