Unit I – Fundamental Concepts

  • Design process and factors influencing machine design
  • Material selection and mechanical properties
  • Preferred numbers and factor of safety
  • Direct, bending, torsional and combined loading
  • Principal stresses and eccentric loading
  • Curved beams: crane hook and C-frame
  • Theories of failure
  • Strength, stiffness, stress concentration and fluctuating stresses
  • Endurance limit and finite/infinite life design

Unit II – Shafts and Keys

  • Torque from power and speed
  • Design of shafts under bending and torsion
  • Maximum shear stress / specified failure theory
  • Keys: shear and crushing checks
  • Use PSG standard dimensions

Unit III – Couplings and Power Screws

  • Muff/flange coupling design sequence
  • Power screw: lead, helix angle and friction angle
  • Raising and lowering torque
  • Efficiency of screw
  • Welded joints and eccentric loading

Unit IV – Springs, Flywheels and Bearings

  • Spring stiffness and basic spring relations
  • Flywheel energy storage and speed fluctuation
  • Antifriction bearing principle
  • Hydrodynamic bearing: pressure, Sommerfeld number, friction, power loss, film thickness and oil flow

Unit V – Joints and Machine Components

  • Joint design and failure checks
  • Socket/cotter-joint proportions and stresses
  • Check tension, compression and shear as applicable
  • Follow standard proportions from PSG/reference data

Exam-Day Method

  1. Read the complete question and identify the design condition.
  2. Write Given and Find.
  3. Draw a small free-body/geometry sketch when required.
  4. Choose the stated failure theory and FOS.
  5. Write the governing formula before substitution.
  6. Check units and dimensions.
  7. Compare with a standard PSG size and state the adopted value.
  8. Box the final answer.

Final 30-Minute Checklist

  • Revise formulas and standard dimensions.
  • Practice at least one shaft/key problem.
  • Practice one power-screw problem.
  • Review coupling and weld-group design steps.
  • Review bearing terminology and hydrodynamic-bearing calculations.
  • Do not memorise an answer without understanding the design sequence.
