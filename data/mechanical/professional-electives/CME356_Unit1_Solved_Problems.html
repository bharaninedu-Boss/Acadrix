CME356 — Unit I Solved Problems

R-2021 • Rotating Machinery Design

Practice2M + 12M preparation

These are worked study problems built from the official Unit I syllabus. They are not claimed as verified Anna University PYQs.

Important: The official syllabus specifies conceptual coverage of operating regimes, creep, fatigue, influencing parameters and experimental characterisation. Where the syllabus does not provide numerical constants or a prescribed design equation, the problems below use clearly stated illustrative assumptions rather than invented university data.

Problem 1 — Operating-regime comparison

Question: A gas turbine is required to run continuously for long periods in one application and only during demand spikes in another. Identify the appropriate operating regimes and state the principal design concerns.

Given: Application A = continuous demand; Application B = demand spikes.
Step 1 — Identify regime: Continuous demand corresponds to base-load operation. Demand spikes correspond to peak-load operation.
Step 2 — Base-load concerns: Long-duration reliability, efficiency, thermal stability, creep resistance, fatigue life and service life.
Step 3 — Peak-load concerns: Load variation, transient response, start-stop effects, thermal cycling and fatigue caused by repeated changes in operating condition.
Final answer: Application A → base load; Application B → peak load. The design emphasis changes from long-duration durability to transient/load-cycle capability.

Problem 2 — Creep strain-rate calculation

Question: During a simplified constant-temperature creep test, the measured creep strain changes from 0.80% at 100 h to 1.10% at 250 h. Determine the average creep strain rate over this interval.

Given: ε₁ = 0.80% at t₁ = 100 h; ε₂ = 1.10% at t₂ = 250 h.
Find: Average creep strain rate.
Formula: Average creep rate = ε₂ − ε₁t₂ − t₁
Substitute: Average rate = (1.10 − 0.80) / (250 − 100) %/h
Arithmetic: = 0.30 / 150 = 0.002 %/h
Final answer: Average creep strain rate = 0.002 %/h over the specified interval.

This is an illustrative calculation showing how creep data are processed; it does not establish a material's allowable creep rate.

Problem 3 — Fatigue-cycle interpretation

Question: A component experiences a repeated loading cycle at a fixed stress amplitude. Explain what should be examined to assess fatigue damage.

Step 1: Determine the stress amplitude and mean stress of the loading cycle.
Step 2: Identify geometric stress concentrations such as fillets, grooves, holes and keyways.
Step 3: Consider surface finish, material condition, temperature, environment and residual stress.
Step 4: Relate the loading condition to fatigue data such as an S-N relationship where applicable.
Step 5: Assess crack initiation, crack propagation and final fracture risk.
Final answer: Fatigue assessment is not based only on nominal stress; stress amplitude, mean stress, cycles, geometry, surface and material/environmental factors must be considered.

Problem 4 — Fatigue-life percentage comparison

Question: In an illustrative fatigue test, Specimen A survives 120,000 cycles and Specimen B survives 80,000 cycles under the same stated test condition. By what percentage is the life of A greater than B?

Given: NA = 120,000 cycles; NB = 80,000 cycles.
Formula: Percentage increase = NA − NBNB × 100
Substitute: = (120,000 − 80,000) / 80,000 × 100
Arithmetic: = 40,000 / 80,000 × 100 = 50%
Final answer: Specimen A has 50% greater fatigue life than Specimen B under the stated illustrative condition.

Problem 5 — Creep versus fatigue diagnosis

Question: A turbine component operates at high temperature under nearly sustained stress for a long period, while another component undergoes repeated start-stop thermal cycles. Identify the dominant damage mechanism expected in each case.

Case A: High temperature + sustained stress + long duration → creep is a major concern.
Case B: Repeated thermal/loading cycles → fatigue is a major concern.
Engineering note: Real gas-turbine components can experience interacting creep-fatigue damage, so the mechanisms should not be treated as completely independent in a detailed assessment.

2-Mark Numerical/Concept Checks

  1. If creep strain changes from 0.5% to 0.9% in 200 h, calculate the average strain rate.
  2. A component survives 150,000 cycles while another survives 100,000 cycles. Calculate the percentage increase in life.
  3. For a constant-temperature test, which quantity is plotted against time to observe creep behaviour?
  4. Which damage mechanism is primarily associated with repeated or fluctuating loading?

12-Mark Answer Practice

  1. Explain creep and fatigue mechanisms in gas-turbine components and compare their influencing parameters.
  2. Explain the experimental procedure for a creep test and discuss the information obtained from the test.
  3. Explain fatigue testing, crack initiation, propagation and the engineering factors affecting fatigue life.
  4. Discuss how gas-turbine operating regimes influence creep-fatigue damage and mechanical design.
Full-working standard: Always write Given → Find → Formula → Substitute actual values → Arithmetic → Intermediate result → next formula if required → Final answer with units. For theory answers, use definition → mechanism → diagram/curve → factors → application → conclusion.
