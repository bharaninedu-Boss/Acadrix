CME362 — Unit IV

Energy Conservation in Major Electrical Utilities

R-2021 • Exam-focused notes

This unit covers energy conservation in motors, pumps, fans, blowers, compressed-air systems, refrigeration and air-conditioning systems, and illumination systems.

1. Electric Motors

Motors convert electrical energy into mechanical energy. Energy conservation mainly means operating the motor close to its suitable load, maintaining correct electrical conditions and avoiding unnecessary running.

Electrical supply → Motor → Shaft power → Driven equipment ↓ losses: copper + core + mechanical + stray

Important conservation measures

  • Select a motor of suitable rating instead of grossly oversized motors.
  • Maintain rated voltage and balanced three-phase supply.
  • Keep bearings, coupling and alignment in good condition.
  • Avoid running unloaded equipment unnecessarily.
  • Consider high-efficiency motors where operating hours and load justify replacement.
  • Use variable-speed control for suitable variable-load applications.
Motor efficiency η = Output power / Input power × 100%

2. Pumps and Pumping Systems

A pump supplies the required flow and pressure to a fluid system. Conservation requires matching pump capacity to actual system demand.

Reservoir → Pump → Pipe → Valve → Process ↑ Motor input power

Energy-saving measures

  • Operate near the pump's suitable operating region.
  • Avoid excessive throttling where a better control method is practical.
  • Use correctly sized pumps and motors.
  • Reduce unnecessary pressure drop in pipes, bends and valves.
  • Repair leakage and maintain seals.
  • Use variable-speed control for appropriate variable-flow systems.
Hydraulic power ≈ ρgQH

3. Fans and Blowers

Fans and blowers move air or gas. Their energy use increases when unnecessary pressure drop, excessive flow or poor control is present.

Air inlet → Fan / Blower → Duct → Filter → Process ↓ Motor power

Conservation measures

  • Keep filters, ducts and passages clean.
  • Minimize unnecessary system resistance.
  • Prevent air leakage.
  • Match fan capacity to the required flow.
  • Use suitable speed or flow control for variable demand.
  • Maintain alignment, bearings and belts.

4. Compressed Air Systems

Compressed air can be an energy-intensive utility because electrical energy is first converted to compressed-air energy and then used by pneumatic equipment.

Atmospheric air → Compressor → After-cooler / receiver → Dryer → Distribution → User ↓ pressure losses / leakage

Energy-saving measures

  • Find and repair leaks in the distribution network.
  • Keep compressor intake filters clean.
  • Avoid unnecessarily high system pressure.
  • Prevent artificial demand and unnecessary blow-off.
  • Use appropriately sized compressors and sequence multiple compressors according to demand.
  • Maintain dryers, drains, receivers and distribution lines.
  • Switch off compressors or isolated sections when production is stopped, where appropriate.

5. Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems

Refrigeration systems remove heat from a low-temperature space and reject it to a higher-temperature environment. Energy conservation focuses on reducing the work required for the desired cooling effect.

Evaporator → Compressor → Condenser → Expansion device ↑ ↓ └──────────── Refrigerant ─────────────┘

Important conservation measures

  • Maintain clean condenser and evaporator surfaces.
  • Prevent refrigerant leakage and maintain proper refrigerant charge.
  • Maintain correct chilled-space temperature rather than excessive cooling.
  • Improve insulation of cold surfaces and refrigerated spaces.
  • Maintain airflow and avoid blocked filters.
  • Use efficient control and avoid simultaneous unnecessary heating and cooling.
  • Maintain compressors, fans, pumps and controls properly.
COP = Refrigerating effect / Compressor work input

6. Illumination Systems

Energy conservation in lighting means providing the required illumination with minimum electrical energy while maintaining suitable visual conditions.

Electrical energy → Lamp / luminaire → Light → Work area ↓ useful illumination

Conservation measures

  • Use efficient light sources appropriate to the application.
  • Use daylight wherever practical.
  • Switch off unnecessary lighting.
  • Use occupancy or automatic controls where suitable.
  • Keep lamps and luminaires clean.
  • Use appropriate illumination levels rather than over-lighting.
  • Improve layout so light is directed effectively toward the required area.
Lighting efficacy = Light output / Electrical power input

How to Write a 13-Mark Answer

  1. Start with a short definition and objective.
  2. Draw a neat labelled system diagram.
  3. Explain the working or energy flow.
  4. Identify major losses or causes of high energy consumption.
  5. List practical conservation measures under clear subheadings.
  6. Add a relevant formula or performance indicator.
  7. Finish with a short conclusion.

Exam focus: For this unit, diagrams + conservation measures + performance indicators are more useful than writing long general introductions.
