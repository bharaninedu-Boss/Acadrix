Problem 1 — Wind Power Available at a Rotor

Given: Air density ρ = 1.225 kg/m³, rotor radius R = 20 m, wind speed V = 10 m/s.

Find: Theoretical power available in the wind.

Formula: 12ρAV³, where A = πR².

Area: A = π(20)² = 1256.64 m².

Substitute: P = 12(1.225)(1256.64)(10³).

Calculation: P ≈ 769,690 W.

Final answer: Pwind ≈ 769.7 kW.

Interpretation: This is the kinetic power crossing the swept area, not the actual electrical output of the turbine.

Problem 2 — Actual Turbine Power from Power Coefficient

Given: Wind power = 500 kW and power coefficient Cp = 0.40.

Find: Mechanical power extracted by the rotor.

Formula: Pturbine = CpPwind.

Substitute: Pturbine = 0.40 × 500.

Final answer: Pturbine = 200 kW.

Interpretation: Only 40% of the available wind power is converted to rotor mechanical power in this example.

Problem 3 — Betz Limit

Given: Wind power crossing a rotor = 1 MW. Assume an ideal turbine operating at the Betz limit.

Find: Maximum theoretical power extraction.

Formula: Cp,max = 1627 ≈ 0.5926.

Substitute: Pmax = 1627 × 1,000 kW.

Final answer: Pmax ≈ 592.6 kW.

Interpretation: The ideal actuator-disc limit is about 59.3% of the incoming wind power. Real turbines produce less because of practical losses.

Problem 4 — Wind Power for a Different Wind Speed

Given: A turbine operates at two wind speeds, V₁ = 8 m/s and V₂ = 12 m/s. Rotor area and air density are unchanged.

Find: Ratio of theoretical wind powers.

Formula: P ∝ V³.

Therefore: P₂P₁ = V₂³V₁³ = 12³8³ = 3.375.

Final answer: P₂/P₁ = 3.375, or 337.5% of P₁.

Interpretation: A 50% increase in wind speed produces a 237.5% increase in power relative to the original value, under the idealized proportionality.

Problem 5 — Swept Area and Wind Power

Given: Rotor diameter D = 30 m, air density ρ = 1.2 kg/m³, wind speed V = 8 m/s.

Find: Swept area and theoretical wind power.

Radius: R = D/2 = 15 m.

Area: A = π(15)² = 706.86 m².

Power: P = 12(1.2)(706.86)(8³) ≈ 173,580 W.

Final answer: A ≈ 706.86 m² and Pwind ≈ 173.6 kW.

Problem 6 — Turbine Output with Mechanical and Generator Efficiency

Given: Wind power = 800 kW, Cp = 0.42, mechanical efficiency ηm = 0.95, generator efficiency ηg = 0.92.

Find: Approximate electrical output.

Formula: Pe = PwindCpηmηg.

Substitute: Pe = 800 × 0.42 × 0.95 × 0.92.

Final answer: Pe ≈ 293.7 kW.

Interpretation: Aerodynamic, mechanical and electrical efficiencies compound, so electrical output is substantially below available wind power.

Problem 7 — Betz-Limit Check

Given: A proposed ideal turbine is claimed to have Cp = 0.65.

Find: Whether the claim is theoretically possible under the Betz actuator-disc limit.

Limit: Cp,max = 1627 ≈ 0.593.

Comparison: 0.65 > 0.593.

Final answer: The claimed coefficient exceeds the Betz limit and is not possible for an ideal actuator-disc wind turbine under the assumptions of the Betz limit.

Problem 8 — Capacity Factor

Given: Rated wind turbine capacity = 2 MW. Annual energy generated = 3,504 MWh. Time = 8,760 h/year.

Find: Capacity factor.

Formula: CF = Annual energy generatedRated power × hours.

Substitute: CF = 3,5042 × 8,760 = 0.20.

Final answer: Capacity factor = 20%.

Interpretation: The turbine generated energy equivalent to operating at rated power for 20% of the year.

Problem 9 — Wind-Farm Power from Multiple Turbines

Given: 10 turbines, each with rated electrical output 2 MW. Assume each operates at an average capacity factor of 0.30.

Find: Average farm output.

Total rated capacity: 10 × 2 = 20 MW.

Average output: Pavg = CF × rated capacity = 0.30 × 20.

Final answer: Average farm output = 6 MW.

Problem 10 — Energy Produced from Average Power

Given: Average electrical output = 4 MW over 24 h.

Find: Electrical energy generated.

Formula: E = Pt.

Substitute: E = 4 × 24 = 96 MWh.

Final answer: E = 96 MWh.
