Scope: rotational inertia, flight loading, precession, pressure gradient, torsion, seizure, blade release, engine mountings, bearings and major loads on rotating and pressure-casing components.

Problem 1 — Centrifugal force on a rotating component

Question: A simplified rotating mass of 2.5 kg is located at a radius of 0.18 m and rotates at 12,000 rpm. Determine the centrifugal force.

Given: m = 2.5 kg; r = 0.18 m; N = 12,000 rpm.
Find: Centrifugal force F.
Formula:F = mω²r
Step 1 — Convert speed: ω = 2πN60 = 2π(12000)60 = 1256.64 rad/s
Step 2 — Substitute: F = 2.5 × (1256.64)² × 0.18
Arithmetic: F ≈ 355,306 N.
Final answer: F ≈ 355.3 kN, acting radially outward.

Problem 2 — Torque required for angular acceleration

Question: A rotor has mass moment of inertia 4 kg·m² and is accelerated at 80 rad/s². Neglecting resisting torque, determine the accelerating torque.

Given: I = 4 kg·m²; α = 80 rad/s².
Formula:T = Iα
Substitute: T = 4 × 80
Arithmetic: T = 320 N·m.
Final answer: Accelerating torque = 320 N·m.

Problem 3 — Pressure-gradient force

Question: A simplified casing area of 0.025 m² is subjected to a pressure difference of 0.45 MPa. Determine the resultant pressure force.

Given: Δp = 0.45 MPa = 450,000 Pa; A = 0.025 m².
Formula:F = ΔpA
Substitute: F = 450,000 × 0.025
Arithmetic: F = 11,250 N.
Final answer: Pressure force = 11.25 kN.

Problem 4 — Torsional shear stress in a solid shaft

Question: A solid circular shaft of diameter 40 mm transmits a torque of 500 N·m. Determine the maximum torsional shear stress.

Given: T = 500 N·m = 500,000 N·mm; d = 40 mm.
Find: Maximum shear stress τmax.
Polar second moment:J = πd⁴32
Torsion equation:τmax = TrJ,   r = d2
Substitute J: J = π(40)⁴/32 = 251,327 mm⁴.
Substitute stress: τmax = (500,000 × 20) / 251,327
Arithmetic: τmax ≈ 39.79 N/mm².
Final answer: τmax ≈ 39.8 MPa.

Problem 5 — Angle of twist

Question: The same 40 mm solid shaft transmits 500 N·m over a length of 1.2 m. If G = 80 GPa, determine the angle of twist.

Given: T = 500,000 N·mm; L = 1200 mm; G = 80,000 N/mm²; J = 251,327 mm⁴.
Formula:θ = TLGJ
Substitute: θ = (500,000 × 1200) / (80,000 × 251,327)
Arithmetic: θ ≈ 0.02984 rad.
Convert to degrees: θ ≈ 0.02984 × 180/π ≈ 1.71°.
Final answer: Angle of twist ≈ 0.0298 rad ≈ 1.71°.

Problem 6 — Load identification for blade release

Question: A turbine blade is released from its attachment during operation. Identify the principal mechanical design checks that should follow.

Step 1: Determine blade mass, rotational speed and radial location.
Step 2: Evaluate the centrifugal loading associated with the released mass.
Step 3: Examine the blade attachment, disc integrity and surrounding casing.
Step 4: Assess transient impact/containment and possible secondary damage.
Step 5: Check inspection, fatigue, material and safety requirements.
Final answer: Blade release is an abnormal transient event requiring retention, containment and secondary-damage assessment rather than only a steady-state stress check.

Problem 7 — Bearing and mounting load path

Question: Explain how a rotating shaft load is transferred through bearings and engine mountings to the supporting structure.

Step 1: Identify radial, axial, torque and dynamic loads acting on the rotating assembly.
Step 2: Bearings provide radial support and/or axial location while permitting rotation.
Step 3: Bearing reactions are transmitted to the bearing housing and engine mounting structure.
Step 4: Mountings transfer the resulting loads to the supporting structure while maintaining alignment and accommodating controlled thermal movement.
Final answer: Shaft → bearings → housings/mountings → supporting structure is the basic load path, with dynamic and thermal effects included in detailed design.
