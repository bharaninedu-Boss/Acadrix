CME372 — Unit III: Visual Realism and CAD Standards

R-2021 • CAD and CAE • Exam-oriented notes

Syllabus: hidden surface removal; depth-buffer, scan-line/span/area coherence, Warnock and priority algorithms; shading, coloring, animation; GKS, OpenGL; IGES, STEP and CALS.

1. Visual Realism

Visual realism means producing a displayed image that gives a convincing representation of the shape, depth, surfaces, lighting and material appearance of an object. In CAD, realistic visualization helps designers inspect form and communicate designs.

2. Hidden Surface Removal

When a 3D model is projected onto a 2D display, surfaces may overlap. Hidden-surface algorithms determine which surfaces should be visible from the selected viewpoint.

Depth-buffer method

The depth buffer stores the depth value associated with each displayed pixel. When a new surface is considered, its depth is compared with the stored value; the nearer surface is retained.

  • Simple conceptual approach.
  • Works at pixel level.
  • Requires additional depth memory.

Scan-line and span coherence

Scan-line methods process the image one horizontal line at a time. Coherence exploits the fact that nearby pixels or scan lines often have similar visibility relationships, reducing repeated work.

Area coherence

Area coherence groups regions with similar visibility characteristics instead of treating every pixel independently.

Warnock algorithm

Warnock uses recursive area subdivision. If visibility for a region cannot be determined directly, the region is subdivided until visibility can be resolved at a sufficiently small area.

Priority algorithms

Priority methods assign an ordering to surfaces and use that order to resolve visibility. They are useful when surface relationships can be determined reliably.

3. Shading

Shading calculates how light intensity varies over a surface so that its shape appears three-dimensional.

  • Flat shading: one intensity/normal treatment per polygon.
  • Gouraud-type interpolation: intensity is interpolated from vertex values.
  • Smooth/normal-based methods: produce a more continuous appearance.

4. Colouring and Animation

Colouring assigns visual attributes to objects or surfaces to improve identification and realism. Animation displays successive frames to show motion, assembly, mechanism operation or deformation concepts.

5. GKS

GKS (Graphical Kernel System) is a standardized graphics interface concept intended to provide a consistent set of graphics functions independent of a particular application.

6. OpenGL

OpenGL is a graphics API used for rendering 2D and 3D graphics. It provides a standardized programming interface for graphical operations and is useful for visualization applications.

7. CAD Data-Exchange Standards

StandardPurpose
IGESExchange of product-definition data between CAD systems.
STEPStandardized representation and exchange of product data across the product life cycle.
CALSStandards-oriented approach associated with digital technical information and data exchange.

8. Why CAD Standards Matter

  • Reduce dependence on one proprietary system.
  • Support data exchange between organizations and software.
  • Improve long-term accessibility of engineering data.
  • Support collaborative design and manufacturing.
