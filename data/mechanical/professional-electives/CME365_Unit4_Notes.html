CME365 — Unit IV: Bio-Energy

R-2021 • Renewable Energy Technologies

2 Marks12 Marks

Official syllabus: Bio resources; direct combustion; thermochemical, biochemical and mechanical conversion; gasifiers and types; cogeneration; carbonisation; pyrolysis; biogas and digesters; biodiesel; ethanol; applications.

1. Bio-Energy and Bio-Resources

Bio-energy is useful energy obtained from biological material called biomass. Biomass may come from agricultural residues, forestry residues, animal wastes, organic municipal wastes and energy crops. The resource should be assessed for moisture, availability, composition, collection and transport requirements.

ResourceExamples
Agricultural residuesStraw, husks, stalks and other crop residues.
Forestry residuesWood residues, branches and sawmill wastes.
Animal wastesCattle dung and other biodegradable wastes.
Organic wastesBiodegradable municipal and food-processing wastes.
Energy cropsPurpose-grown biomass selected for energy production.

2. Biomass Conversion Routes

Biomass can be converted into heat, power or fuels through several routes. The choice depends on moisture content, feedstock type, desired product and process conditions.

Biomass ├── Direct combustion → Heat / Steam → Power ├── Thermochemical → Char / Gas / Liquid fuels ├── Biochemical → Biogas / Ethanol └── Mechanical → Oils / densified fuels / prepared feedstock

3. Direct Combustion

Direct combustion burns biomass with an adequate supply of oxygen to release heat. The heat can be used directly for process heating or to produce steam for power generation.

  • Simple and well-established route.
  • Suitable for relatively dry biomass.
  • Moisture reduces useful thermal performance.
  • Combustion equipment must control emissions and ash.

4. Thermochemical Conversion

Thermochemical processes use heat to transform biomass into useful solid, gaseous or liquid products.

ProcessMain ideaTypical products
CarbonisationHeating biomass with limited oxygen to form a carbon-rich solid.Char/charcoal, gases and vapours.
PyrolysisThermal decomposition in the absence or near absence of oxygen.Char, gases and liquid bio-oil.
GasificationPartial oxidation at high temperature.Combustible producer/synthesis gas.

5. Biochemical Conversion

Biochemical conversion uses microorganisms or biological processes to convert suitable biomass into fuels.

  • Anaerobic digestion: microorganisms decompose biodegradable material without oxygen and produce biogas.
  • Fermentation: sugars or other suitable feedstocks are biologically converted to ethanol.

6. Mechanical Conversion

Mechanical processing changes the physical form or handles biomass without fundamentally changing its chemical composition. Examples include size reduction, drying, pressing and densification. These operations improve handling, transport, storage or preparation for later conversion.

7. Gasifiers

A gasifier converts carbonaceous biomass into a combustible gas by controlled partial oxidation at elevated temperature. The resulting gas may contain carbon monoxide, hydrogen, methane, carbon dioxide, nitrogen and other constituents depending on the feedstock and gasifying medium.

Biomass + controlled air/steam ↓ GASIFIER ↓ Producer / synthesis gas ↓ Cleaning → Engine / burner / process

Types of Gasifiers

TypeBasic characteristic
DowndraftGas and solid flow generally move downward; can provide relatively lower-tar gas under suitable conditions.
UpdraftGas generally rises against downward-moving solid fuel; good thermal efficiency but gas may contain more tar.
CrossdraftGasifying medium and gas flow across the fuel bed.
Fluidized-bedParticles are suspended by the gasifying medium, providing good mixing and heat transfer.

8. Cogeneration

Cogeneration, or combined heat and power (CHP), produces useful electrical/mechanical power and useful heat from the same primary energy input. Biomass cogeneration can use steam systems, gas engines or other suitable conversion technologies.

Overall cogeneration efficiency = Useful power + useful heatFuel energy input

9. Carbonisation

Carbonisation is the thermal conversion of biomass into a carbon-rich solid, commonly called charcoal, by heating with restricted oxygen. It also produces gases and vapours that may be recovered depending on the process.

10. Pyrolysis

Pyrolysis is thermal decomposition of biomass in the absence or near absence of oxygen. Product distribution depends on temperature, heating rate, residence time and feedstock properties.

  • Slow pyrolysis: favours solid char formation.
  • Fast pyrolysis: can favour liquid bio-oil when suitable feedstock and process conditions are used.
  • Products: solid char, condensable liquids and non-condensable gases.

11. Biogas

Biogas is produced by anaerobic digestion of biodegradable organic matter. It mainly contains methane and carbon dioxide, with smaller quantities of other gases depending on the feedstock and process.

Organic feed → Mixing / preparation → Anaerobic digester → Biogas → Cleaning / use ↓ Digestate

Stages of Anaerobic Digestion

  1. Hydrolysis: complex organic matter is broken into simpler compounds.
  2. Acidogenesis: simpler compounds are converted into organic acids and related intermediates.
  3. Acetogenesis: intermediates are converted mainly into acetate, hydrogen and carbon dioxide.
  4. Methanogenesis: methanogenic microorganisms form methane.

Digesters

A biogas digester provides an enclosed environment for anaerobic digestion. Important considerations include feedstock characteristics, temperature, retention time, pH, loading and gas collection.

12. Biodiesel

Biodiesel is a renewable diesel-type fuel produced from suitable vegetable oils, animal fats or other lipid feedstocks. A common production route is transesterification, in which triglycerides react with an alcohol in the presence of a catalyst to form fatty-acid esters and glycerol.

Triglyceride + alcohol → Fatty-acid ester (biodiesel) + glycerol

Feedstock quality, catalyst, alcohol ratio, temperature and reaction time influence the process. Biodiesel can be used in compression-ignition engines subject to fuel specifications and engine/manufacturer requirements.

13. Ethanol

Bioethanol is ethanol produced from biological feedstocks. Sugars can be fermented directly, while starch-containing materials generally require conversion to fermentable sugars before fermentation.

Biomass / starch feedstock → Pretreatment / hydrolysis → Sugars → Fermentation → Ethanol → Purification

Ethanol can be used as a fuel or fuel blend where standards and engine compatibility permit.

14. Applications of Bio-Energy

  • Cooking and heating.
  • Industrial process heat.
  • Steam and electricity generation.
  • Biogas for heat and power.
  • Biodiesel for suitable diesel applications.
  • Bioethanol as a transport-fuel component.
  • Biomass-based cogeneration in suitable industries.
