1. Why are there different views of PLM?

In product development, information is not created in one place or by one department. Engineering teams create drawings and models, product-development teams define product structures, different teams collaborate on product decisions, and the wider organisation needs product information throughout the life cycle.

Because the scope of information management developed over time, several related terms are used. They should be understood by their main emphasis and scope, rather than treated as unrelated subjects.

Core idea: EDM → engineering information focus; PDM → product-definition focus; cPDm → collaborative product-definition focus; CPC → broader collaborative product activity; PLM → broader life-cycle management.

2. Engineering Data Management (EDM)

Meaning

Engineering Data Management (EDM) focuses on organising and controlling engineering data and documents used during engineering activities.

What type of information is involved?

  • Engineering drawings
  • Design documents
  • Technical specifications
  • Engineering calculations and reports
  • Other engineering records

Why is EDM needed?

Engineering information can exist as many files produced by different engineers. Without control, users may work with duplicate, incorrectly named or obsolete documents. EDM provides a structured way to manage engineering information.

Simple example

Suppose an engineer creates a drawing for a gearbox housing. EDM-oriented management is concerned with keeping that engineering document organised, identifiable and controlled so that the correct engineering information can be found and used.

Exam points

  • Focus: engineering data and documents.
  • Main concern: organisation and control of engineering information.
  • It represents a narrower information-management view than full PLM.

3. Product Data Management (PDM)

Meaning

Product Data Management (PDM) manages product-definition information in a controlled environment. The scope includes documents and engineering information together with product structures, versions, revisions and related information.

Why is PDM broader than basic engineering-document management?

A product is more than one drawing. A manufactured product may consist of assemblies, subassemblies and individual parts. Each part can have drawings, specifications, CAD models and revisions. PDM helps maintain the relationships between these pieces of product information.

Important PDM concepts

  • Product structure: represents how parts and assemblies are related.
  • Revision: distinguishes controlled versions of product information.
  • Document control: keeps documents associated with the relevant product definition.
  • Identification: helps uniquely recognise product items and information.
  • Access control: controls who may view or modify information.

Example

For a gearbox assembly, PDM can maintain the assembly structure, individual gear and shaft information, associated drawings and controlled revisions. The important idea is the relationship between product information, not merely storing individual files.

4. Collaborative Product Definition Management (cPDm)

Meaning

Collaborative Product Definition Management (cPDm) emphasises the collaborative management of product definition during product development.

Modern products are developed by people from different functions. Mechanical design, electrical design, manufacturing, quality, procurement and other participants may contribute to the product definition. Collaboration therefore becomes an important part of managing the product definition.

What does “collaborative” mean here?

  • Multiple participants can work with controlled product information.
  • Relevant users can access information according to their roles.
  • Product-development activities can be coordinated.
  • Changes and decisions can be communicated through controlled processes.
  • Participants can work from a common product definition instead of isolated copies.

Example

Consider a new gearbox. The mechanical designer defines the housing and gears, manufacturing evaluates manufacturability, quality checks requirements, and other functions provide relevant input. cPDm represents the collaborative management of the product definition across these participants.

5. Collaborative Product Commerce (CPC)

Meaning

Collaborative Product Commerce (CPC) represents a broader collaborative view around product information and product-related activities.

The important word is collaborative. Product-related information is valuable beyond the engineering department. Different functions and participants need to work together using consistent information.

Typical emphasis

  • Collaboration around product information.
  • Coordination between product-related activities.
  • Sharing relevant product knowledge across organisational functions.
  • Connecting product information with wider business activities.
Exam caution: Do not define CPC simply as “selling a product online.” In the CME344 terminology, the emphasis is on collaborative product-related activities and information.

6. Product Life Cycle Management (PLM)

Meaning

Product Life Cycle Management (PLM) is the broader approach for managing product information, product-related processes and collaboration throughout the product life cycle.

The life-cycle perspective means that product information is considered from early product definition and development through production, use/service and later life-cycle activities. PLM therefore provides a wider framework than a system concerned only with engineering documents or product-definition data.

What does PLM manage?

  • Product information
  • Product structures and related information
  • Processes and workflows associated with product information
  • Changes and revisions
  • Collaboration between relevant participants
  • Information required at different life-cycle stages

Example

For an automobile component, PLM can provide the broader life-cycle context: product definition, engineering information, controlled changes, manufacturing-related use, quality information, service feedback and future product improvement.

9. One Product Example — Gearbox

Use one common example to understand all five terms.

EDM view

The engineering team needs controlled drawings, specifications and engineering documents for the gearbox.

PDM view

The gearbox is represented as a product structure containing housing, shafts, gears and other components, with associated documents and revisions.

cPDm view

Different product-development participants collaborate around the controlled gearbox definition and related development information.

CPC view

The product information supports broader collaborative product-related activities across relevant functions and business participants.

PLM view

The gearbox information is considered across its broader life cycle, including development, production, use/service, changes and feedback for improvement.

Memory technique: Ask five questions — “Am I controlling engineering documents? Product definition? Collaboration on the definition? Wider product collaboration? Or the whole life cycle?”

10. Important 2-Mark Questions

Q1. What is EDM?

Answer: Engineering Data Management is the organised management and control of engineering data and documents used in engineering activities.

Q2. What is PDM?

Answer: Product Data Management is the controlled management of product-definition information such as documents, product structures, versions and related data.

Q3. Expand cPDm.

Answer: cPDm stands for Collaborative Product Definition Management.

Q4. What is the main idea of cPDm?

Answer: It focuses on collaborative management of the product definition among participants involved in product development.

Q5. Expand CPC.

Answer: CPC stands for Collaborative Product Commerce.

Q6. What is PLM?

Answer: PLM is an organised approach for managing product information, related processes and collaboration throughout the product life cycle.

Q7. Differentiate EDM and PDM.

Answer: EDM mainly focuses on engineering data and documents, whereas PDM manages broader product-definition information including product structures, revisions and related documents.

Q8. Why is PLM considered broader than PDM?

Answer: PDM primarily controls product-definition information, while PLM considers product information, related processes and collaboration across the broader product life cycle.

11. Full 12-Mark Answer

Q. Explain the different views of PLM — EDM, PDM, cPDm, CPC and PLM.

Introduction

Product development generates large quantities of engineering and product information. Different departments must create, use, modify and communicate this information. The terminology EDM, PDM, cPDm, CPC and PLM represents different views and scopes of managing such information and collaboration.

1. Engineering Data Management (EDM)

EDM focuses on engineering data and documents. It helps organise and control drawings, specifications, reports and other engineering information. The major concern is that engineering personnel can identify and use the required information without confusion caused by duplicated or uncontrolled documents.

2. Product Data Management (PDM)

PDM extends the information-management view to the product definition. Along with engineering documents, it deals with product structures, versions, revisions and relationships between product information. For example, a gearbox assembly can be associated with its components, drawings and controlled revisions.

3. Collaborative Product Definition Management (cPDm)

cPDm emphasises collaboration around the product definition. Product development involves multiple participants and functions. Controlled access to common product information helps these participants coordinate their activities and work with a consistent product definition.

4. Collaborative Product Commerce (CPC)

CPC provides a broader collaborative view around product information and product-related activities. The emphasis is on collaboration among relevant functions and participants rather than limiting product information to an individual engineering group.

5. Product Life Cycle Management (PLM)

PLM provides the broader life-cycle perspective. It manages product information, product-related processes and collaboration throughout the product life cycle. Thus, information is considered not only during engineering development but also in its wider product context and later life-cycle activities.

Diagram

Draw the EDM → PDM → cPDm → CPC → broader PLM relationship diagram and explain that the diagram represents increasing scope and emphasis, not necessarily a rigid sequence of software products.

Conclusion

EDM, PDM, cPDm, CPC and PLM are related views of managing engineering/product information and collaboration. PLM provides the broadest life-cycle perspective, while EDM and PDM are more focused on engineering and product-definition information respectively.

12. How to Score Better in This 12-Mark Question

  1. Start with the common problem: large and distributed product information.
  2. Write the full forms correctly.
  3. Explain each term separately; do not only give a comparison table.
  4. Give one practical example for at least PDM and PLM.
  5. Draw the relationship diagram neatly.
  6. Use the comparison table near the end to reinforce the differences.
  7. Finish with a clear conclusion.
