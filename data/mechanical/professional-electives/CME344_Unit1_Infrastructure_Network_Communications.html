1. What is PLM/PDM Infrastructure?

PLM/PDM infrastructure is the technical foundation that allows product information to be created, identified, stored, communicated, controlled, accessed and connected with other engineering or enterprise applications.

A PLM system is useful only when engineers and other authorised users can reliably reach the right product information. Therefore, infrastructure includes more than the PLM application itself. It involves connectivity, communication facilities, data-management mechanisms, access control and interfaces to other systems.

Easy idea: PLM is the controlled environment for product information; infrastructure is the foundation that allows that environment to operate.

2. Why is Infrastructure Needed?

Modern products generate information in many departments and at many stages. For example, a mechanical product may have requirements, CAD models, drawings, bills of materials, specifications, analysis results, manufacturing documents, quality records and service information.

  • Users may be located in different offices or plants.
  • Different applications may create different types of information.
  • Product information must be available to authorised users.
  • Large files such as CAD models need dependable transfer and storage.
  • Different applications may need to exchange related information.
  • Access and revision control must be maintained while information is shared.
  • The system should support collaboration without creating uncontrolled copies.

3. Basic PLM/PDM Infrastructure Architecture

The exact implementation differs between organisations, but the important examination idea is the relationship between network, communications, data management and connected applications.

4. Network in PLM/PDM

A network provides connectivity between the people, computers, servers and locations that participate in product development and product information management.

4.1 Role of the Network

  • Connects users to PLM/PDM services.
  • Connects distributed engineering teams.
  • Provides a path for accessing product data.
  • Supports communication between PLM/PDM and other applications.
  • Allows product information to be used across departments and sites.
  • Supports controlled sharing instead of uncontrolled manual transfer.

4.2 Example

Suppose a company has design engineers at one site and manufacturing engineers at another. The network provides connectivity so that authorised users can access the PLM/PDM environment and work with the required released product information.

Important distinction: The network provides the connectivity path. It does not by itself decide which drawing is approved or who may modify it. Those controls belong to the PLM/PDM application, data-management and access-control mechanisms.

5. Communications in PLM/PDM

Communication refers to the controlled exchange of information between users, PLM/PDM components and connected applications.

5.1 Why communication matters

Product development is collaborative. A change in one part of the product definition can affect design, analysis, manufacturing, purchasing, quality and service. The infrastructure therefore needs reliable information exchange.

  • Exchange of product information between authorised users.
  • Exchange between PLM/PDM and engineering applications.
  • Communication of product status and changes.
  • Transfer of required documents and data.
  • Support for geographically distributed collaboration.

6. Data Management in PLM/PDM

Data management is the controlled handling of product information so that it can be stored, identified, related, retrieved and maintained throughout its required life-cycle context.

6.1 Types of Product Information

BOMList of components in an assemblyDefines product structure.
SpecificationMaterial or performance requirementDefines required characteristics.

6.2 What Data Management Controls

  1. Identification: gives product information a recognisable identity.
  2. Storage: maintains information in the controlled environment.
  3. Metadata: stores information about the product data, such as description, status or revision.
  4. Relationships: connects related information such as a CAD model, drawing and BOM.
  5. Revision: distinguishes successive versions of product information.
  6. Status: indicates the state of information, such as being under development or released.
  7. Retrieval: helps authorised users find the required information.
  8. Traceability: preserves the history and relationships needed to understand changes.

7. Why Product Data Cannot Be Treated as Ordinary Files

A product file often has relationships with other product information. A CAD model may correspond to a drawing; the drawing may belong to a particular part number and revision; that part may occur in a BOM; the BOM may belong to an assembly; and the assembly may be used in a higher-level product.

8. Heterogeneous Data Sources

Heterogeneous data sources are sources that differ in application, format, structure, platform or method of storing information.

Examples in a Mechanical Engineering Environment

  • CAD system containing geometry.
  • CAE system containing analysis models and results.
  • ERP system containing enterprise or planning information.
  • Legacy database containing older product records.
  • Document repositories containing specifications and reports.
  • Quality systems containing inspection information.

Problems Created by Heterogeneity

  • Different file formats.
  • Different data structures.
  • Different identification conventions.
  • Different revision and status methods.
  • Duplicate information.
  • Difficulty maintaining a consistent product definition.
  • Difficulty integrating older applications with newer systems.

PLM/PDM can exist as a central environment while interacting with other engineering and enterprise applications.

Exam memory: Think of PLM/PDM as the controlled centre and CAD, CAE, ERP and legacy systems as connected information environments.

10. How the Infrastructure Works as One System

Step 1 — User creates or requests information

An authorised engineer may create a CAD model or request an existing product document.

Step 2 — Network provides connectivity

The user's workstation reaches the PLM/PDM environment through the available network infrastructure.

Step 3 — Communication enables exchange

Product information and requests are exchanged between the user, PLM/PDM services and connected applications.

Step 4 — Data management identifies the information

The system associates the information with the required product identity, attributes, relationships and lifecycle status.

Step 5 — Access control is applied

The system determines whether the user is allowed to view, modify, review, approve or use the information.

Step 6 — Related applications exchange required information

Where integration exists, the PLM/PDM environment can work with connected engineering or enterprise applications.

Step 7 — Controlled information is used

Users work with the required product information instead of relying on uncontrolled personal copies.

Step 8 — Changes are managed

When product information changes, the corresponding lifecycle and revision controls maintain the relationship between the old and new information.

11. Complete Practical Example — Gearbox Housing

Situation: A design engineer creates a new gearbox housing.

  1. The engineer creates the CAD model.
  2. The model is identified with the required product/part information.
  3. The CAD data and related information are stored in the controlled PLM/PDM environment.
  4. The drawing and other related information are associated with the product definition.
  5. Authorised reviewers examine the design.
  6. After approval, the appropriate information is released for downstream use.
  7. Manufacturing and other authorised users access the released information.
  8. A design problem is later discovered and a change is required.
  9. The change is processed through the controlled lifecycle, producing a new revision as appropriate.
  10. The previous information remains traceable while users are directed toward the appropriate current status.

What the infrastructure contributes: network connectivity, communication, controlled data handling and connection with relevant applications. The PLM/PDM environment then applies product-information and process controls.

12. Advantages of a Good PLM/PDM Infrastructure

  • Reliable access to controlled product information.
  • Better collaboration between distributed teams.
  • Improved information consistency.
  • Better connection between engineering and enterprise applications.
  • Improved traceability.
  • Reduced uncontrolled duplication.
  • Better support for product changes and revisions.
  • Improved availability of information to authorised downstream users.

13. Problems and Challenges

  • Integration of heterogeneous applications can be difficult.
  • Legacy data may have inconsistent structures or identification.
  • Large engineering files require appropriate infrastructure.
  • Distributed users require dependable connectivity.
  • Access permissions must be maintained correctly.
  • Users need training and process discipline.
  • Data migration can require considerable effort.

14. Important 2-Mark Questions & Answers

Q1. What is PLM/PDM infrastructure?

Answer: PLM/PDM infrastructure is the technical foundation used to connect users and systems and to create, communicate, store, control and integrate product information.

Q2. State the role of a network in PLM.

Answer: A network provides connectivity between PLM/PDM users, servers, locations and connected applications so that authorised product information can be accessed and exchanged.

Q3. What is meant by communication in PLM/PDM?

Answer: Communication is the controlled exchange of product information between users, PLM/PDM components and connected applications.

Q4. What is data management in PLM?

Answer: Data management is the controlled handling of product information including its identification, storage, attributes, relationships, revision, retrieval and traceability.

Q5. Define heterogeneous data sources.

Answer: Heterogeneous data sources are information sources that differ in application, format, structure, platform or method of storing product information.

Q6. Give two examples of applications connected with PLM/PDM.

Answer: CAD and CAE are examples. ERP and legacy applications are also common connected environments.

Q7. Why is data management important in PLM?

Answer: It helps ensure that product information is correctly identified, stored, related, retrieved and maintained so users can work with the required product definition.

Q8. State two problems caused by heterogeneous data.

Answer: Different data formats and structures can make integration difficult; different identification or revision conventions can also create inconsistency and duplication.

15. Full 12-Mark Answer

Explain PLM/PDM infrastructure with network and communications, data management, and heterogeneous data sources and applications.

Introduction

Product Life Cycle Management and Product Data Management require a technical environment in which product information can be created, stored, exchanged and controlled. This technical foundation is called the PLM/PDM infrastructure.

1. Network

The network connects users, servers, sites and applications. It provides the connectivity required for authorised users to access the PLM/PDM environment and product information.

2. Communications

Communication enables the exchange of product information between users, PLM/PDM services and connected applications. It supports collaboration and the movement of required information between participating systems.

3. Data Management

Data management controls product information by providing identification, storage, metadata, relationships, retrieval, revision/status control and traceability. It ensures that product data is treated as controlled product information rather than as unrelated files.

4. Heterogeneous Data Sources

Product information can originate from different applications and systems such as CAD, CAE, ERP, legacy databases and document systems. These sources may use different formats, structures and identification methods.

5. Application Integration

PLM/PDM may need to exchange information with engineering and enterprise applications. Integration helps maintain useful relationships between product definition and information held in other systems.

Working

A user creates or requests product information. The network provides connectivity and communication enables the exchange. The PLM/PDM environment identifies and manages the information, applies access controls and maintains relationships and lifecycle status. Connected applications exchange the information required for their activities. When changes occur, the controlled data-management mechanisms maintain revision and traceability.

Diagram to draw

Draw the architecture shown above: PLM/PDM Core → Network, Communication, Data Management, Access Control → CAD/CAE, ERP and Legacy/Other Applications.

Advantages

  • Controlled access to product information.
  • Improved collaboration.
  • Better information consistency.
  • Improved integration between applications.
  • Better revision and traceability.

Conclusion

A PLM/PDM infrastructure provides the connectivity, communication and data-management foundation required to control product information across users and applications. It is especially important when product information comes from heterogeneous engineering and enterprise systems.
