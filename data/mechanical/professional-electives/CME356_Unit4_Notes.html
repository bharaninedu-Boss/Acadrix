CME356 — Unit IV: Blade Design

R-2021 • Rotating Machinery Design • Exam-oriented notes

2 Marks12 Marks

Official syllabus scope: discs and blades; stresses in conventional axial-flow blades; simple desktop method; leaning effects; flanges and bolted structures; leakage through flanged joints; fatigue failure.

1. Role of Discs and Blades

In an axial-flow gas turbine, blades extract energy from the flowing gas and transfer it to the rotor. The disc carries the blades and transmits torque to the shaft. Both components experience high rotational speed, temperature and mechanical loading.

2. Main Blade Loads

  • Centrifugal force: caused by blade mass rotating at high speed.
  • Aerodynamic force: caused by gas pressure and velocity changes.
  • Thermal loading: caused by high temperature and temperature gradients.
  • Vibratory loading: caused by periodic excitation and flow disturbances.
  • Contact/attachment loads: transferred through the blade root.

3. Centrifugal Stress in a Blade

Fc = mω²r

For a distributed blade, the load is obtained from its mass distribution. The designer considers area, density and radius along the span.

4. Aerodynamic Bending

Gas forces acting on the airfoil produce bending moments.

σb = My/I

5. Torsion of Blades

When aerodynamic force does not pass through the torsional axis, twisting results. Blade shape, twist distribution and attachment geometry influence torsional behaviour.

6. Thermal Stress

Temperature gradients can produce restrained thermal expansion and thermal stress.

σth ≈ EαΔT

This is an idealised fully restrained elastic relation; real blades require thermo-mechanical analysis.

7. Simple Desktop Method

Define geometry, speed, temperature and material → estimate mass and centrifugal load → estimate aerodynamic load → calculate critical-section stresses → combine relevant stresses → check vibration, fatigue, creep and attachment.

8. Blade Leaning Effects

Blade leaning displaces the blade stack from a simple radial arrangement. It can alter aerodynamic loading, load distribution, centrifugal effects, stress distribution and vibration behaviour.

9. Flanges and Bolted Structures

  • Bolt preload creates clamping force.
  • External loads redistribute load between bolt and joint.
  • Temperature differences can change preload.
  • Joint stiffness and contact surfaces influence load sharing.
  • Threads and local geometry can create stress concentrations.

10. Leakage Through Flanged Joints

Leakage can result from inadequate preload, distortion, surface imperfections, thermal deformation or gasket/seal problems.

Good joint design: sufficient preload + controlled deformation + suitable sealing surface + thermal compatibility + correct tightening.

11. Fatigue Failure of Blades

Repeated aerodynamic excitation, start-stop cycles, thermal cycling and vibratory stress can initiate and propagate fatigue cracks.

Repeated excitation → cyclic stress → crack initiation → crack growth → possible fracture

12. Blade Root and Attachment

The blade root transfers centrifugal and aerodynamic loads to the disc. High local stress, fretting, contact pressure and manufacturing variation must be controlled.

13. Combined Stress Check

σcombined = σcentrifugal ± σbending ± σthermal ± σvibratory

The actual signs and stress state depend on the location and loading condition.

16. Quick Revision

Centrifugal: Fc = mω²r.
Bending: σb = My/I.
Thermal: σth ≈ EαΔT for a simple fully restrained case.
Blade design: centrifugal + aerodynamic + thermal + vibratory checks.
Joint: preload must maintain sealing/contact under operating loads.
