CME380 — Unit I: Vehicle Structure and Engines

R-2021 • Important 12/13-Mark Focus

Simple exam-oriented notes with standard diagrams. Learn the structure first, then the engine and aerodynamic ideas.

Syllabus verified: Unit I covers types of automobiles, vehicle construction and layouts, chassis, frame and body, vehicle aerodynamics, IC-engine components/functions/materials and variable valve timing (VVT). Official syllabus.

1. Types of Automobiles

An automobile is a self-propelled vehicle used mainly for transporting people or goods. Vehicles can be classified by purpose, load capacity, number of wheels, fuel/power source and drive arrangement.

By use

  • Passenger vehicles — cars, buses
  • Goods vehicles — trucks, vans
  • Special-purpose vehicles — ambulances, fire vehicles

By drive arrangement

  • Front-wheel drive (FWD)
  • Rear-wheel drive (RWD)
  • Four-wheel drive (4WD)

Exam: Be ready to explain different vehicle layouts with a simple power-flow sketch and compare their main features.

2. Vehicle Construction — Chassis, Frame and Body

Chassis: the basic supporting structure on which major automobile systems are mounted. It carries the power plant, transmission, axles, suspension, steering and braking systems and supports the body.

Main functions of the frame

  • Supports vehicle components and body.
  • Carries static and dynamic loads.
  • Provides adequate stiffness with controlled weight.
  • Maintains correct relative position of mounted components.

Common construction idea

12/13-mark: Explain chassis, frame and body construction with a neat labelled sketch. Include functions and suitable construction materials.

3. Vehicle Layouts

A vehicle layout describes the relative position of the engine and the driven wheels.

LayoutBasic ideaMain point
Front engine–front wheel driveEngine at front; front wheels driveCompact power train and good cabin packaging
Front engine–rear wheel driveEngine at front; rear wheels driveSeparate drive path to rear axle
Four-wheel driveDrive supplied to all four wheelsImproved traction on difficult surfaces

Important: Draw a simple block/power-flow diagram rather than writing only definitions.

4. Vehicle Aerodynamics

Aerodynamic forces act on a moving vehicle because air flows around the vehicle body. The major effects considered in the syllabus are resistances and moments.

Main aerodynamic effects

  • Drag: resistance opposing forward motion.
  • Lift: vertical aerodynamic force.
  • Side force: lateral force caused by airflow, especially in crosswinds.
  • Pitch, yaw and roll moments: turning effects produced by aerodynamic forces.

Exam point: A smooth body reduces flow separation and can reduce aerodynamic drag. Explain the force direction clearly in the diagram.

6. Variable Valve Timing (VVT)

VVT changes the timing of valve operation according to engine operating conditions. The objective is to improve breathing and obtain a better balance between performance, fuel economy and emissions over different speeds and loads.

Basic idea

  1. Engine operating condition is detected by the control system.
  2. The valve timing mechanism changes the cam/valve timing.
  3. Valve opening and closing are adjusted to suit the operating condition.
  4. The engine can operate more effectively over a wider speed range.

12/13-mark: Explain the need, basic operation and benefits of VVT with a simple valve-timing sketch. Do not confuse valve timing with fuel injection timing.
