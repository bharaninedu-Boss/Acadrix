Versatility of Value Engineering

Exam-friendly notes on applying Value Engineering beyond conventional product-development studies, including maintenance, non-hardware projects, programme initiation, training and career development.

Syllabus basis: Anna University R-2021 lists Maintenance and Repair, Non-Hardware Projects, Programme Initiation, Training Plan and Career Development under Unit V of CME338.

1. Maintenance and Repair

Value Engineering principles can be applied after a product, plant or system has entered service. The objective is to improve the value of maintenance and repair activities while preserving required performance, safety and reliability.

Areas for investigation

  • Identify the function that maintenance or repair must provide.
  • Separate essential functions from unnecessary or excessive requirements.
  • Study recurring failures and high maintenance-cost items.
  • Consider alternative materials, components, methods and maintenance intervals.
  • Reduce downtime, labour, spare-parts consumption and other relevant costs without compromising safety.

Example: If a machine repeatedly requires replacement of an expensive component, a VE study can investigate whether another component, maintenance method or design arrangement can perform the required function with lower life-cycle cost.

2. Non-Hardware Projects

VE is not limited to physical products. The same function-oriented thinking can be applied to services, administrative activities, procedures, information systems and other non-hardware projects.

Function-oriented questions

  • What function is required?
  • What result must be achieved?
  • What is the current cost of providing that function?
  • Are all existing steps necessary?
  • Can the same function be achieved by a simpler or more effective method?

For a non-hardware study, the “component” may be a process step, document, service activity, information flow or organisational task rather than a physical part.

Key point: VE focuses on required functions, so its principles can be transferred from hardware products to many service and process environments.

3. Programme Initiation

A VE programme should be deliberately initiated rather than treated as an isolated cost-cutting exercise. Management support, a suitable project and a capable study team are important for a successful programme.

Typical initiation sequence

  1. Recognise the opportunity: identify a product, process, service or project with meaningful value-improvement potential.
  2. Define the objective: state the performance, cost, quality, reliability, delivery or other requirements that must be protected.
  3. Select the study: prioritize projects where the expected benefit justifies the study effort.
  4. Form the team: include people with relevant technical, manufacturing, purchasing, quality, maintenance or user knowledge.
  5. Plan the study: establish scope, information needs, schedule and responsibilities.
  6. Conduct and implement: apply the VE job plan, develop alternatives, evaluate them and obtain the required approval for implementation.
  7. Review results: measure the realised benefits and capture lessons for future studies.

4. Training Plan

VE requires people who understand both the technical subject and the value-analysis process. A training plan develops these capabilities systematically.

Useful training areas

AreaPurpose
VE fundamentalsUnderstand value, function, cost and worth.
Function analysisExpress required functions clearly and analyse their relationships.
Creativity techniquesGenerate alternative ways of achieving required functions.
Evaluation methodsCompare alternatives using cost, performance, risk and other requirements.
FAST and VE toolsUse structured techniques during a VE study.
Case-study practiceApply the process to realistic engineering and organisational problems.
ImplementationDevelop, present and follow up feasible proposals.

Training should progress from concepts to guided application and then to practical VE studies. The exact programme can be adapted to the organisation and the experience of its personnel.

5. Career Development

Career development in Value Engineering involves progressively building technical, analytical, creative, communication and leadership skills.

Capabilities to develop

  • Technical knowledge: understand products, processes, materials, manufacturing and service systems relevant to the work.
  • Function-analysis skill: identify and communicate what a system must accomplish.
  • Creative problem solving: generate alternatives without becoming fixed on the existing design.
  • Economic evaluation: understand cost, worth, life-cycle cost and financial implications.
  • Team facilitation: encourage multidisciplinary participation and constructive discussion.
  • Communication: present proposals clearly to technical and management audiences.
  • Implementation ability: follow ideas through development, approval and measurement of results.

A developing VE professional should gain experience through training, case studies, multidisciplinary projects and progressively more responsible VE assignments.

6. Why VE is versatile

The central idea of VE is function analysis. Because functions can describe a physical product, a maintenance activity, a service or a process, the approach can be adapted to many situations.

ApplicationTypical VE focus
Product designAchieve required functions with better value.
ManufacturingImprove process functions and reduce avoidable cost.
MaintenanceProvide required availability and reliability efficiently.
ServicesDeliver the required service function effectively.
Administrative processesRemove unnecessary steps and improve information flow.

  3. Explain the steps involved in initiating a Value Engineering programme.
  4. Describe a suitable training plan for Value Engineering personnel.
  5. Explain the importance of career development in Value Engineering.
  6. Discuss why Value Engineering is considered a versatile approach.

2-Mark Quick Revision

  • Maintenance VE: applies function and value analysis to maintenance and repair activities.
  • Non-hardware VE: applies VE to services, processes and organisational activities, not only physical products.
  • Programme initiation: selecting and planning a VE study with defined objectives, scope and team.
  • VE training: develops knowledge of function analysis, creativity, evaluation and implementation.
  • Career development: progressive development of technical, analytical, creative, communication and leadership capabilities.
  • Core idea: VE can be adapted wherever required functions can be identified and evaluated.

Memory chain: Maintain → Extend to Services → Initiate → Train → Develop.
