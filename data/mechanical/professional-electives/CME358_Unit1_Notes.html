R-2021 • Industrial Layout Design and Safety

1 Mark3 Marks12 Marks

Syllabus: industrial facility layout definition and types; engineering-design approach; product analysis; equipment selection; personnel requirements; space; process/material flow; layout data and presentation tools.

1. Industrial Facility Layout

An industrial facility layout is the physical arrangement of departments, machines, workstations, storage areas, material-handling paths, utilities and support facilities within a plant.

The objective is to achieve an effective flow of people, materials and information while using space efficiently and maintaining safety, flexibility and productivity.

2. Types of Layout

TypeBasic ideaTypical application
Product/line layoutResources arranged according to operation sequence.High-volume repetitive production.
Process/functional layoutSimilar machines or functions grouped together.Varied products and job-shop production.
Fixed-position layoutProduct remains in one place; resources move to it.Large/immovable products.
Cellular layoutMachines grouped into cells for product families.Part-family/batch production.

3. Engineering Design Problem Approach

Facility-layout design can be treated as an engineering design problem: define requirements, collect data, generate alternatives, evaluate them and select an appropriate layout.

  1. Define objectives and constraints.
  2. Analyse products and processes.
  3. Determine equipment and personnel requirements.
  4. Estimate space requirements.
  5. Analyse material and process flow.
  6. Generate alternative layouts.
  7. Evaluate cost, flow, safety, flexibility and space utilisation.
  8. Select and implement the preferred arrangement.

4. Product Analysis

Product analysis examines what is produced, its dimensions/materials, production volume, product variety, operation sequence and handling requirements.

Important questions

  • What products or product families are produced?
  • What is the production volume?
  • What operations are required?
  • What is the routing sequence?
  • Which components require special handling?
  • How often does the product mix change?

5. Equipment Selection

Equipment must be selected according to the product, process, required capacity, quality, safety and economic constraints.

FactorWhy it matters
CapacityMust satisfy required production demand.
AccuracyMust meet product quality requirements.
FlexibilityImportant when product mix changes.
SpaceIncludes machine footprint and operating/service clearance.
SafetyGuarding, access and operating hazards must be considered.
CostPurchase, installation, operation and maintenance affect total cost.

6. Personnel Requirement Analysis

Layout planning must consider operators, supervisors, maintenance personnel, material handlers, quality staff and other support workers.

  • Number of workers.
  • Skill requirements.
  • Shift pattern.
  • Operator access and ergonomic needs.
  • Emergency exits and safe circulation.
  • Maintenance and inspection access.

7. Space Requirement

Required space is more than the machine footprint. It includes operating space, material storage, aisles, maintenance access, safety clearances, utilities and support activities.

Layout space idea: Machine/operation area + material/access area + maintenance/safety clearance + supporting space.

8. Process and Material Flow Analysis

Material flow analysis studies the movement of raw materials, work-in-process and finished products through the facility.

Good flow characteristics

  • Shorter travel distances.
  • Fewer backtracking movements.
  • Less congestion.
  • Reduced handling and waiting.
  • Clear and safe routes.
  • Easy supervision and control.
Simple flow: Receiving → Storage → Processing → Inspection → Assembly → Finished goods → Dispatch.

9. Data Required for Layout Decisions

  • Product and product-family information.
  • Production volume and variety.
  • Routing/process sequence.
  • Machine/equipment list and dimensions.
  • Material movement quantities.
  • Personnel and shift information.
  • Space availability and building constraints.
  • Utilities and service requirements.
  • Safety and statutory constraints.

10. Tools for Presenting Layout Designs

Layout alternatives can be communicated using block layouts, flow diagrams, relationship charts, scaled drawings, CAD layouts and simulation models.

A good presentation should make department relationships, movement paths, space usage and major constraints easy to understand.
