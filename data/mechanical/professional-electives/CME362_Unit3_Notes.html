1. Stoichiometry of Combustion

Stoichiometry determines the theoretical amount of oxygen or air required for complete combustion of a fuel.

For a general hydrocarbon

For fuel CxHy, complete combustion requires:

CₓHᵧ + (x + y/4)O₂ → xCO₂ + (y/2)H₂O

Theoretical air is obtained from the theoretical oxygen requirement using the oxygen fraction of air. In an actual furnace, excess air is generally supplied to promote complete combustion.

Why excess air matters

  • Too little air → incomplete combustion and CO/unburnt fuel.
  • Too much air → larger quantity of hot gases and increased stack heat loss.
  • Correct excess air → better combustion with lower avoidable losses.

2. Combustion Principles

Combustion is a chemical reaction between fuel and oxidant accompanied by heat release. Industrial combustion efficiency depends on fuel quality, air supply, mixing, temperature and residence time.

Important losses

  • Heat carried away by dry flue gas.
  • Heat associated with moisture in products.
  • Incomplete combustion.
  • Radiation and convection losses from equipment surfaces.
  • Unaccounted or miscellaneous losses.
Combustion energy flow
Fuel + Air → Combustion chamber → Useful heat + Flue-gas heat + Other losses

3. Boilers

A boiler converts the chemical energy of fuel into heat and transfers it to water to produce steam.

Main energy-conservation measures

  • Maintain suitable excess air through combustion control.
  • Keep heating surfaces clean.
  • Recover heat from flue gas using suitable devices.
  • Control blowdown to the required level.
  • Return good-quality condensate wherever practical.
  • Maintain insulation and prevent steam leakage.
  • Monitor steam generation, fuel consumption and flue-gas temperature.
Simple boiler energy flow
Fuel → Burner → Boiler furnace → Heat transfer → Steam
                             ↓
                         Flue gas → Heat recovery → Stack
Boiler efficiency = useful heat gained by water/steam ÷ heat supplied by fuel × 100

4. Steam Distribution Systems

Steam distribution carries steam from the boiler to process users. Energy is lost through leakage, condensation, poor insulation, incorrect pressure and malfunctioning steam traps.

Conservation measures

  • Repair steam leaks quickly.
  • Insulate steam lines, valves and fittings.
  • Maintain steam traps.
  • Recover condensate where practical.
  • Avoid unnecessarily high distribution pressure.
  • Provide proper drainage and remove condensate from steam lines.

5. Furnaces

Industrial furnaces supply controlled heat for heating, melting, forging, heat treatment and other processes.

Energy-saving methods

  • Optimize the air-fuel ratio.
  • Prevent air infiltration through openings.
  • Maintain burners properly.
  • Use appropriate insulation and refractory lining.
  • Recover heat from exhaust gases.
  • Optimize furnace loading and operating temperature.
Furnace energy flow
Fuel + Air → Burner → Furnace → Heated product
                             ↓
                         Hot exhaust → Heat recovery

6. Thermic Fluid Heaters

A thermic fluid heater heats a circulating heat-transfer fluid, which then supplies heat to process equipment without directly using steam.

Conservation measures

  • Maintain clean heat-transfer surfaces.
  • Optimize combustion and excess air.
  • Prevent fluid leakage.
  • Maintain insulation.
  • Recover useful heat from exhaust gases when feasible.
  • Monitor inlet/outlet fluid temperatures and fuel consumption.

7. Cooling Towers

A cooling tower rejects heat from warm circulating water to the atmosphere, mainly through evaporation and sensible heat transfer.

Energy-conservation measures

  • Maintain correct water flow and air flow.
  • Keep fill material clean.
  • Maintain fans and motors efficiently.
  • Control fan operation according to cooling demand.
  • Reduce unnecessary blowdown while maintaining water quality.
  • Prevent nozzle blockage and uneven water distribution.
Cooling tower flow
Warm water ↓
         [ FILL ] ← Air
             ↓
        Cool water → Process
             ↑
       Warm moist air ↑

8. D.G. Sets

A diesel generator set converts the chemical energy of diesel fuel into electrical energy through an engine coupled to an alternator.

Energy-saving measures

  • Operate near an efficient loading range rather than excessive light loading.
  • Maintain the engine, air filter, injectors and cooling system.
  • Use good-quality fuel and correct lubrication.
  • Monitor fuel consumption and electrical output.
  • Minimize unnecessary running time.
  • Maintain the alternator and electrical connections.
Specific fuel consumption = Fuel consumed / Electrical energy generated

9. Insulation and Refractories

Insulation reduces heat transfer between a hot or cold surface and its surroundings. Refractories are heat-resistant materials used to line furnaces, kilns and other high-temperature equipment.

Good practice

  • Use suitable insulation thickness and material.
  • Repair damaged insulation.
  • Prevent moisture ingress where it degrades insulation performance.
  • Select refractory material according to temperature and chemical environment.
  • Inspect refractory linings for cracks, erosion and spalling.

10. Waste Heat Recovery Devices

Waste heat recovery captures useful heat that would otherwise leave with hot exhaust gases or other waste streams.

Common devices

  • Economizers
  • Air preheaters
  • Waste heat boilers
  • Recuperators
  • Regenerators
Waste heat recovery
Hot exhaust → Heat exchanger → Recovered heat → Feed water / combustion air / process
                             ↓
                           Cooler exhaust

Selection depends on: temperature level, heat-source characteristics, process demand, fouling tendency, pressure drop, cost and maintenance requirements.
