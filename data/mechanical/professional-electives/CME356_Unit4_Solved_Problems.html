Problem 1 — Centrifugal Force on a Blade

Given: blade mass = 0.8 kg; mean radius = 0.25 m; rotational speed = 12,000 rpm.

Find: centrifugal force.
Step 1 — Convert speed:
ω = 2πN/60
ω = 2π(12,000)/60
ω = 1256.64 rad/s
Step 2 — Formula:
Fc = mω²r
Step 3 — Substitute:
Fc = 0.8 × (1256.64)² × 0.25
Step 4 — Calculate:
Fc ≈ 316,827 N
Final answer: Fc ≈ 316.8 kN.

This demonstrates why centrifugal loading is a dominant consideration in high-speed blade and disc design.

Problem 2 — Aerodynamic Bending Stress

Given: aerodynamic force = 2.5 kN, moment arm = 80 mm, section modulus = 1.8 × 10−6 m³.

Step 1 — Bending moment:
M = F × l
M = 2500 × 0.08
M = 200 N·m
Step 2 — Bending stress:
σb = M/Z
Step 3 — Substitute:
σb = 200/(1.8 × 10−6)
Step 4 — Calculate:
σb = 111,111,111 Pa
Final answer: σb ≈ 111.1 MPa.

Problem 3 — Simple Restrained Thermal Stress

Given: Young's modulus E = 200 GPa, coefficient of thermal expansion α = 12 × 10−6/°C, temperature rise ΔT = 150°C.

Step 1 — Idealised relation:
σth = EαΔT
Step 2 — Substitute:
σth = 200 × 109 × 12 × 10−6 × 150
Step 3 — Arithmetic:
12 × 10−6 × 150 = 0.0018
σth = 200 × 109 × 0.0018
σth = 360 × 106 Pa
Final answer: idealised thermal stress = 360 MPa.

Assumption: complete elastic restraint. Real turbine blades have non-uniform temperature fields and partial restraints, so detailed thermo-mechanical analysis may be required.

Problem 4 — Combined Blade Stress

Given: centrifugal tensile stress = 180 MPa; bending stress = 70 MPa; thermal tensile stress = 30 MPa; vibratory stress = 20 MPa. At the selected location all four are tensile and additive for this simplified practice check. Allowable combined stress = 320 MPa.

Step 1 — Combine stresses:
σcombined = σc + σb + σth + σv
Step 2 — Substitute:
σcombined = 180 + 70 + 30 + 20
Step 3 — Arithmetic:
σcombined = 300 MPa
Step 4 — Compare:
300 MPa < 320 MPa
Final answer: simplified combined-stress check is SAFE, with 20 MPa remaining below the stated allowable stress.

In real design, the actual multiaxial stress state, temperature dependence, fatigue/creep criteria and load directions must be considered.

Problem 5 — Blade Design Workflow Question

Question: Explain how a preliminary blade-design calculation can be organised.

1. Define: speed, temperature, material, blade geometry and service condition.
2. Determine mass distribution: estimate blade mass and radial distribution.
3. Calculate centrifugal loading: evaluate the internal tensile load generated by rotation.
4. Determine aerodynamic loading: estimate force distribution and bending moment.
5. Calculate section stresses: evaluate centrifugal, bending and thermal contributions at critical sections.
6. Check attachments: blade-root contact, local stress, clearances and retention.
7. Check durability: fatigue, creep, vibration and thermal effects.
8. Refine: use detailed analysis where simplified assumptions are inadequate.

Quick Formula Revision

Centrifugal: Fc = mω²r
Angular speed: ω = 2πN/60
Bending: σb = M/Z
Simple fully restrained thermal stress: σth = EαΔT
Combined practice check: σcombined = σcentrifugal ± σbending ± σthermal ± σvibratory

Exam Writing Method

For a numerical: Given → Find → Formula → unit conversion → substitute actual values → arithmetic → intermediate result → next formula → substitute again → final answer with unit → design check/conclusion.
