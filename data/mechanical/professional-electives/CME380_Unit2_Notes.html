1. Electronically Controlled Gasoline Injection System

An electronic gasoline injection system supplies the required quantity of petrol using electronically controlled injectors. The main sections are fuel delivery, air induction and electronic control.

Working

  1. Sensors measure engine operating conditions such as air flow/load, temperature and engine speed.
  2. The ECU processes the sensor signals.
  3. The ECU decides injector opening duration.
  4. The injector sprays a controlled quantity of fuel.
  5. The mixture enters the engine and is burned for power production.
12/13-mark focus: Draw the block diagram and explain fuel delivery, air induction, sensors/ECU and injector operation in sequence.

2. Electronically Controlled Diesel Injection

The syllabus specifies three important arrangements: unit injector system, rotary distributor type and common rail direct injection (CRDI).

Unit Injector System

The pump and injector are integrated into one unit for each cylinder. High-pressure fuel is generated close to the injector and then injected into the cylinder at the required time.

Rotary Distributor Type

A single injection pump distributes fuel to the individual cylinders in the required firing sequence.

Common Rail Direct Injection

In CRDI, fuel is maintained at high pressure in a common rail and supplied to individual electronically controlled injectors. Injection timing and quantity can therefore be controlled according to engine conditions.

3. Electronic Ignition Systems

Electronic ignition replaces mechanical contact switching with electronic triggering and switching. Two systems named in the syllabus are transistorized coil ignition (TCI) and capacitive discharge ignition (CDI).

Transistorized Coil Ignition

A sensor/trigger signal controls a transistor. The transistor switches primary current through the ignition coil. When primary current is interrupted, the collapsing magnetic field produces high voltage in the secondary winding and the spark plug produces the spark.

Capacitive Discharge Ignition

An electrical capacitor is charged and then rapidly discharged through the ignition coil. The rapid discharge produces a high-voltage pulse for the spark plug.

4. Turbochargers — WGT and VGT

A turbocharger uses exhaust-gas energy to drive a turbine connected to a compressor. The compressor supplies denser air to the engine, allowing greater air mass to enter the cylinders.

Wastegate Turbocharger (WGT)

A wastegate controls boost by bypassing some exhaust gas around the turbine when the required boost level is reached.

Variable Geometry Turbocharger (VGT)

VGT uses adjustable vanes around the turbine to vary the effective gas-flow area. This helps maintain useful turbine response over a wider operating range.

5. Three-Way Catalytic Converter

A three-way catalytic converter is an exhaust after-treatment device used to reduce major gaseous pollutants from a gasoline engine. The three main reactions are oxidation of CO, oxidation of unburned hydrocarbons (HC), and reduction of NOx.

  • CO is oxidized to CO₂.
  • HC is oxidized mainly to CO₂ and H₂O.
  • NOx is reduced mainly to nitrogen.

6. Emission Norms — Euro and BS

Emission norms set limits on pollutants emitted by vehicles. The syllabus specifically requires Euro and BS (Bharat Stage) norms.

For exam writing, explain the purpose of emission norms, identify the major regulated pollutants and describe how emission-control technologies such as electronic fuel control and catalytic converters help engines meet tighter limits.

Do not invent numerical limits in the exam unless your prescribed class material provides the exact values. Limits vary with vehicle category, fuel, test procedure and regulatory stage.
