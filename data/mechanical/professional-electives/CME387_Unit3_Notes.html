1. Electric Discharge Machining (EDM)

Principle: EDM removes electrically conductive material by controlled electrical sparks between a tool electrode and workpiece separated by a small dielectric-filled gap. Each spark produces intense local heating and a tiny molten/vaporized zone; flushing removes debris.

Exam diagram: clearly label tool, workpiece, dielectric, spark gap and power supply.

Equipment

  1. DC pulse power supply
  2. Tool electrode and workpiece
  3. Dielectric tank
  4. Servo feed/control system
  5. Flushing and filtration system
  6. Machine table and work holding system

Working

  1. The workpiece and tool are immersed in dielectric or supplied with dielectric at the machining zone.
  2. The servo system maintains a small controlled gap.
  3. A voltage pulse is applied across the gap.
  4. The dielectric breaks down locally and a spark is produced.
  5. Local heat melts/vaporizes a small amount of material.
  6. The pulse stops; the dielectric recovers its insulating condition.
  7. Flushing removes debris and the cycle repeats.

Important process parameters

  • Peak current
  • Pulse-on time
  • Pulse-off time
  • Applied voltage
  • Duty factor
  • Dielectric condition and flushing
  • Tool-work gap
  • Electrode material and polarity
Exam point: Increasing discharge energy generally increases material removal but can also increase surface roughness, crater size and heat-affected effects. The exact optimum depends on the required machining condition.

Advantages

  • Can machine very hard electrically conductive materials.
  • No conventional cutting force.
  • Complex cavities and intricate profiles can be produced.
  • Useful for dies, moulds and precision features.

Limitations

  • Workpiece must be electrically conductive.
  • Material removal can be relatively slow.
  • Tool wear occurs.
  • Recast/heat-affected surface may require finishing.

Applications

Die sinking, mould cavities, precision holes, tool and die manufacture and difficult-to-machine conductive materials.

2. Wire Electric Discharge Machining (WEDM)

WEDM uses a continuously moving fine wire electrode as the tool. Electrical discharges occur between the wire and conductive workpiece while dielectric, commonly deionized water in modern machines, is supplied through the cutting zone.

Standard WEDM sketch: moving wire, workpiece, dielectric jets and wire drive.

Important parameters

  • Wire tension
  • Pulse duration and frequency
  • Wire speed
  • Dielectric flow rate and conductivity
  • Gap and servo control
  • Workpiece thickness

Advantages

  • Excellent for complex 2-D profiles and narrow slots.
  • Small cutting forces.
  • Hard conductive materials can be cut.
  • No shaped solid electrode is required.

Limitations

  • Conductive workpiece required.
  • Cutting speed and surface integrity depend strongly on settings.
  • Very thick or unstable workpieces require careful parameter selection.

3. Laser Beam Machining (LBM)

LBM uses a focused high-energy laser beam to heat, melt and/or vaporize material in a very small region. It is a non-contact thermal process.

Important characteristics of laser

  • High energy density
  • Strong directionality
  • Good focusing capability
  • Non-contact machining
  • Suitable for small holes and fine features

Process parameters

  • Laser power/energy
  • Pulse duration and frequency for pulsed operation
  • Spot size
  • Focal position and focal length
  • Beam travel speed
  • Workpiece material and thickness

Advantages

  • No tool wear in the conventional sense.
  • Very small machining zone is possible.
  • Can machine difficult shapes and small holes.
  • Suitable for automation.

Limitations

  • Equipment can be costly.
  • Thermal damage/HAZ may occur.
  • Reflective materials can be challenging depending on wavelength and setup.

Applications

Micro-drilling, cutting thin materials, trimming, marking and selected aerospace/electronic manufacturing operations.

4. Plasma Arc Machining (PAM)

PAM uses a high-temperature, high-velocity plasma jet to melt and remove material. The process is mainly used for thermal cutting of electrically conductive materials.

Principle and working

An arc ionizes the supplied gas and produces plasma. The concentrated plasma jet transfers intense thermal energy to the workpiece, melting the material; the high-velocity jet expels the molten metal from the cut.

Important parameters

  • Arc current
  • Plasma gas type and flow
  • Nozzle geometry
  • Stand-off distance
  • Travel speed
  • Material thickness

Advantages

  • High cutting speed.
  • Useful for relatively thick conductive plates.
  • Can cut many electrically conductive metals.

Limitations

  • Thermal effects and HAZ.
  • Noise, fumes and intense radiation require controls.
  • Cut quality depends on current, gas and travel speed.

5. Electron Beam Machining (EBM)

EBM removes material using a focused high-velocity electron beam. The beam converts kinetic energy into intense heat at the work surface. A high vacuum is used so electrons can travel without significant scattering by air molecules.

Standard answer diagram: electron gun, accelerating system, magnetic lens, vacuum chamber and workpiece.

Equipment

  1. Electron gun
  2. High-voltage accelerating system
  3. Electromagnetic focusing lens
  4. Deflection/control system
  5. Vacuum chamber and pumps
  6. Work table

Working

  1. Electrons are emitted from the heated cathode.
  2. They are accelerated by high voltage.
  3. Magnetic/electromagnetic lenses focus the beam.
  4. The focused beam strikes the workpiece in vacuum.
  5. Its kinetic energy becomes heat and removes a small volume of material.
Why vacuum? It reduces collisions and scattering of electrons by gas molecules, allowing a concentrated beam to reach the workpiece efficiently.

Important parameters

  • Accelerating voltage
  • Beam current
  • Focal spot size
  • Pulse duration
  • Focus position
  • Beam scanning/deflection

Advantages

  • Very high power density.
  • Fine machining capability.
  • Small heat-affected region can be obtained with suitable conditions.
  • Useful for micro-holes and precision work.

Limitations

  • Vacuum equipment is required.
  • High capital and operating cost.
  • Workpiece size can be limited by the vacuum chamber.
  • Process control requires skilled operation.

6. Ion Beam Machining (IBM)

Ion Beam Machining uses accelerated ions directed onto the work surface. Material is removed mainly by momentum transfer and sputtering from the surface.

Basic working

  1. Generate ions from a suitable source.
  2. Accelerate the ions using an electric field.
  3. Focus or direct the ion beam toward the workpiece.
  4. Ion impacts eject atoms from the surface.
  5. Controlled scanning produces the required micro-feature.

Important parameters

  • Ion energy
  • Beam current
  • Beam diameter
  • Incidence angle
  • Exposure time

General trend: Increasing ion energy or beam current can increase material removal, but excessive energy can affect surface damage and dimensional control. The selected values depend on the required accuracy and material.

Applications

Micro-machining, precision surface modification and very small-feature manufacturing.

How to write a 12/13-mark process answer

  1. Start with a one- or two-line definition/principle.
  2. Draw the standard labelled diagram.
  3. Explain construction/equipment.
  4. Explain working step-by-step.
  5. List the important process parameters and their effects.
  6. Write advantages and limitations.
  7. Finish with applications.

Exam tip: A clean diagram plus a clear working sequence makes the answer easy to evaluate. Do not fill the answer with unrelated theory.
