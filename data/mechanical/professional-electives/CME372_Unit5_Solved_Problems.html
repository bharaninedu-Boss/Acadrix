Problem 1 — Nonlinear Load Increments

Practice • Calculation

Given: A nonlinear analysis uses a total applied load of 100 kN divided into 5 equal load increments.

Find: Load applied in each increment.

Formula: Load per increment = Total load / Number of increments
Substitute: Load per increment = 100 / 5
Arithmetic: Load per increment = 20 kN
Final answer: Each load increment = 20 kN.

Problem 2 — Mesh-Convergence Percentage Change

Practice • Calculation

Given: Maximum stress from the first mesh is 200 MPa. After mesh refinement it is 194 MPa.

Find: Percentage change in maximum stress.

Formula: Percentage change = |New − Old| / Old × 100
Substitute: Percentage change = |194 − 200| / 200 × 100
Arithmetic: = 6 / 200 × 100 = 3%
Final answer: Stress changed by 3% after refinement.

Interpretation: A small change indicates improved mesh convergence, but an acceptable limit must be specified for the particular analysis.

Problem 3 — Elastic Strain from Hooke's Law

Practice • Calculation

Given: A uniaxial elastic region has Young's modulus E = 200 GPa and stress σ = 100 MPa.

Find: Elastic strain.

Formula: ε = σ / E
Unit conversion: E = 200 GPa = 200,000 MPa
Substitute: ε = 100 / 200000
Arithmetic: ε = 0.0005
Final answer: Elastic strain = 5 × 10−4.

Problem 4 — Stress Stiffening: Percentage Increase in Stiffness

Practice • Calculation

Given: A simplified model has an initial transverse stiffness of 10 kN/mm. After applying prestress, the effective stiffness is 12 kN/mm.

Find: Percentage increase in stiffness.

Formula: Increase = New stiffness − Initial stiffness
Substitute: Increase = 12 − 10 = 2 kN/mm
Percentage increase: = 2 / 10 × 100 = 20%
Final answer: Effective stiffness increased by 20%.

Note: This is an illustrative calculation to explain the concept of stress stiffening; it is not a universal formula for every prestressed structure.

Problem 5 — Contact Gap Interpretation

Practice • Conceptual calculation

Given: Two bodies initially have a 0.20 mm gap. During loading, their relative approach is 0.12 mm.

Find: Whether the surfaces have closed the initial gap.

Given gap: 0.20 mm
Relative approach: 0.12 mm
Remaining gap: 0.20 − 0.12 = 0.08 mm
Final answer: The gap remains 0.08 mm; therefore the surfaces have not yet established contact based on this simplified displacement condition.

Problem 6 — Mesh Aspect Ratio

Practice • Calculation

Given: A simplified rectangular element has a longest characteristic dimension of 8 mm and a shortest characteristic dimension of 2 mm.

Find: Aspect ratio.

Formula: Aspect ratio = Longest dimension / Shortest dimension
Substitute: Aspect ratio = 8 / 2
Arithmetic: = 4
Final answer: Aspect ratio = 4.

Interpretation: Whether this is acceptable depends on the element type, analysis and solver guidance; aspect ratio should not be judged in isolation.

Problem 7 — Nonlinear Analysis: 12-Mark Answer Framework

12M • Theory practice

Question: Explain nonlinear finite-element analysis and discuss material nonlinearity, geometric nonlinearity and contact nonlinearity.

Step 1 — Introduction: Define nonlinear analysis and state why a linear load-response assumption may become invalid.

Step 2 — Material nonlinearity: Explain yielding, plastic deformation, hardening and time/rate-dependent viscoplastic behaviour.

Step 3 — Geometric nonlinearity: Explain large displacement/rotation and the effect of changed configuration on equilibrium and stiffness.

Step 4 — Contact nonlinearity: Explain open gap, contact and sliding conditions and why the active contact state can change.

Step 5 — Solution procedure: Divide the load into increments, iterate to equilibrium, check residual/convergence, then continue to the next increment.

Step 6 — Modelling: Use suitable material data, element type, mesh, contacts, constraints and convergence criteria.

Step 7 — Conclusion: State that reliable nonlinear CAE results depend on physically correct modelling and convergence verification.
