CME372 — Unit V: Non-linear Analysis

R-2021 • CAD and CAE • Exam-oriented notes

Syllabus: nonlinear problems and solution techniques; material nonlinearity, plasticity and viscoplasticity; stress stiffening; contact interfaces and gaps; geometric nonlinearity; modelling considerations; free/mapped meshing; mesh quality; error estimate; analysis software.

1. Nonlinear Analysis

A nonlinear analysis is required when the response is not proportional to the applied load or when stiffness changes during deformation. Nonlinearity may arise from material behaviour, geometry or changing contact conditions.

Major sources

  • Material nonlinearity: constitutive behaviour changes with stress, strain, temperature or history.
  • Geometric nonlinearity: large displacement or rotation changes the structural equilibrium and stiffness.
  • Contact nonlinearity: surfaces may separate, touch or slide during loading.

2. Solution Techniques

Nonlinear finite-element equations are normally solved incrementally. The load is divided into increments and an iterative procedure seeks equilibrium at each increment.

Conceptual procedure: apply increment → predict response → form residual → update solution/stiffness → iterate until convergence → proceed to next increment.

Newton-Raphson-type procedures are commonly used for equilibrium iterations.

3. Plasticity

Plasticity describes permanent deformation after the material reaches its yield condition. Unlike elastic deformation, plastic strain is not fully recovered when the load is removed.

  • Yield criterion defines onset of plastic flow.
  • Plastic strain develops after yielding.
  • Hardening rules describe how material strength evolves.
  • Material data must be appropriate for the analysis temperature and loading.

4. Viscoplasticity

Viscoplasticity combines time-dependent effects with permanent inelastic deformation. It is important for materials and loading conditions where strain rate, temperature and time affect the response.

5. Stress Stiffening

Stress stiffening refers to the change in structural stiffness caused by an existing stress state. For example, tensile pre-stress can increase resistance to certain transverse deformations.

6. Contact Interfaces and Gaps

Contact problems are nonlinear because the active contact condition can change during loading.

ConditionMeaning
Open gapSurfaces are separated and transmit no compressive contact force through that interface.
ContactSurfaces interact and transmit appropriate normal forces.
SlidingTangential relative motion occurs while contact is maintained.

7. Geometric Nonlinearity

When deformation becomes large enough to alter the geometry significantly, the equilibrium equations must account for the changed configuration. Large rotations, large displacements and buckling-type problems can require geometric nonlinear analysis.

8. Modelling Considerations

  • Select realistic material data.
  • Use appropriate element type and mesh density.
  • Represent contacts and constraints correctly.
  • Apply loads and boundary conditions gradually where required.
  • Use suitable convergence criteria.
  • Check whether the numerical result is physically reasonable.

9. Free and Mapped Meshing

Free meshing allows the mesher to generate elements without requiring a regular structured pattern and is useful for complex geometry.

Mapped meshing produces a more structured element arrangement in regions whose geometry supports a mapping scheme. It can give better control over element orientation and quality.

10. Mesh Quality

Mesh quality affects numerical accuracy and convergence. Common checks include element distortion, aspect ratio, skewness, Jacobian-related measures and smoothness of element transitions, depending on the solver.

11. Error Estimate

An error estimate helps determine whether the numerical solution is sufficiently accurate. Comparing solutions after mesh refinement is a practical way to assess mesh sensitivity.

Mesh-convergence idea: solve → refine mesh → solve again → compare important results → continue refinement until changes are acceptably small.

12. Nonlinear Analysis Software

Engineering CAE packages provide nonlinear material models, contact formulations, nonlinear geometry options, meshing tools, solvers and post-processing. The software must be used with physically correct assumptions and verified input data.
