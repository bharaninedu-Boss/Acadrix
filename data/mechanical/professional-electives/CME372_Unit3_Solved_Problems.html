Problem 1 — Depth-Buffer Comparison

Given: A pixel currently stores depth z = 0.70. A new surface produces z = 0.45. Use the convention that the smaller positive z is nearer to the viewer.
Find: which surface is displayed.
Step 1 — Compare: 0.45 < 0.70.
Step 2 — Decision: The new surface is nearer, so its colour/intensity replaces the stored pixel value and the depth buffer is updated to 0.45.
Final: New surface is visible at this pixel; stored depth = 0.45.

Problem 2 — Depth-Buffer Multiple Surfaces

Given: Three candidate surfaces at one pixel have depths 0.82, 0.37 and 0.54, with smaller depth meaning nearer.
Find: visible surface.
Comparison: min(0.82, 0.37, 0.54) = 0.37.
Final: The surface at depth 0.37 is selected.

Problem 3 — Scan-Line Workload Concept

Given: An image contains 800 scan lines. A simplified algorithm examines 1,000 candidate spans per line. An improved coherence-based process avoids 25% of these candidate checks.
Find: approximate checks avoided and remaining.
Step 1: Original checks = 800 × 1,000 = 800,000.
Step 2: Avoided = 25% × 800,000 = 200,000.
Step 3: Remaining = 800,000 − 200,000 = 600,000.
Final: Approximately 200,000 checks are avoided; 600,000 remain in this simplified model.

Problem 4 — Warnock Subdivision

Given: A viewing region is initially 256×256 pixels. A simplified recursive subdivision halves each dimension whenever visibility cannot be resolved. Assume three subdivisions are required.
Find: final region size.
After subdivision 1: 256/2 × 256/2 = 128×128.
After subdivision 2: 128/2 × 128/2 = 64×64.
After subdivision 3: 64/2 × 64/2 = 32×32.
Final: Each resulting subregion is 32×32 pixels in this simplified model.

Problem 5 — Depth-Buffer Memory

Given: A display has 1920×1080 pixels and uses a 24-bit depth value per pixel.
Find: approximate raw depth-buffer storage in bytes and MiB, ignoring overhead.
Step 1 — Pixels: 1920×1080 = 2,073,600 pixels.
Step 2 — Bits: 2,073,600 × 24 = 49,766,400 bits.
Step 3 — Bytes: 49,766,400/8 = 6,220,800 bytes.
Step 4 — MiB: 6,220,800/1,048,576 ≈ 5.93 MiB.
Final: approximately 6.22 MB decimal, or 5.93 MiB.

Problem 6 — CAD Data-Exchange Selection

Question: A team needs a standardized format for exchanging product data between CAD systems and across the product life cycle. Which syllabus standard is the strongest match?
Reasoning: The Unit III notes describe IGES as CAD product-definition exchange and STEP as standardized product-data representation/exchange across the product life cycle.
Final: STEP is the stronger match when product-life-cycle-wide data exchange is the stated requirement.
