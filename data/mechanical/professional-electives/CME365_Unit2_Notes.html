1. Solar Energy — Basic Idea

Solar energy is the radiant energy received from the Sun. It can be used directly as heat through solar-thermal systems or converted into electricity through photovoltaic (PV) devices.

Exam point: Solar-thermal conversion uses solar radiation mainly to produce useful heat. Photovoltaic conversion produces electricity directly from incident light.

2. Solar Radiation

Solar radiation reaching an installation is affected by the Sun's position, time, location, atmospheric conditions and surface orientation. The radiation incident on a surface can be considered in terms of direct (beam), diffuse and reflected components.

  • Beam/direct radiation: radiation arriving from the Sun without significant scattering in the atmosphere.
  • Diffuse radiation: radiation scattered by atmospheric constituents and arriving from the sky.
  • Reflected radiation: radiation reflected from surrounding surfaces onto the receiving surface.
  • Global radiation: total radiation incident on a surface, commonly considered as beam plus diffuse components on a horizontal surface.
For a horizontal surface: Global radiation = Beam component + Diffuse component

3. Solar Spectrum

The solar spectrum contains electromagnetic radiation over a range of wavelengths. Solar-energy devices interact differently with different wavelength regions. The atmosphere absorbs and scatters selected portions of incoming radiation, so the spectrum at the Earth's surface differs from the spectrum outside the atmosphere.

RegionGeneral significance
UltravioletShort-wavelength radiation; a relatively small portion of solar energy.
VisibleImportant portion of sunlight perceived by the human eye and used by photovoltaic materials.
InfraredLonger-wavelength radiation strongly associated with thermal effects.

4. Measurement of Solar Radiation and Sunshine

4.1 Pyranometer

A pyranometer measures global solar irradiance on a surface. It can be used for solar-resource assessment and performance evaluation of solar collectors and PV systems.

4.2 Pyrheliometer

A pyrheliometer measures direct or beam solar radiation from the Sun, normally using a narrow field of view aligned toward the Sun.

4.3 Sunshine Recorder

A sunshine recorder determines the duration of bright sunshine over a specified period. Sunshine duration is useful in solar-resource assessment and energy-yield estimation.

Remember: Pyranometer → global radiation. Pyrheliometer → direct/beam radiation. Sunshine recorder → duration of bright sunshine.

5. Solar Geometry — Why Orientation Matters

The amount of solar radiation received by a surface depends on the relative position of the Sun and the receiving surface. Solar collectors and PV modules are therefore installed with suitable orientation and tilt to improve annual or seasonal energy capture.

Sun →→→ solar rays →→→ [ tilted collector / PV module ] ↗ useful incident radiation / surface normal

6. Solar Thermal Collectors

A solar collector receives solar radiation and transfers useful energy to a working fluid. Solar thermal collectors are broadly classified as non-concentrating and concentrating collectors.

6.1 Flat-Plate Collector

A flat-plate collector is a non-concentrating collector. A typical arrangement contains a transparent cover, absorber plate, fluid passages, insulation and casing.

Solar radiation ↓↓↓ ┌──────────────────────────┐ │ Transparent cover │ ├──────────────────────────┤ │ Absorber plate │ ← absorbs radiation ├──────────────────────────┤ │ Fluid passages │ → useful heated fluid ├──────────────────────────┤ │ Thermal insulation │ └──────────────────────────┘

Working: radiation passes through the cover and is absorbed by the absorber plate. Heat is transferred to the working fluid flowing through passages. Insulation reduces heat loss from the rear and sides.

6.2 Concentrating Collector

A concentrating collector uses reflective or refractive elements to direct solar radiation toward a smaller receiver area. It is useful when higher operating temperatures are required.

Flat-plate collectorConcentrating collector
Non-concentrating.Concentrates solar radiation on a receiver.
Can use direct and diffuse radiation.Primarily depends on direct/beam radiation.
Suitable for low-to-moderate temperature applications.Can achieve higher temperatures.
Generally simpler construction.Often requires accurate tracking and optical components.

7. Solar Thermal Applications

  • Water heating
  • Space heating
  • Drying of agricultural products
  • Solar cooking
  • Industrial process heat
  • Solar desalination
  • Thermal power generation using concentrating systems

8. Thermal Energy Storage

Solar radiation varies with time and is unavailable at night. Thermal energy storage allows heat collected during periods of solar availability to be used later.

Main storage approaches

  • Sensible heat storage: stores energy by raising the temperature of a material.
  • Latent heat storage: stores/release energy through a phase change.
  • Thermochemical storage: stores energy through reversible chemical reactions.
Sensible heat storage: Q = mcΔT

where m is storage-material mass, c is specific heat capacity and ΔT is temperature rise.

9. Photovoltaic Conversion

Photovoltaic conversion is the direct conversion of solar radiation into electrical energy using semiconductor devices. A PV cell uses the photovoltaic effect: incident photons transfer energy to the semiconductor and contribute to the generation and separation of charge carriers.

Sunlight ↓ ┌──────────────────────┐ │ PV cell │ │ semiconductor │ └──────────────────────┘ ↓ DC electrical power

10. Solar Cell

A solar cell is the basic electrical unit of a photovoltaic system. It is a semiconductor device designed to generate electrical power when illuminated.

Important cell terms

  • Short-circuit current (ISC): current when the cell terminals are shorted.
  • Open-circuit voltage (VOC): terminal voltage when the external current is zero.
  • Maximum-power point: operating point at which electrical output power is maximum.
  • Fill factor: indicates the squareness/quality of the cell I–V characteristic and is used with VOC and ISC in assessing cell performance.
Electrical output power: P = VI
Fill factor: FF = VmpImpVOCISC

Here Vmp and Imp are voltage and current at the maximum-power point.

11. PV Modules, Arrays and Systems

A single cell provides limited voltage and power. Cells are interconnected to form modules, and modules are interconnected to form arrays to obtain the required electrical output.

LevelMeaning
CellBasic photovoltaic semiconductor device.
ModuleInterconnected PV cells packaged as a practical unit.
ArrayCombination of PV modules connected to provide the required voltage/current and power.
PV systemPV array plus the electrical and control components needed for the intended application.

12. PV System Configurations

Grid-connected PV

The PV system operates in coordination with the utility grid. Depending on system design, power can be supplied to local loads and excess generation can be exported to the grid under applicable rules.

Standalone PV

A standalone system is not dependent on the utility grid. Energy storage is commonly used when loads must be supplied when solar generation is unavailable.

Hybrid PV

A hybrid system combines PV with another energy source and/or storage technology to improve supply flexibility and reliability.

13. Factors Affecting PV Performance

  • Solar irradiance
  • Cell/module temperature
  • Shading
  • Orientation and tilt
  • Dust and soiling
  • Electrical mismatch and interconnection losses
  • Power-conditioning losses
  • Ageing and environmental exposure
