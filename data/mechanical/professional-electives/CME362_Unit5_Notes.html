Energy Monitoring, Targeting, Labelling and Economics

Simple exam-focused notes with important 12/13-mark questions and standard diagrams.

1. Monitoring and Targeting (M&T)

Monitoring means regularly measuring and recording energy use. Targeting means setting a desired energy-consumption level and comparing actual performance with it.

Basic elements

  1. Measure energy consumption.
  2. Record production or activity data.
  3. Establish a suitable baseline.
  4. Relate energy use to production or operating conditions.
  5. Set a target.
  6. Compare actual and target performance.
  7. Investigate deviations and take corrective action.
ENERGY METERS → DATA COLLECTION → ANALYSIS → BASELINE → TARGET → COMPARISON → ACTION → REVIEW

2. CUSUM

CUSUM means Cumulative Sum. It is used to identify whether actual energy performance is better or worse than an established expected or baseline performance over time.

For each period, determine the difference between actual energy use and the expected energy use. Add these differences cumulatively.

CUSUM = cumulative sum of (actual energy − expected energy)

A rising cumulative trend indicates that actual consumption is generally above the expected level; a falling trend indicates savings relative to the expected level.

Difference each period → cumulative addition → CUSUM plot → trend identified → saving / excess consumption investigated

3. Energy / Cost Index Diagram

An energy or cost index relates energy consumption or energy cost to a useful production or activity measure. It helps compare performance between periods, processes or production levels.

Energy index = Energy consumed / Suitable production or activity output

Similarly, a cost index can relate energy cost to production or another relevant output measure. A lower index generally indicates better energy performance when the comparison basis is consistent.

4. Energy Labelling

Energy labelling communicates the energy-performance information of equipment or appliances to help users compare options. In an exam answer, explain the purpose, information conveyed and how labels support informed selection of efficient equipment.

  • Helps compare energy performance.
  • Encourages selection of efficient equipment.
  • Supports reduction in energy consumption and operating cost.
  • Improves awareness of energy performance.

5. Energy Economics

Energy economics evaluates whether an energy-efficiency measure is financially worthwhile. The analysis should consider investment, operating savings, useful life and relevant cash flows.

Cost of production

Energy cost is one component of production cost. Energy-saving measures can reduce the energy component and therefore reduce the cost of producing the product or service, provided other effects are acceptable.

Life Cycle Costing (LCC)

LCC considers the costs associated with an equipment or project over its useful life rather than looking only at the initial purchase price.

Initial cost + installation + operating energy cost + maintenance/replacement costs → Life-cycle cost

6. Economic Evaluation Techniques

Simple Payback Period

The payback period is the time required for cumulative savings to recover the initial investment.

Payback period = Initial investment / Annual monetary savings

Discounting and Non-discounting

Non-discounting methods do not explicitly account for the time value of money. Simple payback is a common example.

Discounting methods account for the time value of money by considering when future cash flows occur. They are useful for comparing investments over time.

For long-term projects, discounted cash-flow methods can provide a more complete economic assessment than simple payback alone.

7. ESCO Concept

An Energy Service Company (ESCO) provides energy-efficiency services and can arrange implementation and performance-based energy projects. The ESCO concept links technical energy-saving measures with financial and performance considerations.

Industry → identifies saving opportunity → ESCO → project implementation / performance arrangement → measured energy savings → financial settlement

8. PAT Scheme

PAT means Perform, Achieve and Trade. It is an energy-efficiency mechanism associated with India's designated energy-intensive industries. The scheme uses performance targets and provides a mechanism for trading energy-saving certificates as applicable under the scheme.
