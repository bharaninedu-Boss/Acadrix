Problem 1 — Small-Hydro Hydraulic Power

Given: Water density ρ = 1000 kg/m³, flow rate Q = 2 m³/s, effective head H = 25 m, g = 9.81 m/s².

Find: Hydraulic power available.

Formula: P = ρgQH.

Substitute: P = 1000 × 9.81 × 2 × 25.

Final answer: P = 490,500 W = 490.5 kW.

Interpretation: This is hydraulic power before turbine, generator and other losses.

Problem 2 — Small-Hydro Electrical Output

Given: ρ = 1000 kg/m³, Q = 3 m³/s, H = 18 m and combined efficiency η = 0.82.

Find: Approximate electrical output.

Formula: Pout = ηρgQH.

Substitute: Pout = 0.82 × 1000 × 9.81 × 3 × 18.

Final answer: Pout ≈ 434.0 kW.

Problem 3 — Effect of Flow Rate on Hydro Power

Given: Head remains constant. Flow rate changes from Q₁ = 2 m³/s to Q₂ = 3 m³/s.

Find: Ratio of hydraulic powers.

Formula: P ∝ Q.

Calculation: P₂P₁ = Q₂Q₁ = 32 = 1.5.

Final answer: P₂ is 1.5 times P₁, i.e. 50% higher.

Problem 4 — OTEC Maximum Ideal Efficiency

Given: Warm seawater temperature TH = 25°C and cold seawater temperature TC = 5°C.

Find: Maximum ideal heat-engine efficiency using the Carnot expression.

Convert: TH = 298.15 K and TC = 278.15 K.

Formula: ηmax = 1 − TCTH.

Substitute: ηmax = 1 − 278.15298.15.

Final answer: ηmax ≈ 0.0671 = 6.71%.

Interpretation: The small temperature difference illustrates why OTEC has intrinsically low ideal cycle efficiency.

Problem 5 — OTEC Temperature Difference

Given: Surface-water temperature = 28°C and deep-water temperature = 6°C.

Find: Temperature difference.

Calculation: ΔT = 28 − 6 = 22°C = 22 K.

Final answer: ΔT = 22 K.

Interpretation: A sufficiently sustained temperature difference is necessary for practical OTEC operation.

Problem 6 — Hydro Energy Generated

Given: Electrical output = 500 kW maintained for 8 h.

Find: Electrical energy generated.

Formula: E = Pt.

Substitute: E = 500 × 8 = 4000 kWh.

Final answer: E = 4,000 kWh = 4 MWh.

Problem 7 — Binary-Cycle Geothermal Output

Given: Geothermal thermal input = 10 MW and overall conversion efficiency = 12%.

Find: Approximate electrical output.

Formula: Pe = ηPth.

Substitute: Pe = 0.12 × 10.

Final answer: Pe = 1.2 MW.

Interpretation: The assumed efficiency represents the combined conversion performance for this simplified calculation.

Problem 8 — Compare Geothermal Plant Outputs

Given: Plant A produces 8 MW and Plant B produces 12 MW.

Find: Percentage by which Plant B output exceeds Plant A.

Calculation: Difference = 12 − 8 = 4 MW.

Percentage increase: 48 × 100 = 50%.

Final answer: Plant B output is 50% higher than Plant A.

Problem 9 — Small-Hydro Capacity Factor

Given: Rated capacity = 1 MW. Annual generation = 4,380 MWh. Hours/year = 8,760 h.

Find: Capacity factor.

Formula: CF = Annual energyRated power × hours.

Substitute: CF = 4,3801 × 8,760 = 0.50.

Final answer: Capacity factor = 50%.
