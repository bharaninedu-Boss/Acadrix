1. Product Life Cycle

A product life cycle describes the stages through which a product passes from initial idea and development through production, use, service and end-of-life. Modern engineering requires information to remain consistent across these stages.

Typical flow: Idea → Requirements → Design → Development → Production → Distribution → Use/Service → Retirement/Reuse.

2. What is PLM?

Product Life Cycle Management (PLM) is an organised approach for managing product information, processes and collaboration across the product life cycle. It connects people, processes, product data and digital tools.

Why PLM is needed

  • Product information is generated by many departments.
  • Multiple revisions must be controlled.
  • Teams need a common view of product definition.
  • Changes must be communicated and traceable.
  • Global development creates distributed data and collaboration challenges.
  • Regulatory, quality and service information must remain connected.

3. Different Views of PLM

TermMeaning
EDMEngineering Data Management — manages engineering documents/data and their organisation.
PDMProduct Data Management — manages product definition data, documents, structures, versions and related workflows.
cPDmCollaborative Product Definition management — extends product-definition management into collaborative engineering processes.
CPCCollaborative Product Commerce — broader collaborative management of product-related information and business interactions.
PLMBroader life-cycle approach connecting product information, processes, people and business objectives.

4. PLM Opportunities

  • Single source of controlled product information.
  • Improved collaboration between design, manufacturing, quality, purchasing and service.
  • Faster change management.
  • Better configuration and revision control.
  • Reuse of engineering knowledge.
  • Reduced errors caused by outdated information.
  • Improved traceability and compliance.

5. PLM/PDM Infrastructure

PLM depends on an information infrastructure capable of storing, controlling, retrieving and exchanging product information.

Major elements

  • Network and communication: connect users, applications and sites.
  • Data management: store and control documents, models, metadata and product structures.
  • Application integration: connect CAD, simulation, ERP and other systems.
  • Security and access: ensure users see and modify appropriate information.
  • Backup and recovery: protect product data from loss.

6. Heterogeneous Data Sources

Engineering organisations use many formats and systems: CAD files, drawings, bills of materials, specifications, simulation results, manufacturing records, service information and enterprise data. These sources may differ in structure and software platform.

PLM helps establish controlled relationships between these information types rather than treating every file as an isolated document.

7. Simple PLM Information Flow

Customer need → Product requirements → CAD/CAE → Product structure/BOM → Manufacturing → Quality → Service → Feedback → Next product revision.
