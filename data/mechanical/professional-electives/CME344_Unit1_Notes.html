1. Product Life Cycle — Basic Concept

A product passes through a series of stages from the initial idea to development, production, use/service and end-of-life. PLM provides a controlled way to manage the product information and processes associated with these stages.

2. What is PLM?

Product Life Cycle Management (PLM) is an organised approach for managing product information, product-related processes and collaboration throughout the product life cycle.

Need for PLM

  • Many departments create and use product information.
  • Different versions and revisions must be controlled.
  • Design, manufacturing, quality and service teams need consistent information.
  • Changes need approval, communication and traceability.
  • Distributed teams need controlled access to common product information.
  • Product information must remain useful from development through service and end-of-life.

Opportunities offered by PLM

  • Single controlled source of product information.
  • Better collaboration between departments.
  • Faster and safer engineering change management.
  • Reuse of product knowledge and previous designs.
  • Improved traceability and configuration control.
  • Reduced errors caused by outdated or duplicated information.
  • Better visibility of product-development status.
Easy memory line: PLM connects people + process + product information + technology across the product life cycle.

3. Different Views of PLM

TermFull formMain idea
EDMEngineering Data ManagementControls and organises engineering data/documents.
PDMProduct Data ManagementControls product-definition data, documents, versions, structures and related information.
cPDmCollaborative Product Definition ManagementExtends product-definition management into collaborative product-development activities.
CPCCollaborative Product CommerceBroader collaboration around product information and product-related business activities.
PLMProduct Life Cycle ManagementLife-cycle-wide management of product information, processes and collaboration.

How they differ

Think of the terms as progressively broader ways of managing engineering/product information and collaboration. EDM is strongly data/document oriented; PDM adds product definition and structure; cPDm emphasises collaborative product definition; CPC extends collaboration into product-related commerce; PLM covers the broader product life cycle.

4. PLM/PDM Infrastructure

The infrastructure is the technical foundation that allows PLM/PDM information to be created, stored, controlled, shared and connected with other applications.

Main infrastructure elements

  1. Network: provides connectivity between users, servers and sites.
  2. Communication: enables controlled exchange of information between people and applications.
  3. Data management: stores product data, documents, metadata, structures and relationships.
  4. Security/access: controls who can view, edit, approve or release information.
  5. Application integration: connects PLM/PDM with CAD, CAE, ERP and other systems.

5. Heterogeneous Data Sources and Applications

Heterogeneous means that the information comes from different systems, formats, applications or structures. A mechanical product may involve CAD models, drawings, BOMs, specifications, analysis results, manufacturing data, quality records and service information.

Why it is a challenge

  • Different file formats and data structures.
  • Different software platforms.
  • Different naming and identification methods.
  • Different revision/status conventions.
  • Duplicate or disconnected information.

How PLM helps

PLM/PDM provides controlled relationships, identification, access and lifecycle context so that related product information can be managed as part of a common product definition.

6. Working / Operating Principle of PLM

PLM is not a physical machine; its "working principle" is an information and process flow.

  1. Product information is created or captured.
  2. The information is identified and stored in the controlled environment.
  3. Access rights determine who can use or modify it.
  4. Workflow controls review, approval and release.
  5. Related product structures and documents are linked.
  6. Changes create controlled revisions and traceability.
  7. Approved information is made available to downstream teams.
  8. Feedback from later life-cycle stages supports future decisions.

7. Advantages of PLM

  • Centralised control of product information.
  • Improved collaboration.
  • Better revision and configuration control.
  • Improved traceability.
  • Reduced information duplication and errors.
  • Better reuse of engineering knowledge.
  • Improved visibility of product-development activities.

8. Limitations / Disadvantages

  • Initial implementation can require significant time and investment.
  • Integration with legacy systems can be difficult.
  • Users need training and process discipline.
  • Poorly defined product data and workflows reduce the value of the system.
  • Migration of old data can be complex.
  • Security and access administration must be maintained carefully.
Exam point: Do not write PLM as simply "a database". It combines product information management with processes, collaboration and life-cycle control.

9. Important 2-Mark Questions & Answers

Q1. Define PLM.

Answer: Product Life Cycle Management is an organised approach for managing product information, product-related processes and collaboration throughout the product life cycle.

Q2. What is the need for PLM?

Answer: PLM is needed to control product information, revisions and changes and to provide consistent information for different departments throughout the product life cycle.

Q3. Expand EDM, PDM, cPDm and CPC.

Answer: EDM — Engineering Data Management; PDM — Product Data Management; cPDm — Collaborative Product Definition Management; CPC — Collaborative Product Commerce.

Q4. What is PDM?

Answer: PDM is the management of product-definition data such as documents, product structures, versions and related information in a controlled environment.

Q5. What is cPDm?

Answer: cPDm means Collaborative Product Definition Management. It focuses on managing product definition while supporting collaboration among product-development participants.

Q6. What is CPC?

Answer: CPC means Collaborative Product Commerce. It represents broader collaboration around product information and product-related business activities.

Q7. What is a heterogeneous data source?

Answer: It is a product-information source that differs from another source in format, structure, application or platform, such as CAD files, ERP records and legacy databases.

Q8. State two opportunities of PLM.

Answer: Improved cross-functional collaboration and better control of revisions/engineering changes. Other opportunities include information reuse, traceability and reduced duplication.

Q9. What is PLM/PDM infrastructure?

Answer: It is the combination of network, communication, data-management, security and application-integration facilities used to manage and exchange product information.

Q10. Why is data management important in PLM?

Answer: It ensures product information is stored, identified, controlled, retrieved and linked correctly so that users work with the required product definition.

10. Important 12-Mark Questions with Answer Notes

12-Mark Q1. Explain PLM, its need, opportunities and role throughout the product life cycle.

Introduction: PLM manages product information, processes and collaboration from product idea through development, production, service and end-of-life.

Need:

  1. Large quantity of product information.
  2. Multiple departments and users.
  3. Revision and change control.
  4. Need for traceability.
  5. Need for consistent product definition.
  6. Distributed development and collaboration.

Working: capture information → control and store → review/approve → release → use downstream → collect feedback → manage changes/revisions.

Opportunities: collaboration, information reuse, faster change management, traceability, reduced duplication, improved visibility and better control of the product life cycle.

Conclusion: PLM provides a common controlled framework for managing product knowledge and activities across the complete life cycle.

12-Mark Q3. Explain PLM/PDM infrastructure with networks, communication, data management and heterogeneous data sources/applications.

Construction/architecture: PLM/PDM core, network, communication layer, data-management facilities, security/access control and application-integration interfaces.

Operation: users and applications send/receive controlled product information through the infrastructure. Product data is stored and related; access and workflow rules control its use; integrated applications exchange relevant information.

Heterogeneous sources: CAD/CAE, drawings, BOMs, specifications, ERP, legacy databases and other applications may use different formats and structures.

Advantages: common information environment, controlled access, improved collaboration, traceability and integration.

Limitations: integration complexity, migration effort, training requirements, security administration and implementation cost.

11. How to Write a 12-Mark PLM Answer

  1. Write a short definition/introduction.
  2. Draw a clean labelled architecture or flow diagram.
  3. Explain the concept/component-wise.
  4. Explain the working/operation in numbered steps.
  5. Add advantages and limitations where applicable.
  6. Use a small comparison table when the question asks to compare terms.
  7. Finish with a short conclusion.
