CME380 — Unit IV

Steering, Brakes and Suspension Systems

Simple exam-focused notes with standard engineering sketches and important 12/13-mark preparation.

Syllabus coverage: steering geometry and steering gear boxes, power steering, front axles, suspension systems, pneumatic and hydraulic braking systems, ABS, EBD and traction control.

1. Steering System

The steering system changes the direction of the vehicle by turning the road wheels. A good steering system should give accurate control, minimum effort, stability and proper return of the wheels after a turn.

Main requirements

  • Easy steering with reasonable driver effort.
  • Directional stability during straight travel.
  • Minimum tyre wear.
  • Proper return to the straight-ahead position.
  • Good road feel and reliable operation.

2. Steering Geometry

Steering geometry describes the angular arrangement of the front wheels and steering components. Important terms are camber, caster, toe-in/toe-out and steering axis inclination.

  • Camber: inward or outward inclination of the wheel when viewed from the front.
  • Caster: inclination of the steering axis when viewed from the side.
  • Toe-in: front edges of the wheels are closer together than the rear edges.
  • Toe-out: front edges are farther apart than the rear edges.

Exam focus: Define camber, caster, toe-in and toe-out with simple sketches and state their purpose.

3. Steering Gear Boxes

The steering gear converts the steering-wheel rotation into suitable movement of the steering linkage while providing mechanical advantage.

Common types to remember

  • Worm and sector
  • Worm and roller
  • Worm and nut
  • Rack and pinion

Rack and pinion — simple working

Rotation of the steering pinion moves the rack sideways. The rack movement is transferred through the tie rods to turn the front wheels.

4. Power Steering

Power steering assists the driver by supplying additional steering force. The assistance may be hydraulic or electric.

  • Reduces steering effort.
  • Improves manoeuvrability at low speed.
  • Hydraulic systems use fluid pressure; electric power steering uses an electric motor and control system.

12/13-mark answer: Explain power steering with construction, working, advantages and limitations, using a neat labelled diagram.

5. Front Axle

The front axle supports the front portion of the vehicle and carries steering and wheel loads. Front axles may be broadly classified according to construction and drive arrangement.

  • Dead front axle
  • Live front axle

For an exam answer, draw the axle arrangement and explain the load-carrying and steering functions.

6. Suspension Systems

The suspension system connects the vehicle body to the wheels and helps absorb road shocks while maintaining tyre contact with the road.

Functions

  • Absorb road irregularities.
  • Maintain road-wheel contact.
  • Improve riding comfort.
  • Maintain vehicle stability and handling.
  • Reduce shock transmitted to the body and chassis.

Types

  • Dependent suspension
  • Independent suspension
  • Leaf spring suspension
  • Coil spring suspension
  • Torsion bar suspension
  • Air suspension

7. Pneumatic and Hydraulic Braking

Hydraulic braking

When the brake pedal is pressed, the master cylinder produces hydraulic pressure. Brake fluid carries this pressure to the wheel brakes, producing friction and slowing the vehicle.

Pneumatic braking

Compressed air is used to operate the brakes. Pneumatic systems are particularly suitable where a large braking force and reliable air supply are required.

8. Anti-lock Braking System (ABS)

ABS prevents excessive wheel lock during hard braking by sensing wheel speed and controlling brake pressure. This helps the driver retain steering control during braking.

Main advantages

  • Reduces wheel lock during severe braking.
  • Helps preserve steering control.
  • Can improve braking stability on suitable surfaces.

9. Electronic Brake-force Distribution (EBD)

EBD electronically adjusts brake-force distribution between the wheels/axles according to vehicle operating conditions. It works with the braking control system to use available tyre grip more effectively.

Remember: ABS mainly prevents wheel lock; EBD manages brake-force distribution.

10. Traction Control

Traction control limits excessive drive-wheel slip during acceleration. When a driven wheel starts spinning, the control system can reduce engine torque and/or apply braking to help restore traction.

Important long answer: Explain traction control with its purpose, basic control sequence, advantages and limitations.
