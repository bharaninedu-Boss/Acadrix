1. Geometric Modeling

Geometric modeling is the mathematical representation of the shape, size and spatial relationships of an engineering component. It provides the geometry required for CAD, visualization, analysis and manufacturing.

2. Hermite Cubic Spline

A Hermite cubic curve is defined by end points and tangent vectors at those end points. It is useful when both position and slope information are known.

P(u) = H₁(u)P₀ + H₂(u)P₁ + H₃(u)T₀ + H₄(u)T₁,   0 ≤ u ≤ 1

The four Hermite basis functions control the contribution of the two end positions and two tangent vectors.

3. Bezier Curve

A Bezier curve is controlled by a set of control points. The curve generally does not pass through all intermediate control points; instead, they influence its shape.

P(u) = Σ Bᵢ,ₙ(u)Pᵢ,   Bᵢ,ₙ(u) = C(n,i)uⁱ(1−u)ⁿ⁻ⁱ
  • First and last control points are the end points.
  • Curve lies within the convex hull of its control points.
  • Moving a control point changes the curve shape.
  • Higher degree increases global control and computational complexity.

4. B-Spline Curve

A B-spline uses piecewise polynomial segments and a knot vector. It provides local control: changing a control point normally affects only a portion of the curve.

  • Local shape control.
  • Flexible degree selection.
  • Continuity can be controlled at knots.
  • Well suited to complex CAD geometry.

6. Surface Entities and Representation

A CAD surface represents a two-dimensional manifold in three-dimensional space. Common surface descriptions use parametric coordinates such as u and v.

Bezier Surface

A Bezier surface is controlled by a rectangular grid of control points. It extends the Bezier concept into two parameters.

B-Spline Surface

A B-spline surface uses control-point grids and knot vectors in two parametric directions. It provides local shape control and is widely useful for free-form design.

Coons Surface

A Coons surface is generated from boundary curves. It is useful when four boundary curves are known and a smooth surface patch must be constructed between them.

7. Solid Modeling

Solid modeling represents a complete three-dimensional object including its enclosed volume. It supports mass-property calculations, interference checks, sectioning and manufacturing preparation.

Boundary Representation (B-Rep)

B-Rep describes a solid using vertices, edges and faces, together with their topology and geometry.

Sweep Representation

A sweep creates a solid or surface by moving a profile along a path. Extrusion and revolution are common sweep operations.

Constructive Solid Geometry (CSG)

CSG builds a solid from simple primitives such as blocks, cylinders and cones using Boolean operations.
