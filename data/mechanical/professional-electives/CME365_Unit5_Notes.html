CME365 — Unit V: Ocean and Geothermal Energy

R-2021 • Renewable Energy Technologies

2 Marks12 Marks

Official syllabus: Small hydro, tidal, wave, open and closed OTEC, limitations, geothermal sources, geothermal power plants, applications and environmental impact.

1. Small Hydro Power

Small hydro systems generate electricity by using the energy of flowing or falling water. They are suitable for selected rivers, canals and streams where adequate head and flow are available.

Hydraulic power: Phyd = ρgQH

where ρ is water density, g is gravitational acceleration, Q is flow rate and H is effective head. Actual electrical output is lower because turbine, generator and other system losses must be considered.

  • Uses a local water resource without requiring large-scale fuel combustion.
  • Suitable for grid-connected or appropriately designed isolated systems.
  • Site assessment must consider flow, head, seasonal variation and environmental constraints.

2. Tidal Energy

Tidal energy uses the periodic rise and fall or movement of seawater caused mainly by gravitational interactions involving the Earth, Moon and Sun. Energy can be extracted from tidal range or tidal currents.

Tidal Range

A tidal-range system uses the difference in water level between high tide and low tide. A barrage or lagoon arrangement can store and release water through turbines.

High tide → Reservoir / basin → Turbine → Low tide ↑ Water-level difference

Tidal-Stream Systems

Tidal-stream turbines extract kinetic energy from moving tidal currents. They resemble underwater turbines and are selected where current speed and site conditions are favourable.

3. Wave Energy

Wave energy is obtained from the motion of ocean waves generated primarily by wind acting over the sea surface. Wave-energy converters transform wave motion into mechanical or electrical power.

  • Wave resource depends on wind conditions, fetch, water depth and local geography.
  • Devices must withstand cyclic loading, corrosion and severe marine conditions.
  • Energy conversion methods vary with the type of wave-energy converter.

4. Ocean Thermal Energy Conversion (OTEC)

OTEC uses the temperature difference between relatively warm surface seawater and colder deep seawater to operate a heat-engine cycle. The resource is most suitable where a sufficiently large and sustained temperature difference exists.

Open-Cycle OTEC

In an open cycle, warm seawater itself acts as the working fluid. It is evaporated under low pressure, producing steam that expands through a turbine. The steam can then be condensed using cold deep seawater, producing fresh water as a useful by-product.

Warm seawater → Low-pressure evaporator → Steam → Turbine → Generator ↓ Condenser + cold seawater → Fresh water

Closed-Cycle OTEC

In a closed cycle, a low-boiling-point working fluid circulates in a closed loop. Warm seawater supplies heat to vaporise the working fluid; the vapour drives a turbine and cold deep seawater condenses it for recirculation.

Warm seawater → Evaporator → Working-fluid vapour → Turbine → Generator ↑ ↓ Working fluid ← Condenser ← Cold deep seawater

OTEC Limitations

  • Small temperature difference results in low cycle efficiency.
  • Large seawater flow rates and heat exchangers are required.
  • Marine construction and maintenance are difficult.
  • Corrosion, biofouling and seawater handling require careful design.
  • Suitable sites are geographically limited.

5. Geothermal Energy

Geothermal energy is heat obtained from the Earth's interior. Useful resources may occur as hot water, steam or hot rocks at accessible depths and temperatures.

Geothermal Sources

  • Hydrothermal resources: naturally occurring hot water or steam reservoirs.
  • Hot dry rock / enhanced geothermal concepts: heat stored in hot rock may be accessed by creating or improving fluid circulation pathways.
  • Geothermal heat: can be used directly for heating as well as for power generation where resource conditions permit.

6. Geothermal Power Plants

Geothermal power plants convert geothermal heat into electricity. The plant configuration depends on the temperature, pressure and phase of the geothermal resource.

Plant typeBasic principle
Dry steamNatural steam from the reservoir is directed to a turbine.
Flash steamHigh-pressure hot water is depressurised so part of it flashes into steam, which drives a turbine.
Binary cycleGeothermal fluid heats a secondary working fluid with a lower boiling point; the secondary vapour drives the turbine.
Geothermal reservoir → Production well → Steam / hot fluid → Turbine → Generator ↓ Condenser / reinjection → Reinjection well

8. Environmental Impact

Ocean Energy

  • Marine structures can interact with aquatic habitats.
  • Construction and operation may affect sediment transport and local hydrodynamics.
  • Underwater noise and electromagnetic/electrical infrastructure may require assessment.
  • Navigation, fishing and coastal activities must be considered.

Geothermal Energy

  • Drilling and surface infrastructure disturb land.
  • Geothermal fluids may contain dissolved minerals or gases requiring proper handling.
  • Reinjection helps manage fluids and maintain resource use where appropriate.
  • Induced seismicity and subsidence can be concerns for some projects.

9. Important Formulae

Small-hydro hydraulic power: P = ρgQH

Actual electrical output: Pout = ηρgQH, where η represents the combined efficiency used for the selected system model.

12. Quick Revision

Small hydro: Water head + flow → turbine → generator.
Tidal: Tidal range or tidal current → turbine → power.
Wave: Wave motion → converter → power.
OTEC: Warm surface water + cold deep water → temperature-difference cycle → power.
Geothermal: Earth heat → steam/secondary fluid → turbine → generator.
Geothermal plants: Dry steam · Flash steam · Binary cycle.
