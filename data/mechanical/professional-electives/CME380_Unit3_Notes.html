1. Transmission System

The transmission system carries power from the engine to the driving wheels and changes the speed, torque and direction of motion according to vehicle operating conditions.

Main functions

  • Transmit engine power to the wheels.
  • Provide different speed and torque ratios.
  • Allow the vehicle to start from rest smoothly.
  • Provide reverse motion.
  • Disconnect the engine from the wheels when required.

2. Clutch

A clutch connects and disconnects the engine from the transmission system. It permits smooth starting and gear changing.

Requirements of a good clutch

  • Gradual engagement without shock.
  • Complete disengagement when required.
  • Good heat dissipation.
  • Low wear and reasonable service life.
  • Compact construction and easy maintenance.

Types

Common automobile clutches include single-plate, multi-plate, cone and centrifugal clutches. The syllabus requires clutch types and construction.

3. Gearboxes

Manual gearbox

Driver selects a gear ratio mechanically. Different gear pairs provide different speed-torque combinations.

  • Simple and robust.
  • Driver controls gear selection.
  • Requires clutch operation during gear changes.

Automatic gearbox

Gear selection is performed automatically using hydraulic/electronic control with planetary gear arrangements in many designs.

  • Easy driving.
  • Automatic ratio selection.
  • More complex than a basic manual transmission.

Gear-shift mechanism

The gear-shift mechanism selects the required gear ratio. In a manual transmission, the selector mechanism moves the synchronizer/gear arrangement to establish the selected drive ratio.

4. Overdrive and Transfer Box

Overdrive

Overdrive provides an output speed higher than the input speed under suitable operating conditions. It reduces engine speed during cruising and can improve fuel economy and reduce noise.

Transfer box

A transfer box distributes power from the transmission to different driven axles, especially in vehicles requiring multiple driven axles.

5. Fluid Flywheel and Torque Converter

Fluid flywheel

A fluid flywheel, or fluid coupling, transmits power through fluid circulation between an impeller and runner. It provides smooth power transmission but does not by itself provide torque multiplication.

Torque converter

A torque converter is a hydrodynamic unit containing an impeller, turbine and stator. It provides smooth coupling and can multiply torque during vehicle acceleration.

6. Propeller Shaft, Slip Joint and Universal Joint

Propeller shaft: transmits rotary power from the gearbox/transfer case to the differential in vehicles where the axle is separated from the transmission.

Slip joint: permits change in effective shaft length caused by suspension movement and driveline geometry.

Universal joint: transmits torque between shafts whose axes operate at an angle.

7. Differential and Rear Axle

The differential permits the two driving wheels on an axle to rotate at different speeds while transmitting torque. This is essential during cornering because the outer wheel travels a longer path than the inner wheel.

Basic functions

  • Transmit drive to the rear axle/wheels.
  • Allow different wheel speeds during turning.
  • Provide the required final drive reduction in the axle assembly.

8. Hotchkiss Drive and Torque Tube Drive

Hotchkiss drive

The propeller shaft transmits driving torque and the axle assembly is supported through the vehicle's spring arrangement. The springs take driving and braking reactions as well as support the axle.

Torque tube drive

A torque tube encloses/supports the propeller shaft and transmits the driving and reaction forces through the driveline support arrangement.
