1. What is Non-Traditional Machining?

Non-traditional machining (NTM) processes remove material using an energy form other than the direct cutting action of a conventional sharp cutting tool. The energy may be mechanical, chemical, electrochemical, electrical/thermal or a combination of more than one mechanism.

These processes are useful when conventional machining is difficult because of very hard material, brittle material, complex geometry, very small features or special surface requirements.

Exam definition: Non-traditional machining is a group of material-removal processes in which material is removed by mechanical, chemical, electrochemical, thermal or combined energy rather than by the direct action of a conventional cutting tool.

2. Need for Non-Traditional Machining

  • Machining of very hard and high-strength materials.
  • Machining of brittle materials with reduced dependence on conventional cutting action.
  • Production of intricate profiles and small features.
  • Machining where conventional tool access is difficult.
  • Reduction of cutting-force-related problems in suitable processes.
  • Ability to use special energy forms for precision or finishing operations.

Remember: The main reason for selecting an NTM process is that the material, geometry, accuracy or surface requirement may make ordinary cutting uneconomical or unsuitable.

4. Abrasive Jet Machining (AJM)

Principle: In AJM, fine abrasive particles are accelerated by a high-velocity gas stream and directed through a nozzle onto the work surface. Material is removed mainly by abrasive impact and erosion.

Standard schematic for exam drawing — redraw neatly with labels.

Equipment

  1. Compressed gas supply
  2. Abrasive storage/hopper
  3. Mixing chamber
  4. Flow-control arrangement
  5. Nozzle
  6. Work-holding arrangement

Important process parameters

  • Abrasive type, size and concentration
  • Carrier-gas pressure and velocity
  • Nozzle geometry and stand-off distance
  • Impingement angle
  • Work material

Advantages

  • Suitable for hard and brittle materials.
  • Small and intricate features can be produced in suitable materials.
  • Mechanical cutting forces can be relatively low.

Limitations

  • Nozzle wear can affect accuracy.
  • Abrasive handling and disposal are required.
  • Material-removal rate is comparatively low for many applications.
  • Surface quality can depend strongly on process conditions.

Applications

Fine cutting, cleaning, deburring, engraving and machining of suitable hard/brittle materials.

5. Abrasive Water Jet Machining (AWJM)

Principle: AWJM uses a high-velocity water jet, with abrasive particles added for abrasive cutting/erosion of the work material.

Standard schematic for exam drawing.

Important parameters

  • Water pressure and jet velocity
  • Abrasive flow rate and particle size
  • Nozzle diameter and stand-off distance
  • Traverse speed
  • Material thickness and properties

Advantages and limitations

AdvantagesLimitations
Suitable for a wide range of materials.Nozzle/focusing tube wear.
Low thermal effect compared with thermal cutting.Abrasive consumption and disposal.
Useful for complex profiles.Cut quality varies with thickness and settings.

Applications

Profile cutting, trimming and machining of materials that can be processed effectively by abrasive water jets.

6. Ultrasonic Machining (USM)

Principle: USM removes material by abrasive action between the vibrating tool and the workpiece, with abrasive slurry present in the machining gap.

Standard USM arrangement: vibrating tool, abrasive slurry and workpiece.

Working

  1. The transducer produces high-frequency mechanical vibration.
  2. The horn transmits/amplifies the vibration to the tool.
  3. Abrasive slurry is supplied in the gap between tool and workpiece.
  4. Abrasive grains repeatedly impact the work surface.
  5. Small particles are fractured/removed and the tool shape is progressively reproduced in the workpiece.

Important parameters

  • Vibration amplitude and frequency
  • Abrasive type, size and concentration
  • Static tool load
  • Slurry flow
  • Tool material and geometry
  • Work material

Advantages

  • Useful for hard and brittle materials.
  • Can produce intricate shapes and holes.
  • Thermal damage is not the main removal mechanism.

Limitations

  • Material removal rate can be low.
  • Tool wear can occur.
  • Process efficiency depends strongly on abrasive and operating conditions.

Applications

Machining of hard/brittle materials and production of holes, cavities and intricate features in suitable workpieces.
