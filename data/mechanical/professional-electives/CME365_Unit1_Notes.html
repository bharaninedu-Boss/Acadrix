CME365 — Unit I: Energy Scenario

R-2021 • Renewable Energy Technologies

2 Marks12 Marks

Official syllabus: Indian energy scenario in domestic, industrial, commercial, agriculture, transportation and other sectors; present conventional energy status; present renewable energy status; potential of renewable sources; global energy status; per-capita energy consumption; future energy plans.

1. Energy Scenario

An energy scenario describes how energy is produced, converted, transported and consumed by a country or region. It includes energy sources, sector-wise demand, resource availability, infrastructure and future requirements.

2. Conventional and Renewable Energy

3. Indian Energy-Consumption Sectors

  • Domestic: cooking, lighting, appliances, cooling and heating.
  • Industrial: motors, boilers, furnaces, compressors, process heat and production.
  • Commercial: buildings, lighting, HVAC, refrigeration and services.
  • Agriculture: irrigation pumps, farm machinery and processing.
  • Transportation: road, rail, aviation and marine transport.
  • Other sectors: public infrastructure and services with energy demand.

4. Present Conventional Energy Status

Conventional energy remains important because of established infrastructure, high energy density and dispatchable generation in many applications. Its limitations include finite resources, price and supply risks, and environmental impacts associated with extraction and use.

5. Present Renewable Energy Status

Renewable technologies form an increasingly important part of energy systems. Solar and wind are variable resources, while biomass and hydro can provide different operating characteristics depending on the resource and system design. Renewable deployment must be considered together with transmission, distribution, storage and demand management.

6. Renewable-Energy Potential

Resource potential is the amount of energy that may be obtainable from a resource under defined technical, geographic and economic conditions. Potential is not the same as installed capacity or actual annual generation.

Capacity factor = Actual energy generatedRated power × available time

7. Per-Capita Energy Consumption

Per-capita energy consumption is the average energy consumption associated with each person over a specified period.

Per-capita energy consumption = Total energy consumptionPopulation

It is useful for broad comparison but does not by itself measure energy efficiency, income distribution or quality of life.

8. Global Energy Status

The global energy system contains fossil fuels, nuclear energy and renewable resources. Energy transitions involve renewable generation, electrification, efficiency improvement, storage, grid development and cleaner energy carriers.

9. Future Energy Plans

  1. Estimate future energy demand.
  2. Assess available resources and their potential.
  3. Compare technologies technically and economically.
  4. Plan generation, transmission, distribution and storage.
  5. Include environmental and social constraints.
  6. Improve energy efficiency and demand management.
  7. Maintain reliability, resilience and energy security.

10. Energy Security

Energy security means maintaining reliable access to sufficient energy at acceptable cost while managing supply, infrastructure, environmental and geopolitical risks. Diversifying energy sources can reduce dependence on a single resource or supplier.
