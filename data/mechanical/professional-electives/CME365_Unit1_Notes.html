CME365 — Unit I: Energy Scenario

R-2021 • Renewable Energy Technologies

1 Mark3 Marks12 Marks

Syllabus: Indian energy scenario in domestic, industrial, commercial, agricultural, transportation and other sectors; conventional and renewable status; potential; global status; per-capita consumption; future energy plans.

1. What is an Energy Scenario?

An energy scenario describes how energy is produced, converted, transported and consumed by a country or region. It includes the sources used, sector-wise demand, installed capacity, resource availability, environmental impact and future requirements.

2. Conventional and Renewable Energy

Conventional sourcesRenewable sources
Coal, petroleum, natural gas and other finite fuels commonly used at large scale.Solar, wind, biomass, small hydro, geothermal, tidal and wave resources that are naturally replenished.
Many conventional fuels have finite reserves and significant environmental impacts.Generally lower operational emissions, though each technology has its own land, material and ecological impacts.

3. Indian Energy Consumption Sectors

  • Domestic: cooking, lighting, appliances, cooling and heating.
  • Industrial: motors, boilers, furnaces, compressors, process heat and production.
  • Commercial: buildings, lighting, HVAC, refrigeration and services.
  • Agriculture: irrigation pumps, farm machinery and processing.
  • Transportation: road, rail, aviation and marine transport.

4. Why Renewable Energy is Important

  • Reduces dependence on finite fossil resources.
  • Diversifies the energy mix.
  • Can reduce greenhouse-gas emissions when replacing high-emission generation.
  • Supports distributed generation and energy access.
  • Creates opportunities for local manufacturing and employment.
  • Can improve energy security by using indigenous resources.

5. Renewable Energy Potential

Potential is the amount of energy that may be technically and economically obtainable from a resource under specified conditions. Resource potential is not the same as installed capacity or actual annual generation.

Capacity factor: Actual energy generatedRated power × available time

A high theoretical resource potential does not automatically mean a project is feasible. Land, grid connection, intermittency, cost, technology, environmental restrictions and infrastructure also matter.

6. Per-Capita Energy Consumption

Per-capita energy consumption is the average energy consumption associated with each person in a population over a specified period.

Per-capita energy consumption = Total energy consumption / Population

It is an indicator useful for comparing broad energy-use patterns, but it does not by itself describe efficiency, income distribution or quality of life.

7. Global Energy Scenario

The global energy system contains a mixture of fossil fuels, nuclear energy and renewable sources. The transition toward lower-carbon energy involves increasing renewable generation, electrification, energy efficiency, storage, grid development and cleaner fuels.

8. Future Energy Planning

  1. Estimate future demand.
  2. Assess available resources.
  3. Compare technologies on technical and economic grounds.
  4. Plan generation, transmission, distribution and storage.
  5. Include environmental and social constraints.
  6. Improve energy efficiency and demand management.
  7. Maintain reliability and resilience.
