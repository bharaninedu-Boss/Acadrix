ME3591 — Unit II Solved Problems

ACADRIX • R-2021 • Design of Shafts and Couplings

Format followed: Given → To find → loading analysis → formula → substitution → intermediate calculation → check → standard selection → final answer. Only important formulas are boxed. Final answers are not boxed.

1. Square Key — April/May 2024 Q12(a)

Given:

Shaft diameter, d = 25 mm
Power transmitted, P = 15 kW = 15,000 W
Speed, N = 720 rpm
Key material = 50C4 steel
Yield strength, Syt = 460 N/mm²
FOS = 3

To find:

Suitable dimensions of the square key.

Step 1 — Calculate the torque transmitted

T = 60P / (2πN)

Substitute P = 15,000 W and N = 720 rpm.

T = [60 × 15,000 / (2π × 720)] N·m
T = 198.944 N·m
T = 198.944 × 10³ N·mm

Step 2 — Find allowable shear stress

τallow = Syt / (2 × FOS)
τallow = 460 / (2 × 3)
τallow = 76.67 N/mm²

Since compressive yield strength is taken equal to tensile yield strength:

σc,allow = 460 / 3 = 153.33 N/mm²

Step 3 — Select the theoretical square-key size

For the preliminary square-key calculation, take b = h ≈ d/4.

b = h = 25/4 = 6.25 mm

A practical standard size is then checked. Use the standard size available for the shaft diameter.

Step 4 — Calculate key length from shear

T = τallow b l d / 2

Rearrange for l:

l = 2T / (τallow b d)

Using the theoretical b = 6.25 mm:

l = [2 × 198,944] / [76.67 × 6.25 × 25]
l = 33.18 mm

Step 5 — Adopt a practical standard key

Adopt a 7 × 7 mm square key and take a practical length of 35 mm.

Step 6 — Check shear stress for the adopted key

τ = 2T / (bld)
τ = [2 × 198,944] / (7 × 35 × 25)
τ = 64.96 N/mm²

64.96 N/mm² < 76.67 N/mm² → Safe in shear.

Step 7 — Check crushing stress

σc = 4T / (hld)
σc = [4 × 198,944] / (7 × 35 × 25)
σc = 129.0 N/mm²

129.0 N/mm² < 153.33 N/mm² → Safe in crushing.

Final answer: Adopt a 7 × 7 × 35 mm square key.

Common mistake: Do not calculate only shear length and stop. The key must also be checked for crushing.

2. Muff Coupling — April/May 2024 Q12(b)

Given:

Power, P = 25 kW
Speed, N = 360 rpm
Shaft and key material = 30C8/40C8 as stated in the question set
Shaft yield strength, Syt = 400 N/mm²
Sleeve material = FG200, Sut = 200 N/mm²
FOS for shaft/key = 4
FOS for sleeve = 6

To find:

Shaft diameter, sleeve diameter and length, and key dimensions.

Step 1 — Calculate transmitted torque

T = 60P / (2πN)
T = [60 × 25,000 / (2π × 360)] N·m
T = 663.146 N·m
T = 663.146 × 10³ N·mm

Step 2 — Find allowable shear stress for shaft

τallow = Syt / (2 × FOS)
τallow = 400 / (2 × 4)
τallow = 50 N/mm²

Step 3 — Calculate theoretical shaft diameter

T = (π/16) τallow d³

Therefore:

d = [16T / (πτallow)]1/3
d = [16 × 663,146 / (π × 50)]1/3
d = 40.73 mm

Choose the next practical standard shaft diameter:

Adopt d = 45 mm

Step 4 — Design the muff/sleeve

Using the usual Bhandari proportion for sleeve outside diameter:

D ≈ 2d + 13 mm
D = 2(45) + 13 = 103 mm

Adopt:

D = 105 mm

For sleeve length:

L ≈ 3.5d
L = 3.5 × 45 = 157.5 mm

Adopt:

L = 160 mm

Step 5 — Check sleeve shear

Allowable sleeve shear stress:

Ssd,sleeve = 200 / (2 × 6)
Ssd,sleeve = 16.67 N/mm²
τ = 16T / [πD³(1 − (d/D)⁴)]
τ = [16 × 663,146] / [π × 105³ × {1 − (45/105)⁴}]
τ ≈ 3.02 N/mm²

3.02 N/mm² < 16.67 N/mm² → Sleeve is safe.

Step 6 — Select the standard key

For a 45 mm shaft, select the applicable standard parallel key from the prescribed data book:

Key width, b = 14 mm
Key height, h = 9 mm

Step 7 — Find key length from shear

l = 2T / (τallow b d)
l = [2 × 663,146] / (50 × 14 × 45)
l = 42.10 mm

Step 8 — Find key length from crushing

l = 4T / (σc,allow h d)

For equal tensile/compressive yield strengths:

σc,allow = 400 / 4 = 100 N/mm²
l = [4 × 663,146] / (100 × 9 × 45)
l = 65.50 mm

The crushing requirement is larger, so it governs.

Adopt a practical key length:

l = 80 mm

Step 9 — Check the adopted key

Shear stress:

τ = [2 × 663,146] / (14 × 80 × 45)
τ = 26.32 N/mm² < 50 N/mm²

Crushing stress:

σc = [4 × 663,146] / (9 × 80 × 45)
σc = 81.87 N/mm² < 100 N/mm²

Both key checks are safe.

Final answer: Shaft diameter = 45 mm; sleeve outside diameter = 105 mm; sleeve length = 160 mm; key = 14 × 9 × 80 mm.

Common mistake: Sleeve length and key length are different quantities. The key is governed by whichever of shear or crushing gives the larger required length.

3. Line Shaft with Two Pulleys — April/May 2024 Q16(a)

Given:

Tight-side belt tension at pulley A = 2.7 kN = 2700 N
Belt tension ratio = 3 : 1
Shaft material = 40C8
Sut = 650 N/mm²
Syt = 380 N/mm²
Keyed pulleys
ASME Kb = 1.5, Kt = 1.0
Pulley A diameter = 250 mm
Pulley B diameter = 450 mm
Bearing spacing: C–A = 450 mm, A–B = 450 mm, B–D = 250 mm

To find:

Critical bending moment and shaft diameter.

Step 1 — Find the belt tensions at pulley A

Given:

T1 = 2700 N
T1/T2 = 3
T2 = 2700/3 = 900 N

The vertical belt pull on the shaft is:

FA = T1 + T2
FA = 2700 + 900 = 3600 N

Step 2 — Calculate transmitted torque

T = (T1 − T2)D/2
T = (2700 − 900)(250/2)
T = 1800 × 125
T = 225,000 N·mm

Step 3 — Find belt tensions at pulley B

The same shaft torque is transmitted at B.

(T3 − T4)(450/2) = 225,000
T3 − T4 = 1000 N

Since T3/T4 = 3:

3T4 − T4 = 1000
2T4 = 1000
T4 = 500 N
T3 = 1500 N

Horizontal belt pull:

FB = 1500 + 500 = 2000 N

Step 4 — Vertical-plane bearing reactions

Distance between bearings:

CD = 450 + 450 + 250 = 1150 mm

Taking moments about C:

RDv(1150) = 3600(450)
RDv = 1408.7 N

Vertical equilibrium:

RCv + RDv = 3600
RCv = 3600 − 1408.7
RCv = 2191.3 N

Step 5 — Horizontal-plane bearing reactions

Taking moments about C:

RDh(1150) = 2000(900)
RDh = 1565.2 N

Horizontal equilibrium:

RCh = 2000 − 1565.2
RCh = 434.8 N

Step 6 — Bending moment at pulley A

Vertical bending moment:

Mv = RCv(450)
Mv = 2191.3 × 450
Mv = 986,087 N·mm

Horizontal bending moment:

Mh = RCh(450)
Mh = 434.8 × 450
Mh = 195,652 N·mm

Step 7 — Resultant bending moment

M = √(Mv² + Mh²)
M = √[(986,087)² + (195,652)²]
M ≈ 1.005 × 10⁶ N·mm

The corresponding resultant moment at the other critical locations is lower, so section near pulley A governs.

Step 8 — Find allowable shear stress by ASME criterion

τallow = min(0.30Syt, 0.18Sut)
0.30Syt = 0.30 × 380 = 114 MPa
0.18Sut = 0.18 × 650 = 117 MPa
τallow = 114 MPa

Because the shaft is keyed, apply the specified 25% reduction:

τallow,keyed = 0.75 × 114
τallow,keyed = 85.5 MPa

Step 9 — Calculate shaft diameter

d³ = 16/(πτallow) × √[(KbM)² + (KtT)²]

Substitute:

d³ = [16/(π × 85.5)] × √[(1.5 × 1.005×10⁶)² + (1.0 × 225×10³)²]
d³ ≈ 91,000 mm³
d ≈ 44.95 mm

Step 10 — Select standard diameter and verify

Adopt:

d = 45 mm

Check the actual combined design shear:

τactual = [16/(π × 45³)] × √[(1.5 × 1.005×10⁶)² + (225×10³)²]
τactual ≈ 85.2 MPa

85.2 MPa < 85.5 MPa → Safe.

Final answer: Critical section is near pulley A; required diameter ≈ 44.95 mm; adopt shaft diameter = 45 mm.

Common mistakes: (1) using only the vertical bending moment, (2) forgetting the horizontal belt pull, (3) adding bending moments arithmetically instead of taking the resultant, and (4) forgetting the keyed-shaft reduction in allowable stress.

Unit II solving method to remember

  1. Read the figure first.
  2. Write every load with its direction and unit.
  3. Calculate torque from power or belt/gear forces.
  4. Resolve loads into planes.
  5. Find bearing reactions.
  6. Draw/identify bending moments and locate the critical section.
  7. Combine bending and torsion using the specified theory/code.
  8. Calculate theoretical size.
  9. Select the next practical standard size from the prescribed data book.
  10. Recalculate/check the final stress, rigidity or strength.
