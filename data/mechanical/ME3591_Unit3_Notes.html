ME3591 — Unit III: Design of Temporary and Permanent Joints

ACADRIX • Anna University R-2021 • Semester 5

Syllabus scope: Threaded fasteners; bolted and eccentrically loaded bolted joints; knuckle and cotter joints; welded joints including butt, fillet and parallel transverse fillet welds under bending, torsion and eccentric loading; riveted structural joints; theory of bonded joints.

1. Threaded Fasteners

Threaded fasteners are detachable joints used to connect machine parts. The design must consider bolt preload, service loading and possible failure modes such as tensile failure, shear and thread-related failure.

2. Bolted Joints

For a preloaded joint, bolt tension and the clamped members interact. Under service loading, the load shared by the bolt and joint depends on their relative stiffness.

For eccentric loading, the direct load and the moment due to eccentricity must both be considered.

3. Knuckle and Cotter Joints

A knuckle joint connects two rods subjected mainly to tensile load while permitting limited angular movement. A cotter joint connects two rods carrying axial load and uses a tapered cotter to resist separation.

Design checks should cover the relevant rod, pin/cotter, eye, fork and bearing/shear sections identified by the prescribed design procedure.

4. Welded Joints

Welded joints join components permanently. Common forms in this unit are butt welds and fillet welds, including parallel transverse fillet arrangements.

For weld groups under bending, torsion or eccentric loading, identify the weld group centroid, calculate the relevant direct and moment-induced stresses, and check the critical location.

5. Riveted and Bonded Joints

Riveted structural joints are permanent mechanical joints. Bonded joints transfer load through an adhesive layer; their behaviour depends on the adherends, adhesive and load distribution.

6. 12-Mark Answer — Eccentrically Loaded Bolted Joint

  1. Draw the joint and locate the bolt-group centroid.
  2. Resolve the applied load into a direct component and an eccentric moment.
  3. Calculate the direct load shared by the bolt group.
  4. Calculate the moment caused by eccentricity.
  5. Determine the additional load/stress in each bolt according to its distance and direction from the centroid.
  6. Identify the most heavily loaded bolt.
  7. Check the critical bolt/joint section against the applicable allowable condition and factor of safety.
  8. Select practical standard dimensions and re-check.
M = P × e

Conclusion: Eccentric loading must be treated as direct loading plus a moment; the critical bolt is generally the one receiving the greatest combined effect.

7. 12-Mark Answer — Design of a Knuckle Joint

  1. State the function and loading of the joint.
  2. Determine the tensile load.
  3. Select material and allowable stresses.
  4. Size the rod for tensile strength.
  5. Design/check the knuckle pin for shear and bending as applicable.
  6. Check the single eye for tearing and bearing.
  7. Check the fork for tearing, shear and bearing.
  8. Check the pin against the governing failure mode.
  9. Adopt standard dimensions and verify all sections.

Conclusion: The final dimensions are governed by the most critical failure mode after all component checks.

8. 12-Mark Answer — Design of a Cotter Joint

  1. Identify the axial tensile load.
  2. Design the rod section.
  3. Determine cotter dimensions from the applicable shear and bearing checks.
  4. Check the rod section weakened by the cotter slot.
  5. Check the socket/spigot for tearing or crushing as applicable.
  6. Check the cotter and joint components for shear and bearing.
  7. Adopt practical standard dimensions.
  8. Re-check every governing failure condition.

Conclusion: Cotter-joint design is a sequence of strength checks on the rod, cotter and connecting portions.

9. 12-Mark Answer — Welded Joint under Bending/Torsion/Eccentric Load

  1. Draw the weld group and locate its centroid.
  2. Determine direct load on the weld group.
  3. Determine the moment produced by the applied load.
  4. For bending, determine the stress distribution about the relevant axis.
  5. For torsion, determine the shear stress distribution around the weld group.
  6. For eccentric loading, combine direct and moment effects at the critical point.
  7. Determine the maximum resultant weld stress.
  8. Compare with the allowable weld stress and apply the specified factor of safety.
  9. Select weld size/length and verify.

Exam diagram: Show the weld group, load, eccentricity, centroid and critical point clearly.
