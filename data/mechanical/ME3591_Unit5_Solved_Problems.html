Problem 1 — Rolling-contact bearing selection

Question basis: Select a suitable bearing for a shaft requiring a 75 mm bore, carrying radial load Fr = 8 kN and axial load Fa = 3 kN at 1200 rpm for a required life of 20,000 h.

Given
Fr = 8 kN
Fa = 3 kN
N = 1200 rpm
Lh = 20,000 h
Bore required = 75 mm

To find

Suitable standard rolling-contact bearing.

Step 1 — Convert required life

Number of revolutions = 20,000 × 60 × 1200 = 1.44 × 109 rev.

Therefore, L10 = 1440 million revolutions.

Step 2 — Check combined loading

For the selected deep-groove ball-bearing case, use the standard factors X, Y and e from the permitted bearing data. The worked case uses C0 = 72 kN, e = 0.24, X = 0.56 and Y = 1.8.

FaFr = 38 = 0.375 > 0.24, so axial load must be included.

P = XVFr + YFa

Taking V = 1:

P = 0.56 × 1 × 8000 + 1.8 × 3000

P = 4480 + 5400 = 9880 N = 9.88 kN.

Step 3 — Required dynamic load rating

L10 = (C/P)3

Rearranging:

C = P × L101/3

C = 9.88 × 14401/3 ≈ 111.5 kN.

Step 4 — Select standard bearing

Standard bearing 6315 has a 75 mm bore and a dynamic load rating of approximately 112 kN in the worked data.

Dynamic check: 112 kN > 111.5 kN → required dynamic life is satisfied.

Step 5 — Static check

For the worked loading:

P0 = 0.6Fr + 0.5Fa

= 0.6(8) + 0.5(3) = 6.3 kN.

Static check: C0 = 72 kN > P0 = 6.3 kN → satisfactory.

Final answer: Select bearing No. 6315, single-row deep-groove ball bearing, 75 mm bore.

Source status: This is a worked design case, not an official university PYQ. Bearing catalogue ratings/factors must be checked against the permitted design data before an examination answer.

Problem 2 — Hydrodynamic journal bearing

Question basis: A 360° hydrodynamic bearing carries 3.2 kN at 1490 rpm. Journal diameter and bearing length are 50 mm each, radial clearance is 0.05 mm and lubricant viscosity is 25 cP. Find coefficient of friction, power loss, minimum oil-film thickness, oil flow and temperature rise.

Given
W = 3.2 kN = 3200 N
N = 1490 rpm
d = 50 mm
L = 50 mm
c = 0.05 mm
μ = 25 cP = 0.025 Pa·s
L/d = 1

Step 1 — Bearing pressure

p = WL × d

p = 320050 × 50 = 32002500 = 1.28 N/mm² = 1.28 MPa.

Step 2 — Sommerfeld number

S = μNp × 160 × dc2

Use p = 1.28 × 106 Pa.

dc = 500.05 = 1000.

S = [0.025 × 1490/(1.28 × 106)] × 10002 × 1/60 ≈ 0.121.

Step 3 — Read standard performance data

For L/d = 1 and S ≈ 0.121, the worked bearing-data solution uses approximately:

  • Coefficient of friction, f = 0.00644
  • Minimum-film ratio, hmin/c = 0.40
  • Oil flow, Q ≈ 0.403 L/min
Do not guess chart values. Read them from the permitted Raimondi–Boyd/design data or data supplied with the question.

Step 4 — Minimum oil-film thickness

hmin = 0.40 × 0.05 = 0.020 mm = 20 μm.

Step 5 — Power lost in friction

v = πdN60

Convert d = 50 mm = 0.05 m.

v = π × 0.05 × 1490/60 ≈ 3.90 m/s.

Pf = fWv

Pf = 0.00644 × 3200 × 3.90 ≈ 80.4 W.

Step 6 — Temperature rise

Using the stated heat-balance assumption and the supplied oil-flow/lubricant data gives ΔT ≈ 7.9°C.

Checks: S is dimensionless; hmin = 0.020 mm < radial clearance 0.05 mm; friction power has units of watts.

Final answers: f = 0.00644; Pf ≈ 80.4 W; hmin ≈ 0.020 mm; Q ≈ 0.403 L/min; ΔT ≈ 7.9°C.

Source status: This worked bearing case is included as numerical practice. The chart values must be checked against the permitted design data; it is not labelled as an official PYQ here.

Numerical checklist for Unit V

  1. Identify whether the bearing is sliding-contact or rolling-contact.
  2. For hydrodynamic bearings, calculate bearing pressure and Sommerfeld number with consistent units.
  3. Use L/d and Sommerfeld number to read permitted performance data.
  4. Calculate film thickness, friction power, oil flow and heat balance as required.
  5. For rolling bearings, determine equivalent dynamic load and required rating life.
  6. Select a standard bearing only after checking bore, dynamic rating and static capacity.
  7. Never invent catalogue factors, bearing numbers or chart values.

Common mistakes

  1. Using radial clearance as diametral clearance without checking the definition.
  2. Mixing mm and m in velocity/power calculations.
  3. Using the ball-bearing life exponent for a roller bearing without checking the bearing type.
  4. Ignoring axial load when the applicable e criterion requires it.
  5. Selecting a bearing by dynamic rating alone without checking bore and static capacity.
  6. Guessing Raimondi–Boyd chart values.
