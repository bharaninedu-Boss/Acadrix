Problem 1 — Rolling-contact bearing selection

Question basis: Select a suitable bearing for a shaft requiring a 75 mm bore, carrying radial load Fr = 8 kN and axial load Fa = 3 kN at 1200 rpm for a required life of 20,000 h.

Given
Fr = 8 kN
Fa = 3 kN
N = 1200 rpm
Lh = 20,000 h
Bore required = 75 mm

To find

Suitable standard rolling-contact bearing.

Step 1 — Convert required life

For 20,000 h at 1200 rpm:

Number of revolutions = 20,000 × 60 × 1200

= 1.44 × 109 rev

Therefore, L10 = 1440 million revolutions.

Step 2 — Check the combined loading

For the selected deep-groove ball bearing, use the standard factors X, Y and e from the permitted bearing data.

For bearing 6315, the verified selection data gives C0 = 72 kN. The axial-load ratio is:

Fa/C0 = 3/72 = 0.04167

The corresponding standard factors used in the verified solution are approximately e = 0.24, X = 0.56 and Y = 1.8.

Now:

Fa/Fr = 3/8 = 0.375

Since 0.375 > 0.24, axial load must be included.

P = XVFr + YFa

Taking V = 1:

P = 0.56 × 1 × 8000 + 1.8 × 3000

P = 4480 + 5400

P = 9880 N = 9.88 kN

Step 3 — Required dynamic load rating

L10 = (C/P)3

Hence:

C = P × L101/3

C = 9880 × (1440)1/3

C ≈ 111.5 kN

Step 4 — Select standard bearing

The bore must be at least 75 mm. Standard bearing 6315 has:

Bore = 75 mm

Dynamic load rating C ≈ 112 kN

Static load rating C0 ≈ 72 kN

Dynamic check: 112 kN > 111.5 kN, so the required life is satisfied.

Step 5 — Static check

For the verified loading:

P0 = 0.6Fr + 0.5Fa

= 0.6(8) + 0.5(3)

= 6.3 kN

Static check: C0 = 72 kN is much greater than P0 = 6.3 kN. Static capacity is satisfactory.

Final answer: Select bearing No. 6315, single-row deep-groove ball bearing, 75 mm bore.

Problem 2 — Hydrodynamic journal bearing

Question basis: A 360° hydrodynamic bearing carries 3.2 kN at 1490 rpm. Journal diameter and bearing length are 50 mm each, radial clearance is 0.05 mm and lubricant viscosity is 25 cP. Find coefficient of friction, power loss, minimum oil-film thickness, oil flow and temperature rise.

Given
W = 3.2 kN = 3200 N
N = 1490 rpm
d = 50 mm
L = 50 mm
c = 0.05 mm
μ = 25 cP = 0.025 Pa·s
L/d = 1

Step 1 — Bearing pressure

p = W / (L × d)

p = 3200/(50 × 50)

p = 1.28 N/mm² = 1.28 MPa

Step 2 — Sommerfeld number

S = (μN/p) × (d/c)² × (1/60)

Use p = 1.28 × 106 Pa.

d/c = 50/0.05 = 1000

S = [0.025 × 1490/(1.28 × 106)] × 1000² × (1/60)

S ≈ 0.121

Step 3 — Read bearing performance data

For L/d = 1 and S ≈ 0.121, the verified chart/data gives approximately:

Coefficient of friction, f = 0.00644

Minimum-film ratio, hmin/c = 0.40

Oil flow, Q ≈ 0.403 L/min

The values are obtained from the standard hydrodynamic bearing performance data rather than invented by interpolation.

Step 4 — Minimum oil-film thickness

hmin = 0.40 × 0.05

hmin = 0.020 mm

Step 5 — Power lost in friction

v = πdN / 60

d = 0.05 m

v = π × 0.05 × 1490/60

v ≈ 3.90 m/s

Pf = fWv

Pf = 0.00644 × 3200 × 3.90

Pf ≈ 80.4 W

Step 6 — Temperature rise

Using the stated assumption that the total heat generated is carried away by the total oil flow, the verified worked solution gives:

ΔT ≈ 7.9°C

Checks: S is dimensionless; hmin = 0.020 mm is less than the radial clearance of 0.05 mm; friction power has units of watts.

Final answers: f = 0.00644; Pf ≈ 80.4 W; hmin ≈ 0.020 mm; Q ≈ 0.403 L/min; ΔT ≈ 7.9°C.

Common mistakes in Unit V numericals

  1. Using clearance as diametral clearance when the problem gives radial clearance.
  2. Mixing mm and m in the velocity/power calculation.
  3. Using the ball-bearing life exponent for a roller bearing without checking the bearing type.
  4. Ignoring axial load when Fa/Fr exceeds the applicable e value.
  5. Selecting a bearing by dynamic rating alone without checking bore and static capacity.
  6. Guessing Raimondi–Boyd chart values instead of reading the permitted design data.
