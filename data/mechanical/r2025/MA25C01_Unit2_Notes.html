Unit II — Functions of Several Variables

MA25C01 Applied Calculus · R-2025 · B.E. Mechanical Engineering

Exam-friendly ACADRIX study notes aligned to the verified R-2025 syllabus topics.

Syllabus scope: Partial derivatives, chain rule, total derivative, maxima/minima of two variables, Lagrange multipliers and engineering applications.

1. Functions of Several Variables

A function of two variables assigns an output to each permitted pair (x,y), written as z=f(x,y). Its domain is the set of input pairs for which the expression is defined.

For example, if f(x,y)=x²+y², changing either x or y changes z.

2. Partial Derivatives

The partial derivative with respect to one variable measures the rate of change while the other independent variables are held constant.

NotationMeaning
fₓ or ∂f/∂xDifferentiate with respect to x, treating y as constant.
fᵧ or ∂f/∂yDifferentiate with respect to y, treating x as constant.

For f=x²y+3y², fₓ=2xy and fᵧ=x²+6y.

3. Higher-Order Partial Derivatives

Partial derivatives can be differentiated again. Examples are fₓₓ, fᵧᵧ, fₓᵧ and fᵧₓ. When the required continuity conditions hold, mixed partial derivatives are equal: fₓᵧ=fᵧₓ.

4. Chain Rule

If z=f(x,y) and x and y depend on a variable t, then the rate of change of z with respect to t is

dz/dt = (∂f/∂x)(dx/dt) + (∂f/∂y)(dy/dt)

For several intermediate variables, apply the same idea along every dependency path.

5. Total Derivative

The total derivative combines the effects of changes in all independent variables. For z=f(x,y) with independent changes dx and dy, the total differential is

dz = fₓ dx + fᵧ dy

This gives a first-order approximation of how z changes for small changes in x and y.

6. Maxima and Minima of Two Variables

For an interior stationary point of f(x,y), first solve

fₓ=0,   fᵧ=0

Let D=fₓₓfᵧᵧ-(fₓᵧ)² at the stationary point.

ConditionClassification
D>0 and fₓₓ>0Local minimum
D>0 and fₓₓ<0Local maximum
D<0Saddle point
D=0Test is inconclusive; use another method.

7. Lagrange Multipliers

To find extrema of f(x,y) subject to a constraint g(x,y)=c, introduce a multiplier λ and solve

∇f = λ∇g
g(x,y)=c

Equivalently, solve the three equations fₓ=λgₓ, fᵧ=λgᵧ and g=c.

8. Engineering Applications

Multivariable calculus is useful when a quantity depends on several design or operating variables. Examples include analysing how temperature changes with position, optimising dimensions for performance or cost, and studying a response surface from experimental data.

Exam workflow: write the function and constraint clearly → calculate partial derivatives → solve stationary/constraint equations → classify candidates → state the mathematical and engineering interpretation.

Quick Revision

  • Partial derivative: hold other independent variables constant.
  • Chain rule: add the contribution from every dependency path.
  • Total differential: dz=fₓdx+fᵧdy.
  • Two-variable second-derivative test: D=fₓₓfᵧᵧ-(fₓᵧ)².
  • Lagrange multiplier condition: ∇f=λ∇g.
