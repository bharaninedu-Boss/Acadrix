1. Antiderivatives and Indefinite Integrals

An antiderivative of f(x) is a function F(x) such that F'(x)=f(x). The indefinite integral is written as

∫f(x) dx = F(x)+C

The constant C is required because every constant disappears when differentiated.

2. Definite Integrals

A definite integral gives an accumulated signed quantity between two limits:

∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = F(b)−F(a)

The value is signed area when interpreted geometrically. Regions below the x-axis contribute negatively.

3. Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

The Fundamental Theorem connects differentiation and integration. If f is continuous, then

d/dx [∫ₐˣ f(t)dt] = f(x)

It also provides the evaluation rule ∫ₐᵇf(x)dx=F(b)−F(a) when F'=f.

4. Net Change Theorem

If a quantity changes at rate F'(t), then its total change from a to b is

F(b)−F(a)=∫ₐᵇF'(t)dt

For example, integrating velocity over time gives displacement; integrating a flow rate over time gives accumulated volume.

5. Improper Integrals

An integral is improper when an interval is infinite or the integrand becomes unbounded at an endpoint or inside the interval. Treat it as a limit.

∫ₐ∞ f(x)dx = lim(b→∞) ∫ₐᵇ f(x)dx

If the relevant limit exists and is finite, the improper integral converges. Otherwise it diverges.

Example: p-integral

For p>1, ∫₁∞ 1/xᵖ dx converges; for p≤1, it diverges.

6. Area of a Region

For a region between two curves, identify the top and bottom functions carefully:

Area = ∫ₐᵇ [upper function − lower function] dx

If horizontal slices are simpler, integrate with respect to y instead. Always determine the intersection points before setting limits.

7. Arc Length

For a differentiable curve y=f(x) from x=a to x=b, the arc length is

L = ∫ₐᵇ √(1+[f'(x)]²) dx

The formula comes from adding infinitesimal straight-line lengths.

8. Surface Area of Revolution

When y=f(x) is revolved about the x-axis, the surface area is

S = 2π∫ₐᵇ |y|√(1+[y']²) dx

For rotation about the y-axis using x as a function of y, the analogous form is S=2π∫|x|√(1+[dx/dy]²)dy.

9. Exam Problem Strategy

  1. Identify whether the problem asks for an antiderivative, accumulation, area, arc length or surface area.
  2. Sketch the region when geometry is involved.
  3. Find intersections or limits of integration.
  4. Select the correct variable and formula.
  5. Evaluate carefully and include units when the problem has physical meaning.
