Unit III — Integral Calculus

MA25C01 · R-2025 · Detailed exam-oriented notes

1 Mark3 Marks12 Marks

Syllabus: Fundamental Theorem of Calculus; indefinite integrals and Net Change Theorem; improper integrals; arc length; area of a region; surface area of revolution.

Study approach: Understand the meaning first → remember the formula → practise setup → solve complete problems.

1. Antiderivatives and Indefinite Integrals

1.1 Meaning of an Antiderivative

If differentiation of F(x) gives f(x), then F is an antiderivative of f. In symbols, F′(x)=f(x). Therefore integration reverses differentiation.

Example: Since d(x³)/dx=3x², an antiderivative of 3x² is x³. Thus ∫3x²dx=x³+C.

1.2 Indefinite Integral

The indefinite integral represents the family of all antiderivatives:

∫f(x)dx=F(x)+C

The constant C is included because all functions differing only by a constant have the same derivative.

2. Definite Integrals

2.1 Meaning

A definite integral ∫ₐᵇf(x)dx gives the net accumulated value over [a,b]. Geometrically, it represents signed area: regions above the x-axis contribute positively and regions below it negatively.

2.2 Fundamental Evaluation Rule

If F′(x)=f(x), then

∫ₐᵇf(x)dx=F(b)−F(a)
Example: ∫₀²x dx=[x²/2]₀²=2. The result is also the area of the triangle under y=x from x=0 to 2.

3. Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

3.1 Derivative Form

If f is continuous, the accumulation function G(x)=∫ₐˣf(t)dt satisfies G′(x)=f(x).

3.2 Evaluation Form

If F is an antiderivative of f, then ∫ₐᵇf(x)dx=F(b)−F(a).

Exam memory: The first part connects integration to differentiation. The second part gives a practical way to evaluate a definite integral using an antiderivative.

4. Net Change Theorem

If a quantity Q changes at rate Q′(t), then its total change from a to b is the integral of the rate:

Q(b)−Q(a)=∫ₐᵇQ′(t)dt

This is useful for physical and engineering quantities. For example, integrating velocity over time gives displacement; integrating a flow rate over time gives accumulated volume.

Exam workflow: identify the rate → identify the time interval → integrate the rate → apply initial value if an absolute quantity is requested → state the physical meaning.

5. Improper Integrals

5.1 Infinite Interval

When the interval extends to infinity, replace infinity by a variable limit:

∫ₐ∞f(x)dx=lim(b→∞)∫ₐᵇf(x)dx

If this limit is finite, the integral converges. If it does not exist or is infinite, the integral diverges.

5.2 Unbounded Integrand

If f becomes unbounded at an endpoint, define the integral using a one-sided limit. For an interior singularity, split the integral at the problematic point and examine each part separately.

5.3 p-Integral

∫₁∞1/xᵖ dx converges when p>1 and diverges when p≤1.
Example: ∫₁∞1/x² dx=lim(b→∞)[−1/x]₁ᵇ=1, so it converges.

6. Area of a Region

6.1 Between a Curve and the x-axis

If f(x)≥0 on [a,b], area is A=∫ₐᵇf(x)dx. If the curve crosses the axis, split the interval or use absolute area where the question asks for geometric area.

6.2 Between Two Curves

For vertical slices:

A=∫ₐᵇ[upper curve−lower curve]dx
Step 1: Find intersection points.
Step 2: Determine which curve is upper.
Step 3: Write the correct limits.
Step 4: Integrate upper minus lower.
Step 5: Check that the result is non-negative for geometric area.

6.3 Choosing dx or dy

Use dx when vertical slices make the region simple. Use dy when horizontal slices give simpler boundaries.

7. Arc Length

7.1 Formula

For y=f(x) on [a,b]:

L=∫ₐᵇ√(1+[f′(x)]²)dx

For x=g(y), use L=∫√(1+[g′(y)]²)dy.

7.2 Exam Method

Differentiate the curve.
Square the derivative and form 1+(y′)².
Substitute into the arc-length formula.
Apply the correct limits.
Simplify and evaluate.

8. Surface Area of Revolution

8.1 About the x-axis

When y=f(x) is revolved about the x-axis:

S=2π∫ₐᵇ|y|√(1+[y′]²)dx

The expression comes from S=∫2π(radius)ds, where ds=√(1+(y′)²)dx.

8.2 What Students Commonly Miss

  • Using the wrong radius.
  • Forgetting the derivative inside the arc-length factor.
  • Using incorrect limits.
  • Confusing surface area with ordinary planar area.

12. 12-Mark Answer Framework

1. Write the given data and identify exactly what must be found.
2. State the governing formula/theorem.
3. Find intersections, limits or derivatives required for the setup.
4. Write the integral clearly before evaluating it.
5. Show important algebraic/substitution steps.
6. Give the final exact/approximate answer and units where applicable.
7. Add a physical or geometric interpretation when relevant.

14. Exam Tips

  • For 1-mark questions, memorise definitions, standard integrals, conditions and formulas.
  • For 3-mark questions, write a definition followed by 2–4 clear supporting points or a short calculation.
  • For 12-mark questions, marks are easier to secure when the integral setup and intermediate steps are visible.
  • Always check limits before evaluating area, arc-length or surface-area problems.
  • For improper integrals, explicitly write the limiting process; do not simply write “convergent”.
