Unit IV — Multiple Integrals

MA25C01 Applied Calculus · R-2025 · B.E. Mechanical Engineering

Exam-friendly ACADRIX study notes aligned to the verified R-2025 syllabus topics.

Syllabus scope: Iterated integrals/Fubini, double integrals, change of order, Cartesian and polar coordinates, triple integrals, and Cartesian/cylindrical/spherical coordinate transformations.

1. Iterated Integrals

A multiple integral can often be evaluated as a sequence of ordinary integrations. For a suitable function over a rectangular region, Fubini's theorem allows the double integral to be written as an iterated integral:

∫∫R f(x,y) dA = ∫ab ∫cd f(x,y) dy dx

The order of integration may be reversed when the region and integrand satisfy the required conditions.

2. Double Integrals

The double integral of f(x,y) over a region R represents the accumulated value of the function over an area. If f≥0, it can be interpreted geometrically as volume under the surface z=f(x,y).

For a rectangular region a≤x≤b, c≤y≤d, integrate with respect to one variable first and then the other.

3. Changing the Order of Integration

When reversing the order, do not simply interchange the differential symbols. First identify the region of integration, sketch its boundary when needed, and then rewrite the limits in the new order.

Exam method: 1) Read the original limits. 2) Sketch or describe the region. 3) Find the new inner-variable limits. 4) Find the new outer-variable limits. 5) Rewrite and evaluate.

4. Cartesian Coordinates

In Cartesian coordinates an area element is dA=dx dy (or dy dx). A volume element is dV=dx dy dz in an appropriate order.

5. Polar Coordinates

Polar coordinates are useful for circular and radial regions. The transformation is

x=r cos θ
y=r sin θ
dA=r dr dθ

For a circle of radius a centred at the origin, the typical limits are 0≤r≤a and 0≤θ≤2π.

6. Triple Integrals

A triple integral accumulates a function over a three-dimensional volume. It can be evaluated as an iterated integral by choosing a convenient integration order and describing the solid correctly.

∭V f(x,y,z) dV

For f=1, the triple integral gives the volume of the solid V.

7. Cylindrical Coordinates

Cylindrical coordinates extend polar coordinates into three dimensions:

x=r cos θ
y=r sin θ
z=z
dV=r dr dθ dz

They are especially convenient for cylinders and solids with circular symmetry about the z-axis.

8. Spherical Coordinates

Spherical coordinates describe a point using radial distance and two angles. With ρ as radial distance, θ as the azimuthal angle and φ as the polar angle measured from the positive z-axis:

x=ρ sinφ cosθ
y=ρ sinφ sinθ
z=ρ cosφ
dV=ρ² sinφ dρ dφ dθ

These coordinates are useful for spheres and other solids with spherical symmetry.

Quick Revision

  • Fubini/iterated integration converts a multiple integral into successive integrations when applicable.
  • Changing order requires identifying the region and rewriting limits.
  • Polar: x=r cosθ, y=r sinθ, dA=r dr dθ.
  • Cylindrical: dV=r dr dθ dz.
  • Spherical: dV=ρ²sinφ dρ dφ dθ.
Exam workflow: identify the geometry → choose coordinates → sketch the region/solid → determine correct limits → include the Jacobian → integrate carefully → check units and physical meaning.
