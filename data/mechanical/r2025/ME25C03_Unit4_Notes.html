1. What is a Machine Element?

A machine element is an individual component or standardised part of a machine that performs a specific mechanical function. Examples include shafts, gears, bearings, bolts, keys and couplings.

Why machine elements matter

  • They transmit motion and power.
  • They support rotating or stationary loads.
  • They connect components and maintain alignment.
  • They allow assembly, maintenance and replacement.
  • Correct selection improves reliability, safety and service life.

2. Gears

Gears are toothed machine elements used to transmit rotary motion and torque between shafts with a controlled speed ratio. Unlike belt drives, correctly meshing gears provide positive transmission without slip.

Basic gear train

Driver gear ⚙️ → ⚙️ Driven gear

For ideal simple gears: ω₁T₁ ≈ ω₂T₂ and N₁/N₂=T₂/T₁.

Important terms

  • Pitch circle: imaginary reference circle used in gear geometry.
  • Pitch diameter: diameter of the pitch circle.
  • Module: pitch diameter divided by number of teeth, m=d/Z.
  • Pressure angle: angle associated with the common normal at tooth contact.
  • Gear ratio: ratio relating the rotational speeds and tooth numbers of mating gears.

Common gear types

TypeMain featureTypical use
SpurStraight teeth parallel to shaft axisSimple parallel-shaft transmission
HelicalTeeth inclined to shaft axisSmoother, quieter transmission
BevelTeeth on conical surfacesIntersecting shafts
WormWorm and worm wheel arrangementLarge speed reduction applications

3. Bearings

A bearing supports a shaft or moving member and reduces friction while allowing controlled relative motion.

Rolling-contact bearings

These use rolling elements such as balls or rollers between races. They generally provide low starting friction and are available in many standard forms.

  • Ball bearing — balls are rolling elements.
  • Roller bearing — rollers carry the load.
  • Cylindrical, tapered and spherical roller arrangements serve different loading and alignment needs.

Sliding/contact bearings

Plain bearings support motion through sliding contact, often with a lubricating film between surfaces.

Choice factorWhy it matters
Radial loadLoad acting approximately perpendicular to shaft axis.
Axial/thrust loadLoad acting along shaft axis.
SpeedAffects friction, heat generation and lubrication requirements.
EnvironmentDust, moisture, temperature and chemicals affect bearing selection.

4. Shafts

A shaft is a rotating machine member used primarily to transmit torque and power and often to support gears, pulleys, sprockets or other components.

Typical shaft loading

  • Torsion due to transmitted torque.
  • Bending due to transverse forces and component weights.
  • Axial load in some arrangements.
  • Combined loading may occur in practical machines.
Power-torque-speed relation:
P=Tω
For rotational speed N in rpm, a commonly used engineering form is P=2πNT/60.

Good shaft-design considerations

  • Required torque and power.
  • Strength and allowable stress.
  • Stiffness and deflection.
  • Fatigue under fluctuating loads.
  • Stress concentration at keyways, shoulders and grooves.
  • Manufacturing, assembly and maintenance requirements.

5. Fasteners

Fasteners join machine components. They may be removable or permanent depending on the joint and application.

Bolts, nuts and screws

  • A bolt normally works with a nut to clamp parts.
  • A nut provides the threaded mating element for a bolt or stud.
  • A screw is generally tightened into a threaded hole or mating component.

Threaded joint considerations

  • Clamping/preload.
  • External load and joint separation.
  • Thread strength.
  • Vibration and loosening.
  • Corrosion and environment.
  • Correct tightening procedure.

6. Couplings

A coupling connects two shafts so that torque can be transmitted from one shaft to another. It may also accommodate limited misalignment depending on its design.

TypePurpose/feature
Rigid couplingUsed where shafts are well aligned and relative movement is not required.
Flexible couplingTransmits torque while accommodating limited misalignment and/or damping disturbances.

Coupling selection

  • Transmitted torque and power.
  • Speed.
  • Shaft size.
  • Expected angular, parallel or axial misalignment.
  • Shock/vibration.
  • Maintenance and operating environment.

7. Selection of Machine Components

Component selection is an engineering decision, not simply a choice based on size. The component must satisfy functional, strength, reliability, manufacturing, safety and economic requirements.

Step 1: Define function and operating conditions.
Step 2: Determine loads, speed, power, temperature and environment.
Step 3: Identify failure modes and required life.
Step 4: Select suitable type, material and standard size.
Step 5: Check strength, stiffness, wear, heat and compatibility.
Step 6: Check manufacturability, maintenance, safety and cost.

Example: choosing a bearing

Given: A shaft carries a known radial load and rotates continuously at a specified speed.
Approach: identify radial/axial load → determine speed and required life → consider environment/lubrication → select bearing type → verify load capacity and dimensions → confirm standard availability and safety.

8. Quality Control in Mechanical Engineering

Quality control is the operational process of checking and controlling production so that components meet specified requirements.

  • Use drawings, specifications and standards as acceptance criteria.
  • Inspect incoming material and critical dimensions.
  • Use suitable measuring instruments and calibrated equipment.
  • Monitor manufacturing processes rather than relying only on final inspection.
  • Record defects and identify root causes.
  • Apply corrective action and verify improvement.

Quality assurance vs quality control

Quality assuranceQuality control
Process-oriented prevention and system-level confidence.Product/process inspection and detection of nonconformity.

9. Safety in Mechanical Engineering

Engineering safety aims to prevent injury, equipment damage, environmental harm and operational failure.

Basic safety principles

  • Identify hazards before operating equipment.
  • Use guards on moving parts.
  • Use appropriate personal protective equipment.
  • Follow safe operating procedures and manufacturer instructions.
  • Isolate energy before maintenance where required.
  • Keep work areas clean and free from trip/slip hazards.
  • Never bypass safety interlocks without an authorised safe procedure.
  • Report defects, near misses and unsafe conditions.

16. Exam Tips

  • Draw simple labelled sketches for gears, bearings, shafts and couplings in long answers.
  • For component-selection questions, always mention load, speed, environment, life, safety and cost.
  • Do not confuse a shaft with an axle: a shaft generally transmits torque and rotates; an axle primarily supports rotating elements in many applications.
  • For safety answers, combine hazard identification, guarding, PPE, procedures and energy isolation.
  • For quality answers, distinguish prevention/process control from inspection/detection.
