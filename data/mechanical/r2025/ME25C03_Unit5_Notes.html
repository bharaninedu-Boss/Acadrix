1. Manufacturing — Basic Idea

Manufacturing converts raw material into useful products with a required shape, size, surface condition, properties and functional performance. A suitable process is selected according to material, geometry, production quantity, dimensional accuracy, surface finish, cost and required properties.

Typical manufacturing flow

Raw material → Process → Inspection → Finished component

2. Casting

Casting is a manufacturing process in which molten material is poured into a mould cavity and allowed to solidify to obtain the required shape. It is useful for complex shapes and a wide range of component sizes.

Basic casting sequence

Pattern preparation → mould preparation → melting → pouring → solidification → shakeout → cleaning/fettling → inspection.

Important casting terms

  • Pattern: model used to form the mould cavity.
  • Mould: cavity/form into which molten material is introduced.
  • Core: insert used to create an internal cavity or passage.
  • Riser: reservoir that helps compensate for shrinkage during solidification.
  • Gating system: passages that guide molten metal into the mould cavity.

Advantages

  • Complex external and internal shapes can be produced.
  • Large components can be manufactured.
  • Many metals and alloys can be cast.
  • Near-net-shape production can reduce machining requirements.

Common defects

DefectBasic cause/description
BlowholeGas-related cavity or void in the casting.
Shrinkage cavityInsufficient feeding during solidification.
MisrunMolten material solidifies before completely filling the mould.
Cold shutTwo flow fronts meet without proper fusion.

3. Forming Processes

Metal forming changes the shape of a workpiece mainly through plastic deformation. The material is shaped without removing a large amount of material as chips.

ProcessSimple explanationExample
RollingMaterial passes between rotating rolls.Plates, sheets, strips.
ForgingCompressive forces shape the workpiece.High-strength components such as connecting-rod blanks.
ExtrusionMaterial is forced through a die opening.Profiles and rods.
DrawingMaterial is pulled through a die.Wires and tubes.

Why forming is important

  • Good material utilisation.
  • High production rates for suitable processes.
  • Mechanical properties can improve through controlled deformation and processing.
  • Suitable for producing repeatable shapes.

4. Machining

Machining removes unwanted material from a workpiece to obtain the required dimensions, shape and surface finish. Material is commonly removed as chips.

Common machining processes

ProcessPrimary actionTypical result
TurningRotating workpiece with cutting tool movement.Cylindrical surfaces, steps, grooves.
MillingRotating multi-point cutter removes material.Slots, flats, profiles.
DrillingRotating drill produces a hole.Circular holes.
GrindingAbrasive grains remove small amounts of material.High accuracy and surface finish.

Basic cutting terminology

  • Cutting speed: relative speed at the cutting zone.
  • Feed: movement of the tool/workpiece associated with generating the next portion of cut.
  • Depth of cut: thickness of material removed in a cutting pass, according to the operation definition.
  • Tool life: useful cutting time before the tool reaches a specified wear criterion.

Simple turning speed relation

V = πDN/60 when D is in metres and N is in revolutions per second.
For D in mm and N in rpm: V = πDN/(1000×60) m/s.

5. Joining Processes

Joining combines two or more components into an assembly. Joining may be permanent or designed to permit disassembly.

Welding

Welding produces a joint by creating a metallurgical bond, commonly using heat, pressure or a combination. Filler material may or may not be used depending on the process.

Other joining methods

  • Brazing: a filler metal is used at a temperature above the applicable soldering range but below the melting temperature of the base materials.
  • Soldering: joins materials using a lower-temperature filler metal.
  • Mechanical joining: bolts, screws, rivets and related methods can connect components.
MethodKey feature
WeldingStrong permanent joint with local thermal/mechanical action.
BrazingBase materials generally remain solid while filler forms the joint.
SolderingLower-temperature filler joining process.
BoltingRemovable mechanical joint.

6. CNC — Computer Numerical Control

CNC machines use programmed numerical instructions to control machine-tool movements and operations. They improve repeatability and are particularly useful for complex or repeated production.

Basic CNC workflow

Part drawing/model → process planning → tool selection → coordinate/tool-path planning → program preparation → setup/work offset → dry run/simulation → machining → inspection.

Benefits of CNC

  • High repeatability.
  • Ability to machine complex profiles.
  • Reduced manual intervention during cutting.
  • Consistent production when setup and program are controlled.
  • Integration with CAD/CAM systems.

Important caution

A CNC machine follows programmed instructions; an incorrect coordinate, tool offset, feed, speed or tool selection can produce scrap or damage. Simulation, dry runs and correct setup are therefore essential.

7. Additive Manufacturing

Additive manufacturing builds a component by adding material layer by layer from digital geometry, rather than primarily removing material from a solid block.

General workflow

3D CAD model → file preparation/slicing → machine setup → layer-by-layer build → support removal/post-processing → inspection.

Advantages

  • Complex geometries can be produced.
  • Rapid prototyping is possible.
  • Material is added where needed, potentially reducing waste for suitable designs.
  • Mass customisation is practical for some applications.

Limitations

  • Surface finish may require post-processing.
  • Build speed can be slower for some large components.
  • Material/process availability depends on the technology.
  • Dimensional accuracy and mechanical properties can depend on process parameters and build orientation.

8. Smart Manufacturing

Smart manufacturing uses connected machines, sensors, data, automation and digital technologies to improve manufacturing decisions and performance.

Key technologies

  • Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT).
  • Industrial sensors and data acquisition.
  • Automation and robotics.
  • Data analytics and artificial intelligence.
  • Digital models/digital twins.
  • Cloud/edge computing and connected production systems.
Smart manufacturing concept

Machines + Sensors → Data → Analytics → Decision → Control/Optimisation

Potential benefits

  • Real-time monitoring.
  • Predictive maintenance.
  • Improved quality tracking.
  • Better production planning.
  • Energy and resource optimisation.
  • Faster response to process deviations.

9. Process Selection — How to Choose

1. Material: Identify metal, polymer, ceramic or composite and its relevant properties.
2. Geometry: Consider size, complexity, internal cavities and accessibility.
3. Accuracy: Define dimensional tolerance and surface-finish requirements.
4. Quantity: Consider prototype, batch or mass production.
5. Properties: Consider strength, hardness, microstructure and heat-treatment needs.
6. Economics: Compare tooling, cycle time, material utilisation, labour and post-processing.
7. Quality and safety: Check inspection needs, process capability, operator safety and environmental effects.

14. Numerical / Application Framework

For a machining-speed problem: Given tool/workpiece diameter and rotational speed → select the correct speed relation → maintain consistent units → substitute → calculate cutting speed → state units and practical interpretation.
For process-selection questions: Material → geometry → quantity → tolerance/surface finish → required properties → equipment/tooling → production time → cost → quality and safety → final process choice.

16. Exam Tips

  • For process questions, write the principle first, then steps, advantages, limitations and applications.
  • Use a simple flow diagram for casting, CNC and additive-manufacturing long answers.
  • Do not confuse additive manufacturing with machining: one mainly adds material layer by layer, while machining removes material.
  • In numerical problems, write units at every important step and check whether diameter and speed units match the formula.
  • For smart manufacturing, connect sensors to data, analytics, decisions and control rather than listing technologies without explaining their role.
