Unit I — Differential Calculus

MA25C01 · R-2025 · Exam-oriented notes

Syllabus: functions, graphs, new functions, limits, continuity, limits at infinity, derivatives, maxima/minima, mean value theorem and effect of derivatives on graph shape.

1. Core Notes

Functions and Graphs

A function assigns each permitted input exactly one output. Domain is the set of allowed inputs; range is the set of outputs. For transformations, f(x)+k shifts up, f(x-h) shifts right, and -f(x) reflects in the x-axis.

Limits and Continuity

A limit describes the value approached by a function. For a two-sided finite limit, left- and right-hand limits must agree. A function is continuous at a when f(a) exists, the limit exists, and the limit equals f(a).

Standard result: lim(x→0) sin x/x=1 in radians.

Derivative

The derivative is the instantaneous rate of change and geometrically the tangent slope.

Maxima, Minima and Graph Shape

Critical points occur where f'=0 or the derivative does not exist. If f''(a)>0, a stationary point is a local minimum; if f''(a)<0, it is a local maximum. Also, f'>0 means increasing and f'<0 decreasing; f''>0 means concave up and f''<0 concave down.

Mean Value Theorem

If f is continuous on [a,b] and differentiable on (a,b), then at least one c exists such that f'(c)=[f(b)-f(a)]/(b-a).

4. 12-Mark Preparation

  • Analyse a function completely: domain, critical points, intervals of increase/decrease, concavity and local extrema, then sketch the graph.
  • Solve a maxima/minima application by forming the required function, differentiating, finding critical points and interpreting the optimum.
  • Evaluate a limit involving algebraic simplification or standard limits and justify continuity where applicable.
  • Apply the Mean Value Theorem to a given function and interval, verify its conditions and find the required value(s) of c.
