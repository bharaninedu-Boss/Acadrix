1. Core Theory

1.1 Functions

A function f:A→B assigns exactly one output to each allowed input. The domain contains permitted inputs; the range contains actual outputs.

Domain checklist: denominator cannot be zero; even-root radicand must be non-negative for real-valued functions; logarithm argument must be positive.

1.2 Graphs of Functions

The graph of y=f(x) represents all ordered pairs (x,f(x)). Intercepts, symmetry, domain restrictions and end behaviour help in sketching.

1.3 Limits

lim(x→a)f(x)=L means f(x) approaches L as x approaches a. A two-sided finite limit exists when the left-hand and right-hand limits are equal.

Useful approach: direct substitution → factorisation/cancellation → rationalisation → standard limits.

Important standard limits include lim(x→0) sin x/x=1, lim(x→0)(1−cos x)/x²=1/2, and lim(x→0)(eˣ−1)/x=1, with angles in radians.

1.4 Continuity

A function is continuous at x=a when: (1) f(a) exists, (2) lim(x→a)f(x) exists, and (3) the limit equals f(a).

1.5 Limits at Infinity

For rational functions, compare the highest powers. Equal degrees give the ratio of leading coefficients; a lower numerator degree gives limit 0. Other cases may require division or dominant-term analysis.

1.6 Derivative

The derivative is the instantaneous rate of change and the slope of the tangent to a curve. From first principles, f′(x)=lim(h→0)[f(x+h)−f(x)]/h.

Use the product rule (uv)′=u′v+uv′, quotient rule (u/v)′=(vu′−uv′)/v², and chain rule dF(g(x))/dx=F′(g(x))g′(x).

1.7 Maxima and Minima

A critical point occurs where f′(x)=0 or f′ does not exist, provided the point belongs to the domain. For a stationary point, the second-derivative test gives: f′′(a)>0 local minimum, f′′(a)<0 local maximum, and f′′(a)=0 inconclusive.

1.8 Mean Value Theorem

If f is continuous on [a,b] and differentiable on (a,b), at least one c∈(a,b) satisfies f′(c)=[f(b)−f(a)]/(b−a).

  • Define a function.
  • Define domain and range.
  • What is a graph of a function?
  • State the transformation represented by f(x-h).
  • State lim(x→0)sin x/x.
  • State lim(x→0)(1−cos x)/x².
  • Define continuity at a point.
  • Define derivative.
  • Write the derivative of xⁿ.
  • Write the derivatives of sin x, cos x, eˣ and ln x.
  • State the product rule.
  • State the quotient rule.
  • State the chain rule.
  • What is a critical point?
  • State the second-derivative test.
  • State the conditions of MVT.
  • What does f′>0 indicate?
  • What does f′′<0 indicate?

3. 3-Mark Preparation

  • Explain domain and range with a suitable example.
  • Explain common transformations of the graph of a function.
  • Explain the concept of a limit and left/right-hand limits.
  • Explain the three conditions for continuity at a point.
  • Evaluate a simple standard limit with necessary steps.
  • Explain the meaning of derivative geometrically and physically.
  • Differentiate a product or quotient of functions.
  • Differentiate a composite function using the chain rule.
  • Explain critical points and the second-derivative test.
  • State and explain the Mean Value Theorem.
  • Explain increasing/decreasing intervals using the first derivative.
  • Explain concavity using the second derivative.

4. 12-Mark Preparation

  • Study a complete function: determine domain, intercepts/end behaviour, critical points, increasing/decreasing intervals, concavity, local extrema and a suitable sketch.
  • Solve a detailed maxima/minima engineering application by defining variables, forming the objective function, differentiating, testing critical points and interpreting the optimum.
  • Evaluate a non-trivial limit using factorisation, rationalisation or standard-limit methods and discuss continuity where required.
  • Apply the Mean Value Theorem: verify continuity and differentiability, substitute endpoint values, solve for c and state the conclusion.
  • Use first and second derivatives to analyse the complete graph behaviour of a given function.

5. 12-Mark Answer Framework

Step 1: Write the given function and conditions.
Step 2: State the relevant definition/formula.
Step 3: Show differentiation or limit manipulation clearly.
Step 4: Solve critical points or required values.
Step 5: Classify/interpret the result.
Step 6: Draw a labelled sketch when the question requires graph analysis.
