Example 1 — Overhang Crank: Diameter Using Maximum Shear Stress Theory

Problem: A solid circular overhang crank is subjected to a load of 1 kN. The yield strength of the material is 400 N/mm² and the factor of safety is 2. Determine the diameter of the crank shaft. The supplied solution uses the given geometry to obtain the bending and torsional moments.

Step 1 — Given
P = 1 kN = 1000 N
Syt = 400 N/mm²
FOS = 2
Bending-arm distance = 500 mm
Torsional-arm distance = 50 + 100 + 25 = 175 mm
Step 2 — Allowable shear stress
τallow = Syt2 × FOS = 4002 × 2 = 100 N/mm²
Step 3 — Bending moment
M = P × 500 = 1000 × 500 = 500,000 N·mm = 500 × 103 N·mm
Step 4 — Torsional moment
T = P(50 + 100 + 25) = 1000(175) = 175,000 N·mm = 175 × 103 N·mm
Step 5 — Stresses in a solid circular section
σb = 32Mπd³     τ = 16Tπd³
Step 6 — Maximum shear stress theory
τmax = √[(σb/2)² + τ²] ≤ τallow
100 = √{[32(500 × 103)2πd³]2 + [16(175 × 103)πd³]2}
Step 7 — Substitute and simplify completely
100² = [16(500 × 103)]²π²d6 + [16(175 × 103)]²π²d6
10,000π²d6 = 256[(500,000)² + (175,000)²]
d6 = 256[(500,000)² + (175,000)²]10,000π²
d6 ≈ 729,000,000  ⇒  d ≈ 30 mm

⭐ Final Answer: d ≈ 30 mm

Source basis: supplied “Machine Design Solutions: Crank Shaft and Plate Thickness” material identifies this as Q11(a) and gives the same final diameter.

Example 2 — Plate Thickness for Infinite Fatigue Life

Problem: A 45C8 steel plate with Sut = 630 N/mm² is subjected to a completely reversed axial load of ±50 kN. Size factor = 0.85, reliability = 90%, notch sensitivity = 0.8 and FOS = 2. From the supplied problem figure: D = 100 mm, d = 50 mm and r = 5 mm. Determine the required plate thickness.

Step 1 — Given
Sut = 630 N/mm²
P = ±50 kN = ±50,000 N
Kb = 0.85
Reliability = 90%
q = 0.8
FOS = 2
D/d = 100/50 = 2
r/d = 5/50 = 0.1
Step 2 — Uncorrected endurance limit
Se′ = 0.5Sut = 0.5(630) = 315 N/mm²
Step 3 — Surface finish factor

For the specified machined-steel condition, the supplied solution uses Ka = 0.76.

Ka = 0.76
Step 4 — Reliability factor

For 90% reliability, the supplied solution uses Kc = 0.897.

Kc = 0.897
Step 5 — Theoretical stress concentration factor

Using D/d = 2 and r/d = 0.1, the supplied design chart gives Kt = 2.27.

Kt = 2.27
Step 6 — Fatigue stress concentration factor
Kf = 1 + q(Kt − 1)
Kf = 1 + 0.8(2.27 − 1) = 1 + 0.8(1.27) = 1 + 1.016 = 2.016
Kd = 1Kf = 12.016 = 0.496
Step 7 — Corrected endurance limit
Se = KaKbKcKdSe′
Se = (0.76)(0.85)(0.897)(0.496)(315)
0.76 × 0.85 = 0.646
0.646 × 0.897 = 0.579462
0.579462 × 0.496 = 0.287413
0.287413 × 315 = 90.54 N/mm²
Step 8 — Endurance limit for axial loading
(Se)a = 0.8Se = 0.8(90.54) = 72.43 N/mm²
Step 9 — Permissible alternating stress
σa = (Se)aFOS = 72.432 = 36.215 N/mm²
Step 10 — Plate thickness
σa = PA = 50,00050t
36.215 = 50,00050t
36.215(50t) = 50,000
180?  →  36.215 × 50 = 1,810.75
1,810.75t = 50,000
t = 50,0001,810.75 = 27.61 mm

⭐ Final Answer: Required plate thickness t ≈ 27.61 mm.

Source basis: supplied Q11(b) solution material gives the same intermediate factors and final thickness.

Example 3 — Principal Stresses

Given: σx = 80 MPa, σy = 20 MPa, τxy = 30 MPa.

Step 1 — Mean normal stress
σavg = σx + σy2 = 80 + 202 = 50 MPa
Step 2 — Radius of Mohr's circle
R = √{[(σx − σy)/2]² + τxy²}
R = √{[(80 − 20)/2]² + 30²} = √(30² + 30²) = √1800 = 42.43 MPa
Step 3 — Principal stresses
σ1 = 50 + 42.43 = 92.43 MPa
σ2 = 50 − 42.43 = 7.57 MPa
Step 4 — Maximum shear stress
τmax = σ1 − σ22 = 92.43 − 7.572 = 42.43 MPa

Answer: σ1 = 92.43 MPa, σ2 = 7.57 MPa, τmax = 42.43 MPa.

Example 4 — Fluctuating Stress

Given: σmax = 180 MPa and σmin = −60 MPa.

Step 1 — Mean stress
σm = σmax + σmin2 = 180 + (−60)2 = 1202 = 60 MPa
Step 2 — Alternating stress
σa = σmax − σmin2 = 180 − (−60)2 = 2402 = 120 MPa
Step 3 — Stress ratio
R = σminσmax = −60180 = −0.333

Answer: σm = 60 MPa, σa = 120 MPa, R = −0.333.
