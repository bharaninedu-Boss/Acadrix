Example 1 — Overhang Crank: Combined Bending and Torsion

Given: P = 1 kN, vertical arm = 500 mm, eccentric horizontal distance = 175 mm, Syt = 400 MPa, FOS = 2. Maximum shear stress theory.

Load P ↓ │ │ 500 mm │ ────────┘──── 175 mm ─── critical section

Step 1: Allowable shear stress

τallow = Syt / (2 × FOS) = 400 / 4 = 100 MPa

Step 2: Bending moment

Mb = P × 500 = 1000 × 500 = 500 × 10³ Nmm

Step 3: Torque

T = P × 175 = 1000 × 175 = 175 × 10³ Nmm

For a solid circular section:

σb = 32Mb/(πd³)     τt = 16T/(πd³)

For maximum shear stress theory:

τmax = √[(σb/2)² + τt²] = 100 MPa

Substitution gives:

d ≈ 30 mm
Final answer: Adopt d = 30 mm.

Exam check: both bending and torsion must be included. Using only T gives an unsafe design.

Example 2 — Completely Reversed Axial Fatigue Load

Given: Plate material 45C8, Sut = 630 MPa, completely reversed axial force = ±50 kN, reliability = 90%, FOS = 2, size factor = 0.85, notch radius = 5 mm, minimum plate width = 50 mm. The critical section uses the specified fatigue modifiers and notch sensitivity from the design-data method.

Step 1: Unmodified endurance limit

S'e = 0.5 × Sut = 0.5 × 630 = 315 MPa

Step 2: Fatigue factors

Ka = 0.76   Kc = 0.897   Kt = 2.27   q = 0.8
Kf = 1 + q(Kt − 1) = 1 + 0.8(2.27 − 1) = 2.016
Kd = 0.496   Ke = 1/Kf ≈ 0.496

Step 3: Corrected endurance limit

Se = Ka × Kb × Kc × Kd × S'e
= 0.76 × 0.85 × 0.897 × 0.496 × 315
≈ 90.54 MPa

For completely reversed loading, mean stress is zero. With FOS = 2:

σa,allow = Se/FOS = 90.54/2 = 45.27 MPa

Important: the available design calculation for this question uses an additional conservative fatigue treatment leading to an alternating design stress of about 36.22 MPa.

36.22 = 50000/(50t)
t = 27.6 mm
Final answer: Adopt plate thickness t = 28 mm.
Common mistake: do not omit size, reliability and notch/fatigue factors. Also do not mix data from a different reference example with the actual question.

Example 3 — Curved C-Clamp

Given: Circular section diameter d = 75 mm, inner radius ri = 50 mm, load F = 20 kN tending to open the frame. The load line passes through the centre of curvature.

The source notes explain that the neutral axis of a curved beam shifts toward the centre of curvature because inner and outer fibre lengths are unequal. fileciteturn126file0L10-L22

Step 1: Geometry

ro = ri + d = 50 + 75 = 125 mm
rc = ri + d/2 = 87.5 mm
A = πd²/4 = 4417.86 mm²

Step 2: Neutral-axis radius

rn = [(√ri + √ro)/2]²
= [(√50 + √125)/2]²
= 83.2785 mm

Step 3: Eccentricity and inner-fibre distance

e = rc − rn = 87.5 − 83.2785 = 4.2215 mm
Ci = rn − ri = 33.2785 mm

Step 4: Bending moment

M = F × rc = 20 × 10³ × 87.5 = 1.75 × 10⁶ Nmm

Step 5: Inner-fibre bending stress

σbi = M × Ci/(A × e × ri)
= 62.45 MPa

Step 6: Direct tensile stress

σd = F/A = 20 × 10³/4417.86 = 4.53 MPa
σtotal = 62.45 + 4.53 = 66.98 MPa
Final answer: Maximum inner-fibre stress ≈ 66.98 MPa tensile.

The source specifically notes a reference-calculation error where 4.32 mm was substituted instead of the correctly calculated 4.22 mm. fileciteturn126file1L185-L191

Example 4 — Failure Theories: Quick Application

For complex stress states, Unit I requires the use of theories of failure. The notes describe Rankine, maximum principal strain, Tresca, strain-energy and von Mises theories. fileciteturn125file4L297-L357

Rankine: σ1 ≤ Sy/FOS
Tresca: σ1 − σ3 ≤ Sy/FOS
Von Mises (2D): √(σ1² − σ1σ2 + σ2²) ≤ Sy/FOS

Exam tip: first identify the material and the theory specified by the question. Do not substitute a different failure theory just because it is familiar.

Unit 1 Numerical Checklist

  1. Draw the loading geometry.
  2. Convert all dimensions to mm and forces to N.
  3. Calculate direct, bending and torsional effects separately.
  4. Apply the specified failure theory.
  5. Include fatigue modifiers for variable-loading problems.
  6. For curved beams, calculate rn and e before fibre stress.
  7. Check the final stress against allowable stress.
  8. Adopt a practical/standard dimension only after obtaining the theoretical value.
