Example 1 — Overhang Crank: Combined Bending and Torsion

Given:

P = 1 kN = 1000 N; vertical arm = 500 mm; eccentricity e = 50 + 100 + 25 = 175 mm; Syt = 400 N/mm²; FOS = 2. Failure theory: Maximum shear stress theory.

To find:

Diameter d at section XX.

Solution:

At section XX, the load produces bending moment and torque.

Allowable shear stressτallow=Syt2×FOS

τallow = 400/(2 × 2) = 100 N/mm².

Bending momentM=P×L

M = 1000 × 500 = 500 × 10³ Nmm.

TorqueT=P×e

T = 1000 × 175 = 175 × 10³ Nmm.

Bending and torsional stresses for a solid circular sectionσb=32Mπd3;τt=16Tπd3
Maximum shear stress theoryτmax=σb22+τt2

Putting τmax = 100 N/mm² and substituting M and T gives d ≈ 29.8 mm.

Check: τmax must not exceed 100 N/mm².

Result: Adopt d = 30 mm.

Example 2 — Completely Reversed Axial Fatigue Load

Given:

Plate material 45C8; Sut = 630 N/mm²; completely reversed axial force = ±50 kN; reliability = 90%; FOS = 2; size factor = 0.85; notch radius = 5 mm; minimum width b = 50 mm.

To find:

Plate thickness t for infinite fatigue life.

Solution:

For completely reversed loading, the mean stress is zero and the applied stress is alternating stress.

Unmodified endurance limitS′e=Sut2

S′e = 630/2 = 315 N/mm².

Use the applicable fatigue factors from the design-data method: Ka = 0.76, Kb = 0.85, Kc = 0.897 and Kd = 0.496.

Corrected endurance limitSe=Ka×Kb×Kc×Kd×S′e

Se = 0.76 × 0.85 × 0.897 × 0.496 × 315 = 90.54 N/mm².

The available design calculation for this question applies the additional conservative treatment used in the worked solution, giving an alternating design stress of about 36.22 N/mm².

Axial alternating stressσa=Pbt

36.22 = 50000/(50t).

Therefore,

t = 50000/(50 × 36.22) ≈ 27.6 mm.

Check: The adopted thickness must be equal to or greater than the calculated thickness.

Result: Adopt t = 28 mm.

Example 3 — Curved C-Clamp

Given:

Section diameter d = 75 mm; inner radius ri = 50 mm; load F = 20 kN; load line passes through the centre of curvature.

To find:

Maximum inner-fibre stress.

Solution:

The neutral axis of a curved beam shifts toward the centre of curvature because the inner and outer fibres have unequal lengths.

Outer radius and centroidal radiusro=ri+d=125 mm;rc=ri+d2=87.5 mm
Area of circular sectionA=π4d2

A = (π/4)(75²) = 4417.86 mm².

Neutral-axis radius for a circular sectionrn=ri+ro22

rn = [(√50 + √125)/2]² = 83.2785 mm.

Eccentricity and inner-fibre distancee=rc−rn;Ci=rn−ri

e = 87.5 − 83.2785 = 4.2215 mm; Ci = 83.2785 − 50 = 33.2785 mm.

Bending momentM=Frc

M = 20 × 10³ × 87.5 = 1.75 × 10⁶ Nmm.

Curved-beam inner-fibre stressσbi=MCiAeri

σbi = 62.45 N/mm².

Direct tensile stressσd=FA

σd = 20 × 10³/4417.86 = 4.53 N/mm².

Total inner-fibre stressσtotal=σbi+σd

σtotal = 62.45 + 4.53 = 66.98 N/mm².

Check: Use the calculated neutral-axis radius and eccentricity consistently; do not replace them with rounded intermediate values prematurely.

Result: Maximum inner-fibre stress ≈ 66.98 N/mm² tensile.

Example 4 — Failure Theories: Quick Application

Given:

A component is subjected to a complex stress state. The applicable failure theory is specified in the problem.

To find:

Whether the component is safe according to the specified failure theory.

Solution:

First calculate the principal stresses, then apply the theory specified by the question.

Principal stresses in a plane stress stateσ1,σ2=σx+σy2±σx−σy22+τxy2
Rankine / maximum principal stress theoryσ1≤SyFOS
Tresca / maximum shear stress theoryσ1−σ3≤SyFOS
Von Mises theory for plane stressσ12−σ1σ2+σ22≤SyFOS

Check: Use the exact failure theory named in the question and use consistent units for stress and strength.

Result: State Safe/Unsafe only after comparing the calculated failure criterion with the allowable value.
